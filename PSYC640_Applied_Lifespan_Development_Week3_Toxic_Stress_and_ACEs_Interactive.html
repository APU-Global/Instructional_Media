Week 3 Interactive: Toxic Stress and Adverse Childhood Experiences

Week 3 focus: Discussion 3 asks how knowing the effects of childhood trauma will shape your future work and help you approach people with grace. Game of Life Part 3 asks you to plan for an early or middle childhood issue. Practice the distinctions the textbook draws.

The early childhood brain at a glance

Select each card to reveal the fact.

Part 1: What kind of stress is it?

Each child is fictional. Choose the kind of stress the textbook would use to describe the situation, then select Check my answers.

Part 2: Is it one of the eight ACE categories?

The textbook describes a large survey (2011–2014) whose ACE questions were collapsed into eight categories. Decide whether each experience is one of those eight. (Other hard experiences can still matter; this checks the survey's categories only.)

Part 3: Risk or buffer?

Decide whether each condition, as the textbook describes it, raises the risk of lasting harm or helps protect the child.

Takeaway: Everyday stress can build coping skills, and longer stressful events can be managed, but toxic stress from prolonged abuse or extreme stress can change the developing brain, the stress response, and immunity. More ACEs are linked to more health risks across adulthood. The textbook's main buffer is relational: supportive and responsive adults. Name that buffer in your Part 3 interventions.
