Externalities and Policy

When a transaction affects people who are not part of it, the market outcome may not serve society well. Add an external cost or benefit, see the market over- or underproduce, and fix it with a tax or subsidy.

Part 1 · Private versus social

Social cost equals private cost plus external cost. Markets overproduce goods with negative externalities and underproduce goods with positive externalities. Choose a case, set the size of the external effect, and compare the market with the social optimum.

Part 2 · Choose the policy tool

Governments and communities can address externalities. Taxes, subsidies, regulation, and tradable permits are common tools, and clear property rights can sometimes allow private bargaining.

Ethics are part of economics

Who bears the costs, and who gains? Externalities raise questions of responsibility and justice. The people harmed by pollution are often not the ones who profit from the product.
Choose policies carefully. A tax or subsidy can only be set well if decision makers listen to those affected, reflecting care for neighbors and for creation.
