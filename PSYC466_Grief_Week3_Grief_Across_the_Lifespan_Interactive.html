Week 3 Interactive: Grief Across the Lifespan

Week 3 objective: Recognize how a person's developmental stage shapes the way they understand and grieve death.

What each stage tends to understand

Lally and Valentine-French describe a developing understanding of death:

  • Infancy: no grasp of death, but strong reactions to separation (quiet, no smiling or cooing, less sleep, weight loss).
  • Early childhood: death seems temporary or reversible; the person may be "sleeping"; a child may think they caused it.
  • Middle childhood: finality begins to make sense, but until about age 9 magical thinking and guilt may remain.
  • Late childhood: everyone dies, including me; fears that parents will die.
  • Adolescence: adult-like, abstract understanding, yet the personal fable can bring risky behavior.
  • Adulthood: early adults report little death anxiety; middle adults the most, because of caregiving; older adults less, with concern about control over how they die.

Practice 2: Support at home or a red flag?

Takeaway: A child's questions are not a problem to be managed but a window into their stage of understanding. Use real words, listen for guilt and fear, and watch for persistent red flags that call for more help.
