Week 2 Interactive: Public and Private Sector Bargaining Tools

Week 2 objective: Distinguish private and public sector bargaining tools, classify bargaining topics, and tell a distributive move from an integrative one.

Three bargaining reminders

Good faithBoth sides must participate actively and show a desire to find a basis for agreement.
Follow the moneyPrivate raises can come from profit; public raises come from taxes and fees.
No federal strikesFederal employees may not strike, so stalled talks go to mediation and the Impasses Panel.

Practice 1: private, public, or both?

Decide which sector each bargaining tool or move belongs to.

Practice 2: what kind of bargaining topic?

Classify each proposal the way the text classifies bargaining topics.

Practice 3: fixed pie or bigger pie?

Is each fictional negotiator taking a distributive (fixed-pie) or an integrative (win-win) approach?

Takeaway: Know which tools each sector can use, which topics belong at the table, and your BATNA (best alternative to a negotiated agreement) before you start. Public talks are funded by taxpayers, so Romans 13:7 fits both sides: pay what is owed, and give respect and honor where they are owed.
