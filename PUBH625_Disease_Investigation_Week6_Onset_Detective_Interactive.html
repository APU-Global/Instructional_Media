Week 6 Interactive: Reasoning Through a Foodborne Outbreak

Week 6 objective: Use typical onset times, symptoms, and food handling to form a hypothesis about the agent in a foodborne outbreak.

What you need to know

Staphylococcus aureus toxin. Onset 1 to 7 hours; nausea, cramps, vomiting, diarrhea. Linked to foods that need a lot of handling and are kept too warm: egg, tuna, chicken, potato, and macaroni salads, sandwich fillings, cream-filled pastries.
Bacillus cereus. Vomiting type: onset 0.5 to 6 hours, generally associated with rice products and other starchy foods. Diarrheal type: 6 to 15 hours.
Clostridium perfringens. Onset about 16 hours; watery diarrhea and mild cramps. Most common where large quantities of food are prepared several hours before serving.
Norovirus and Salmonella. Norovirus: usually 24 to 48 hours, often explosive vomiting first. Salmonella: 6 to 72 hours, with nausea, vomiting, cramps, diarrhea, fever, and headache.

Takeaway: Onset time is one of the fastest clues in a foodborne outbreak, but it only narrows the suspects. Combine it with symptoms, the foods served, how they were handled, and laboratory results, and report the outbreak so it is counted.
