Week 5 Interactive: What Does the Equal Sign Mean?

Week 5 objective: Read the equal sign as "is the same as," tell expressions from equations, and spot the run-on equations children write.

Two meanings children bring

"Makes" or "the answer is"

A child who reads = as "do it and write the answer" sees 3 + 4 = □ as an instruction. That child may fill in 12 + 7 = □ + 6 with 19, the answer to the left side.

"Is the same number as"

The standards remind teachers that the equal sign "does not always mean makes or results in but always does mean is the same number as." A child who reads = this way solves 12 + 7 = □ + 6 by seeing that the right side needs 13.

0 / 0

Expression or equation?

An expression has a value. An equation states that two expressions are equal and can be true or false.

Precise or run-on?

A student must compute 5 × 4 and then add 3. Mark each record as precise (every "=" is true) or a run-on (some "=" is false).

Takeaway: The equal sign always means "is the same number as." Writing 12 + 7 = 19 + 6 = 25 claims that 19 = 25. Give children true-or-false equations, missing numbers in every position, and balance models, so they learn the relational meaning before they meet algebra.
