Week 3 Interactive: Protecting PHI

Week 3 objective: Distinguish HIPAA Privacy Rule issues from Security Rule issues, and recognize when another law requires disclosure.

Key ideas

Protected health information (PHI) is health information that identifies a person through names, addresses, dates, record numbers, or other identifiers; data stripped of them is de-identified. Under HIPAA (1996), the Privacy Rule governs use and disclosure: PHI may be used for treatment, payment, or health care operations, and other disclosures need the patient's consent or an exception. The Security Rule requires administrative, physical, and technical safeguards for the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of electronic PHI. Some disclosures are required by another law: reporting communicable diseases and suspected child or elder abuse, and, as in the Tarasoff case, protecting a third party.
