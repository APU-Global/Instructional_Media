Week 8 Interactive: Community Policing and Problem-Solving

Week 8 objective: Apply the SARA problem-solving model and the problem analysis triangle to a community policing problem.

SARA

Scanning: identify and prioritize recurring problems that concern the public and police. Analysis: study the conditions behind the problem, collect data, and form a working hypothesis. Response: brainstorm, learn what other communities have done, and carry out a plan with clear roles. Assessment: check whether the plan was implemented and whether its goals were met.

The problem analysis triangle

Problems sit where victims, locations, and offenders meet. Spelman and Eck (1989) found that about 10% of victims are involved in up to 40% of victimizations, 10% of offenders in 50% of crimes, and 10% of addresses in about 60% of crimes.

Practice 1: which SARA stage?

A fictional city is tackling repeated car break-ins at the Riverside Park lot. Place each step in its SARA stage.

Practice 2: which side of the triangle?

Decide whether each fictional finding is mainly about the victim, the location, or the offender.

Takeaway: Community policing shares responsibility for safety: police and residents scan for problems together, analyze causes, respond with partners, and assess results. Trust built before a crisis makes every step easier.
