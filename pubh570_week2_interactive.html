Preventing Sexual Violence

Sexual violence is preventable, and public health looks for ways to stop it before it happens. Explore risk and protection at several levels, match prevention strategies to those levels, and practice a trauma-informed response.

Part 1 · The social-ecological model

Sexual violence includes a range of unwanted sexual acts, affects people of all genders, and has serious health consequences. The model describes risk and protection at multiple levels, and effective prevention works at more than one. Select a ring.

Part 3 · Prevent and support

Prevention can occur before, during, and after violence. Primary prevention stops violence before it occurs; support and treatment reduce harm for survivors. Both are needed.

Part 4 · Respond with care

Trauma-informed responses avoid re-traumatizing survivors, offer choice and respect, and give clear information about resources. Imagine a community health worker hears this from a client.

“Something happened to me a while ago, and I have never told anyone.”
“I am not sure I want to go to the police or anyone yet.”
Faith communities can be places of support when they respond with care: listening without blame, protecting privacy, and connecting people with professional help.
If you or someone you know needs support
In the U.S., the National Sexual Assault Hotline (RAINN) is available 24/7 at 1-800-656-HOPE (4673) or online at rainn.org. If someone is in immediate danger, call 911.
