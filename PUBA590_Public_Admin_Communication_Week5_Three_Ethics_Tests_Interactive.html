Week 5 Interactive: Ethical Decision-Making

Week 5 objective: Distinguish consequentialist and deontological reasoning and apply the three decision tests to a public communication dilemma.

Key ideas from the reading

  • Enlightened self-interest gives preference to the decision maker's desires, but not to the exclusion of others' wishes.
  • Utilitarianism seeks the greatest good for the greatest number by weighing outcomes; it can overlook a valid minority and depends on predicting consequences.
  • Deontology (Kant) asks three questions: Could you will the decision to become universal? Does it maintain the dignity and respect of publics? Is it made from good intention?
  • A decision that passes all three tests is more defensible to the media and to publics.

Practice 1: Which framework is speaking?

Staff at a fictional city communications office are debating a decision. Identify the framework behind each statement.

Practice 2: Run the three tests

A fictional transit agency finds an error that overstated on-time performance in its published annual report. Option 1: quietly fix the number in next year's report without mentioning it. Option 2: publish a correction now with a plain explanation. Decide whether each option passes each test.

Takeaway: When an ethical choice is not obvious, name your framework. Weigh outcomes, but do not stop there: a decision that could be universal, respects the dignity of every public, and comes from good intention is the one you can defend.
