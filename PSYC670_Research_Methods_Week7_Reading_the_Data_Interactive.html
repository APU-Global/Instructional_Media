Part 2: How strong is the effect?

Use Cohen's guidelines: d near 0.20, 0.50, 0.80 and r near ±.10, ±.30, ±.50 are small, medium, and large. All results are fictional except where noted.

Part 3: Correlation or causation?

Takeaway: Describe before you infer: report the mean, median, mode, and spread, and notice when an outlier pulls the mean toward the tail. Read effect sizes alongside p values, because a large sample can make a weak effect significant. Only manipulation with random assignment supports a causal claim; otherwise, report a relationship.

Concepts from Price, Jhangiani, & Chiang, Research Methods in Psychology (2nd Canadian ed., Lumen Learning): Describing Single Variables; Describing Statistical Relationships (Table 12.4, Cohen's guidelines; the d = 0.82 exposure-therapy example); Understanding Null Hypothesis Testing; Experiment Basics.
