Week 5 Interactive: Managing the Fallout

Week 5 objective: Address a public scandal quickly and honestly, balancing transparency with the privacy rights of those involved, and design reforms people will follow.

The situation (fictional)

You have just been appointed Chief of the Harbor City Police Department. A records audit shows that a watch commander changed or discarded use-of-force reports for two years, and that several officers knew. A local reporter has the story. The district attorney has opened a criminal investigation into two officers. You must decide what to do in the first weeks.

For each item, decide whether to disclose it publicly or protect it (withhold or redact under a legal exemption or privacy duty), then select Check Part 2.

Takeaway: “Sunlight is the best of disinfectants,” but transparency is robust, not absolute. Disclose the maximum the law permits, use exemptions sparingly and record why, protect victims and the due-process rights of the accused, investigate independently, and build reforms on training, clear communication, reporting channels, consistent enforcement, and leaders who go first.

Principles from Raber, T. (2026), Governing with Integrity: Ethical Reasoning for Public Service (LibreTexts, CC BY 4.0), Sections 5.3, 7.3, 7.5, 8.3, 8.4, and 9.2. Harbor City and every event in it are fictional.
