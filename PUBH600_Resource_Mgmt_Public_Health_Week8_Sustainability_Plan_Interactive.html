Week 8 Interactive: Ensuring Program Longevity

Week 8 objective: Identify sustainability risks and choose strategies that keep a program's benefits going.

The scenario

Walk Together is a fictional three-year walking program for adults with high blood pressure, run by a rural health coalition. Its startup grant ends next year. The coalition is writing a sustainability plan using the Community Tool Box chapter on planning for sustainability.

Practice 1: which sustainability area is at risk?

Public health sustainability frameworks look at several interacting areas. Match each warning sign to its area.

Practice 2: which evaluation question does this raise?

The Tool Box suggests weighing each possible funding source against several questions. Choose the main concern for each option.

Practice 3: continue, adapt, transfer, or end?

Sustainability does not mean keeping everything unchanged. Decide what the plan should do with each element.

Takeaway: Sustainability means keeping the benefits, not every structure. Ask what should continue, for whom, and for how long; strengthen capacity, partnerships, evaluation, and communication; and build a balanced funding mix that avoids both single-source dependence and unmanageable complexity.

Sustainability definition, framework areas, funding criteria, and continue/adapt/transfer/end questions from Section 1. Strategies for the Long-Term Sustainability of an Initiative: An Overview and Section 2. Strategies for Sustaining the Initiative (Community Tool Box, Chapter 46, University of Kansas). Walk Together is fictional.
