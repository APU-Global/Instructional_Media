Week 2 Interactive: The Case Study Iceberg

Week 2 objective: Look beneath the events in a case to the patterns and structures that drive them, and match community-based interventions to the part of the problem they address.

The iceberg on your Case Study Analysis Preparation Worksheet

EventsWhat’s happening? What is seen.
Patterns of behaviorWhat’s been happening? What are the trends? What changes have occurred?
StructuresWhat has influenced the patterns (rules, plans, policies)? What are the relationships among the parts? What is unseen.

Practice 1: event, pattern, or structure?

Each statement comes from the fictional city of Riverton, where TB rates are falling overall but remain far higher in one neighborhood. Choose the level of the iceberg it shows.

Practice 2: which prong?

The task force in the course’s TB case built a three-pronged intervention. Decide which prong each fictional Riverton activity belongs to.

Takeaway: Events grab attention, but the leverage sits lower: patterns such as delayed care and structures such as funding rules and community distrust. A response that works on all three prongs, built with the community, reaches below the waterline.

Iceberg levels from the course’s Case Study Analysis Preparation Worksheet; facts and the three-pronged intervention from the course’s Case Study #2 Addressing the Tuberculosis Crisis. Riverton and its residents are fictional.
