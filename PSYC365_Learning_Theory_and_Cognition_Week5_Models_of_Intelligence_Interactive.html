Week 5 Interactive: Comparing Models of Intelligence

Week 5 objective: Distinguish the major models of intelligence and weigh the evidence for and against each one.

What the readings say

Charles Spearman proposed a single general intelligence factor (g). Horn and Cattell separated fluid intelligence (thinking on your feet) from crystallized intelligence (using knowledge and experience). Howard Gardner proposed eight multiple intelligences, and Robert Sternberg's triarchic theory names analytical, creative, and practical intelligence. Emotional intelligence is the ability to understand others' emotions and to identify, label, and use your own. Each model has research support and critics, which is what your Intelligence Pitch must weigh.

Practice 1: Match the model

Choose the model each statement describes.

Practice 2: Support or critique?

Decide whether each research finding supports a model or raises a critique of it.

Takeaway: A strong pitch names the model's core claim, shows the evidence, and admits at least one limitation. Be careful with labels: every model is a lens, not a verdict on a person's worth.
