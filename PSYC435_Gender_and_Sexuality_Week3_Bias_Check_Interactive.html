Week 3 Interactive: Recognizing Bias

Week 3 objective: Distinguish stereotypes, prejudice, and discrimination, and recognize blatant and subtle (automatic, ambiguous, ambivalent) forms of bias.

Key terms

  • Stereotype: a belief that characterizes people by their group (thinking).
  • Prejudice: an evaluation or emotion toward people because of their group (feeling).
  • Discrimination: behavior that advantages or disadvantages people because of their group (acting).

Blatant biases are conscious and openly admitted. Subtle biases are automatic (unintended and immediate), ambiguous (such as in-group favoritism, which disadvantages others by exclusion), or ambivalent (mixed, such as seeing a group as warm but not competent).

Practice 1: Thought, feeling, or action?

Choose whether each fictional example shows a stereotype, prejudice, or discrimination.

Practice 2: Blatant or subtle?

Choose the form of bias each fictional example shows.

Takeaway: Good intentions do not make us immune to bias. Reflect on where your biases come from, learn from people whose experiences differ from yours, and watch your own behavior, especially the small, nonverbal signals clients notice.
