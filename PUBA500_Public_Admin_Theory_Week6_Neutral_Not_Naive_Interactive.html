Week 6 Interactive: Neutrality and the Political-Administrative Interface

Week 6 objective: Distinguish merit from favoritism, patronage, cronyism, and nepotism, and separate the roles of elected leaders and public administrators.

Key terms

  • Neutrality: civil servants are apolitical in carrying out their functions and impartial in implementing policies, programs, and projects; appointments and promotions follow public service acts and regulations, not the party in power.
  • Favoritism: favoring a person not because they do the best job but because of some extraneous feature, such as membership in a favored group or personal likes and dislikes.
  • Patronage: giving public service jobs to those who may have helped elect the person who has the power of appointment.
  • Cronyism: partiality toward friends and associates.
  • Nepotism: favoritism toward members of the family.

Part 1: Merit or favoritism?

Each decision is from the fictional City of Northgate. Choose the best label.

Part 2: Whose role is it?

Using the roles Betchoo describes for ministers and officials, decide who should normally do each task in Northgate.

Takeaway: Neutral does not mean naive. Administrators read the political climate to plan timing and message, give honest advice, and then implement lawful decisions impartially. Appointments by merit and a healthy relationship between elected and appointed leaders protect public trust.

Definitions and roles from Betchoo, N. K. (2016). Public sector management: A millennial insight (1st ed.). Bookboon. Chapter 9, “Public Sector’s Political Issues” (neutrality of the public service; ministers’ and officials’ roles; favouritism, cronyism, and nepotism). Northgate and its officials are fictional.
