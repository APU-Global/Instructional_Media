Week 5 Interactive: Who Steps In?

Week 5 objective: Explain how jurisdiction moves among local, state, and federal agencies and how agencies coordinate when crime crosses borders.

Explore: how a multi-level case unfolds

Select each phase, then the coordination pillars, to see how agencies share a case.

Select a card.

Practice 1: who steps in?

Each case is fictional. Choose the highest level of government you would expect to become involved.

Practice 2: which pillar broke down?

When agencies fail to align, cases suffer. Choose the coordination pillar that would have prevented each fictional problem.

Takeaway: Jurisdiction follows the law that was broken and the reach of the crime. Local agencies usually respond first, state agencies add regional coordination and labs, and federal agencies step in for interstate, organized, national security, cyber, and other federal crimes. Cases succeed when communication, resources, information, joint operations, and leadership are coordinated.

Workflow, federal triggers, and coordination pillars from Local, State, and Federal Criminal Justice Systems (CRJU 490 course media, Azusa Pacific University Global, n.d.). Federal crimes and the state and local role from Bureau of Justice Statistics, The Justice System (2021). Cases are fictional.
