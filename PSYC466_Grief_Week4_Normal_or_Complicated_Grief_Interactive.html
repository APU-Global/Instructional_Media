Week 4 Interactive: Normal and Complicated Grief Responses

Week 4 objective: Distinguish typical grief reactions from signs of complicated grief and separate myths about the five stages from what the readings say.

Typical and complicated grief

Typical grief can bring numbness, anger, guilt, anxiety, sadness, poor concentration, and changes in sleep and appetite; these symptoms usually diminish within 6–10 weeks, even though grief itself has no expiration date. Complicated grief includes disbelief, preoccupation with the person who died, distressing memories, feeling unable to move on, and yearning that last six months or longer. It often follows traumatic bereavement: unexpected, multiple, or violent deaths. Only a trained clinician can decide whether grief is complicated; your job here is to notice when an assessment is warranted.
