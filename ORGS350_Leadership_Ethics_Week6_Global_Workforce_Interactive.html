Week 6 Interactive: Respect in a Global Workforce

Week 6 objective: Recognize Hofstede's four cultural dimensions in workplace behavior and choose leadership responses that avoid both cultural relativism and ethnocentrism.

Hofstede's four dimensions

  • Individualism-collectivism: loose ties to groups versus identity defined by the group.
  • Power distance: how acceptable an unequal distribution of power is.
  • Uncertainty avoidance: how threatening ambiguous, risky situations feel.
  • Masculinity-femininity: achievement and competition versus relationships and quality of life.

Practice 1: which dimension?

Sacred Spaces, the course's fictional company, has teams in several countries. Which dimension best explains each observation?

Practice 2: relativist, ethnocentric, or respectful?

A supplier abroad is found employing children under 14, many of them their families' sole breadwinners. Classify each leader's response.

Practice 3: building cultural intelligence

Which actions follow the textbook's suggestions for managing cultural diversity?

Takeaway: Learn how culture shapes expectations, listen to locals, and hold your principles with humility. Avoid both "when in Rome" relativism and ethnocentrism. In Acts 17, Paul first observed and acknowledged the Athenians' culture, then spoke his own convictions clearly.
