Week 5 Interactive: The Job Analysis Process

Week 5 objective: Practice the steps that turn job analysis into fair job descriptions and pay decisions (CLO 1).

Four questions every task statement answers

1. Performs what action?
Start with a verb.
2. To whom or what?
The object of the verb.
3. To produce what?
The output, or why it is necessary.
4. Using what?
Tools, equipment, work aids, or processes.

Practice 1: is the task statement complete?

Statements for a Medical Billing Specialist at a fictional clinic. Choose Complete or the main problem.

Practice 2: essential or not?

A job analysis separates essential duties from preferences. Classify each requirement in a draft Patient Transporter job description.

Practice 3: a paired-comparison job evaluation

Job evaluation sets the relative worth of jobs. In a paired comparison, each job is compared with every other job: 2 points if it is more important to the organization, 1 if about the same, 0 if less. Make your choices for four jobs at a fictional hospital and watch the ranking change.

Takeaway: Job analysis is the building block of HR. Write clear task statements, verify them with the people who do and supervise the work, keep only requirements that are essential, and update the analysis as jobs change. A fair pay structure depends on it.
