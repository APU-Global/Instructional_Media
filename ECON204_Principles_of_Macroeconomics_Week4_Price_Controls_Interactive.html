Week 4 Interactive: Price Control Simulator

Discussion 4 focus: See what happens when a government sets a legal maximum price (a ceiling) or a legal minimum price (a floor), and predict shortages, surpluses, and unintended effects.

The rule

A price ceiling is a legal maximum price; it binds only when it is set below the equilibrium price, and then quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied (a shortage). A price floor is a legal minimum price; it binds only when it is set above equilibrium, and then quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded (a surplus). Either way, some trades that buyers and sellers both wanted never happen.

Takeaway: First compare the controlled price with equilibrium. A binding ceiling creates a shortage and often lower quality; a binding floor creates a surplus, such as unemployed workers or unsold crops the government must buy. Good intentions do not cancel the laws of demand and supply.
