Week 8 Interactive: Precision Public Health

Week 8 objective: Use the CDC’s evidence tiers and the three core public health functions to judge how personalized medicine fits into public health practice.

Two tools for judging new technology

CDC evidence tiers for genomic applications. Tier 1: synthesized evidence supports implementation in practice. Tier 2: evidence is insufficient for routine use but may inform decisions. Tier 3: evidence recommends against use, or is insufficient; not ready for routine practice.

Core functions (Institute of Medicine, 1988): assessment (collecting and analyzing information on community health), policy development (standards and guidelines), and assurance (making sure tests and services are used appropriately).

Practice 1: which evidence tier?

A fictional state health department is reviewing genomic applications. Choose the CDC tier for each.

Practice 2: which core function?

Match each fictional activity to the core public health function it serves.

Takeaway: Precision public health aims for the right intervention for the right population at the right time. Let the evidence tiers decide what is ready, keep the core functions in view, and remember that housing, education, and access to care still shape who benefits.

Evidence tiers, core functions, and examples from Khoury, M. J., Bowen, M. S., Clyne, M., Dotson, W. D., Gwinn, M. L., Green, R. F., Kolor, K., Rodriguez, J. L., Wulf, A., & Yu, W. (2018). From public health genomics to precision public health: A 20-year journey. Genetics in Medicine, 20(6), 574–582. The health department and activities are fictional.
