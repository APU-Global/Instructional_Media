Week 1 Interactive: From Idea to Proposal

Week 1 objective: Use creative thinking and the innovation process to shape a capstone idea, then frame it the way the Capstone Proposal Form asks.

Two kinds of thinking, one process

Creativity needs two forms of thinking. Programmed thinking uses logical, step-by-step methods to make existing products and services better. Lateral thinking changes patterns and perceptions to produce completely new concepts. The course's Innovation Process infographic then moves an idea through seven steps, from defining the problem to monitoring results. Your Capstone Proposal Form captures the result in four parts: Title, Objective (a brief business case), Approach, and Expectations.

Activity 1: Put the innovation process in order

The seven steps below are shuffled. Use the Up and Down buttons to put them in the order the Innovation Process infographic shows, then select Check my answers.

Activity 2: Programmed or lateral thinking?

A fictional leader at Harbor Regional Clinic wants to help new nurses stay in their jobs. Decide whether each idea comes mainly from programmed (logical, improve what exists) or lateral (change the pattern, new concept) thinking.

Activity 3: Which part of the Proposal Form?

Each statement comes from the same fictional proposal. Choose the part of the Capstone Proposal Form where it belongs.

Takeaway: A strong capstone idea comes from both kinds of thinking: lateral thinking to see something new and programmed thinking to make it work. Move it through the innovation process, then state it clearly: a title, a business case, an approach, and specific expectations.
