Week 1 Interactive: Evaluating Personality Assessments

Week 1 objective: Evaluate the strengths and limits of personality assessment methods and the evidence behind them.

Key terms

  • Self-enhancement bias: seeing or presenting oneself too favorably, a special concern in high-stakes testing such as hiring.
  • Reference group effect: judging oneself (or one's child) by comparison with the people nearby rather than by an absolute standard.
  • Letter of recommendation effect: informants who like the person rate too favorably (with newlyweds, the honeymoon effect).
  • Reliability: the consistency of scores across repeated assessments, such as test-retest reliability.
  • Validity: evidence about how scores can be interpreted and used, such as predicting relevant outcomes (criterion validity).
  • Correlational evidence: shows that two variables go together but usually cannot show what causes what.

Practice: what is the main problem?

Every organization and person below is fictional. Choose the main problem with each score or claim, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: No single assessment method is perfect. Ask who supplied the ratings, what biases could shape them, whether scores are reliable, and whether there is validity evidence that they predict outcomes that matter. Then check whether the research design supports the claims being made.
