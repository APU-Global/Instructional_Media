Week 5 Interactive: Thinking Like a One Health Task Force

Week 5 objective: Explain how the One Health approach links human, animal, and environmental health in responding to emerging health threats.

One Health in brief

Pungartnik et al. (2023) describe One Health as integrating human health, animal health, and environmental determinants at local, national, and global levels through multi-, inter-, and transdisciplinary work.

Human healthPeople: patients, clinics, contact tracing, and community communication.
Animal healthLivestock, pets, and wildlife, and the veterinary services that monitor them.
EnvironmentWater, soil, food systems, and the places where people and animals meet.

Practice 1: Which part of One Health?

A fictional respiratory virus has appeared in a farming valley and is spreading from animals to people. Decide which part of One Health each task force step mainly addresses. Choose an answer for each item, then select Check my answers.

Practice 2: Which theme from the review?

The review found seven themes where One Health and global health meet. Match each fictional activity to its theme. Choose an answer for each item, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: No single sector sees a whole outbreak. A One Health task force links human, animal, and environmental health, and the review shows the work clusters around zoonoses, antimicrobial resistance, and food safety, supported by policy and cross-disciplinary training.

(2023), “The interfaces between One Health and Global Health: A scoping review,” One Health, 16, 100573 (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10288129). The outbreak and every activity are fictional.
