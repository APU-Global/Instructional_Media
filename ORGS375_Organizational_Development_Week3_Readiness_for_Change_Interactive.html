Week 3 Interactive: Lewin's Model and ADKAR

Week 3 objective: Sequence change actions with Lewin's three-stage model and write readiness questions for each ADKAR component, then name the type of training that builds knowledge and ability.

Two lenses on change

Lewin's three stages: Unfreezing makes sure people are ready for and receptive to change; Change executes the planned changes; Refreezing makes the new habits, rules, or procedures the norm.

ADKAR (as this week's discussion defines it) assesses stakeholders' Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, and Reinforcement and can be used before an intervention to gauge readiness.

Practice 1: which stage?

Harborview Clinic, a fictional practice, is moving from paper charts to an electronic scheduling and records system. Choose the Lewin stage for each action.

Practice 2: match the ADKAR component

Each readiness question targets one ADKAR component. Choose the component it assesses.

Practice 3: which type of training?

Awareness and desire are not enough; people also need the knowledge and ability to work the new way. This week's textbook chapter, "Training and Development," names several types of training. Choose the type each Harborview activity represents.

Takeaway: Prepare before you implement. Unfreezing builds readiness, change is where support, small wins, and removing obstacles matter, and refreezing makes the new way stick. ADKAR questions show exactly where a group stands, and training builds the knowledge and ability that awareness alone cannot.
