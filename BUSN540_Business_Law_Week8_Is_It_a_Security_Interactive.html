Practice 1: the Howey test

An investment contract is a security when a person invests money in a common enterprise and expects profits solely from the efforts of a promoter or third party. Labels do not control. All offerings are fictional.

Practice 2: short-swing profits under Section 16(b)

Section 16(b) lets the corporation (or a shareholder suing for it) recover any profit an insider (director, officer, or owner of 10% or more) makes from a purchase and sale, or a sale and purchase, within six months, even without inside information. Enter the amount the corporation can recover (enter 0 if none).

Takeaway: Securities law looks at substance, not labels: if investors put money into a common enterprise and rely on others to make it profitable, the disclosure and anti-fraud rules apply. Insiders face a bright-line six-month rule because fair markets depend on everyone trading with the same information.
