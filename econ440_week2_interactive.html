Comparative Advantage

David Ricardo showed that gains from trade exist even when one country is better at everything. Work out the opportunity costs, choose the terms of trade, and see both countries consume beyond their own limits.

Part 1 · Find the comparative advantage

Two countries, Aria and Bora, each have 120 labor hours and can make wheat or cloth. Aria is better at making both. Does trade still help? Comparative advantage depends on opportunity cost.

Part 2 · Trade and consume beyond your limits

Each country specializes in its comparative advantage: Aria makes 60 wheat, Bora makes 30 cloth. They trade at a price you choose (cloth per wheat) and a quantity of wheat. The solid line is what a country can produce alone; the dashed line is what it can consume with trade.

Trade is not zero-sum: when the price lies between the two opportunity costs (2/3 and 3/2 cloth per wheat), both countries end up beyond their own production limits.
