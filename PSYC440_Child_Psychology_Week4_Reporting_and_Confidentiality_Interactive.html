Week 4 Interactive: Ethics and Laws

Week 4 objective: Recognize the categories of child maltreatment and decide when the duty to report, the duty to warn or protect, or confidentiality governs a situation.

Three duties to keep straight

  • Mandatory reporting: all 50 states and the District of Columbia require the reporting of suspected or confirmed child abuse; 46 states impose criminal penalties for failing to report. Immunity laws generally protect good-faith reporters, and parents should usually be told about a report unless the child is in immediate danger.
  • Duty to warn or protect: after Tarasoff, states differ. By one count, 27 to 33 states have mandatory laws, 9 to 11 permissive laws, and 6 to 13 none. Know your own state's rule and consult when unsure.
  • Confidentiality: the default for what a client shares. Many ethics codes make an exception when the law requires disclosure, as reporting laws do.

Takeaway: Ethics and law work together. Report suspected abuse even without proof or the child's permission, follow your state's rule on the duty to warn or protect, and treat everything else as confidential. Explain the limits of confidentiality at the start so a child is never promised a secret you cannot keep.
