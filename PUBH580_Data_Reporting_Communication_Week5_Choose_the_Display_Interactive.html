Week 5 Interactive: Data Visualization

Week 5 objective: Choose a display that fits the question (time, place, or person) and apply CDC's design guidelines.

Start with the question

  • What and how much? Tables of counts and rates characterize the cases.
  • When? Line graphs for rates over time; epidemic curves for case counts by time interval.
  • Where? Spot maps for case locations; area maps for rates.
  • Among whom and how related? Tables by personal attributes; scatter plots for two numeric variables.

Every display needs a title, data, footnotes, and text.

Practice 1: choose the display

All scenarios are fictional. Pick the display that best answers each question.

Practice 2: fix the design

Each draft breaks one CDC guideline. Choose the fix.

Takeaway: Let the question choose the chart: time (line graph, epidemic curve), place (spot map, area map of rates), person and association (tables, scatter plots). Then design for honesty: clear titles and footnotes, uncluttered axes, legends outside the data, separate panels for groups, and one hue for rising values.

Display types and guidelines from Fontaine, "Describing Epidemiologic Data," Chapter 6 of The CDC Field Epidemiology Manual (CDC, 2024). Scenarios are fictional.
