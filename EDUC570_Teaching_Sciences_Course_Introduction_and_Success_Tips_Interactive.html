About the course

Exploration of best practices in teaching and learning science in elementary school classrooms. The course emphasizes ways to encourage scientific inquiry and scientific literacy in relevant and engaging practices, teaching to content and Next Generation Science Standards, and designing learning for diverse student groups. Requires fieldwork. Carries graduate-level credit.

Your textbook is Ambitious Science Teaching (Windschitl, Thompson, & Braaten, 2018). Select a card to see each course learning outcome in detail.

Fieldwork is required to pass. The Fieldwork Observation Form Submissions carry no points, but you must complete 10 hours of observation in TK-6 classrooms, observe at least three different teachers, and upload a separate form for each lesson (10 forms) by Week 8. If you do not complete the 10 hours, you will not pass the course.

Projected total

Respond by Thursday. Discuss through Saturday.

Each of the eight discussions is worth 50 points (Week 8: respond by Wednesday, discuss through Friday). Post at least four substantive responses each week: one initial response and three responses to your colleagues.

INITIAL POST
250–350 words
or a 1–3 minute video/audio post that shows your understanding of the topic and the week’s course material.
THREE COLLEAGUE RESPONSES
125–200 words each
or 30 seconds to 2 minutes each, adding original thought to the conversation.
ACADEMIC DISCOURSE
Connect experience and course knowledge, credit your sources (APA format not required), and avoid simple agreement: if you agree, explain why.

Discussions are graded with a 4-criterion rubric: Engagement (10), Critical Thinking (20), Contributions to Discussion (15), and Communication and Collaboration (5), for 50 points. Posts made after the academic week do not count toward your grade.
