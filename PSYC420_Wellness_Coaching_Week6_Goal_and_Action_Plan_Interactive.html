Week 6 Interactive: Evaluation and Action

Week 6 objective: Evaluate wellness goals and action plans and decide what each one needs so progress can be measured and the plan can pivot.

What a workable plan includes

Setting a goal is seldom enough to change behavior. Goal-setting research recommends these features.

SMART goalSpecific, Measurable, Achievable, Realistic, and Timed, so you have a clear standard to measure progress against.
Approach, not avoidanceSay what you will do ("walk after dinner") rather than what you will stop doing; approach goals bring more positive emotion.
Weekly action planWhere, when, and how, chosen by the person, about a week long, then reviewed. If confidence is below 7 out of 10, pick a more feasible plan.
Coping planA backup for predictable barriers (rain, travel) that shields the action plan from derailment.

Practice: what does this plan need?

Each plan belongs to a fictional client. Choose the one fix the plan most needs, or Ready to go, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: Measure progress against a SMART standard, carry it out through a short action plan with a coping plan, and review it weekly. A plan that misses is feedback: pivot to a more feasible plan rather than giving up the goal.

Criteria from Bailey, R. R. (2019), “Goal setting and action planning for health behavior change,” American Journal of Lifestyle Medicine, 13(6), 615–618 (open access, PMC6796229). Clients and plans are fictional.
