The Shape of Character

Virtue ethics asks a different question: not “what should I do?” but “what kind of person should I be?” Practice finding the mean between two vices, then grow your own character tree.

Part 1 · Find the virtuous response

Each scenario has two vices at the ends of a line. Slide the marker to where you think a virtuous person would act, then check. Aristotle held that the right response is not a fixed number: practical wisdom reads the situation.

Your character tree

Virtues are cultivated through practice and habit, not learned as definitions.

Part 2 · Faith, hope, and love

Christian ethics also emphasizes virtues. Flip each card (Enter or tap) for a question that applies it to everyday life.

Part 3 · Ask the guiding question

How would a virtuous person decide this?

Think of a decision you are facing. Does your proposed choice build or erode the character you want to have? Write a few sentences. They stay on this page only.

Source: PHIL 210 Week 3 Deep Dive. Aristotle (2009), The Nicomachean Ethics; Hursthouse (1999), On Virtue Ethics; Rae (2018), Moral Choices. The “mean between extremes” framing is Aristotle's, used here as an extension of the Deep Dive's treatment of courage and temperance.
