Week 7 Interactive: Courtroom Testimony

Week 7 objective: Recognize what evidence a jury may consider and choose testimony that is accurate, objective, and within the limits of the evidence.

Why your words matter

Under the principle of orality, the trier of fact considers only the evidence developed, presented, and received into the record during trial. If police question a defendant and write a report, the jury cannot consider the report unless it is offered in a way that complies with the rules of evidence and the court receives it. In many civil law countries, by contrast, judges rely on written summaries of witness statements (a dossier).

NIJ's review of forensic testimony in wrongful convictions warns against unexplained phrases such as "consistent with" or "similar to," and calls for impartial conclusions that state their limits. For latent prints, the ACE-V method has four possible outcomes: no value, identification, exclusion, or inconclusive.

Takeaway: In an American courtroom the case is built from admitted evidence, and your testimony is how your work reaches the jury. Answer the question asked, from the record, in plain terms, and say what a finding does not show as clearly as what it does.
