Week 2 Interactive: Strategic Planning Methods

Week 2 objective: Choose a strategic planning method that fits the question you need to answer, and apply SWOT without its common mistakes.

Four tools, four questions

PESTEL
The general environment: political, economic, social, technological, environmental, and legal trends no single firm controls.
Porter's Five Forces
The industry: rivalry, threat of new entrants, threat of substitutes, and the bargaining power of buyers and of suppliers.
SWOT
One firm: internal strengths and weaknesses compared with external opportunities and threats. A brainstorming starting point.
Value chain
The firm's activities, from inbound logistics and operations to marketing, sales, and support, and where technology can make them hard to copy.

Practice 1: Which method fits the question?

Northgate Freight is a fictional regional trucking company. For each question its leaders ask, choose the method that best answers it, then select Check my answers.

Practice 2: Build Northgate's SWOT, carefully

Place each statement in the right SWOT quadrant. One statement is a trap: it is a strategic move, not a SWOT entry.

Takeaway: Pick the method that matches the level of your question. Use PESTEL for the general environment, Five Forces to judge an industry, and SWOT and the value chain to examine the firm and set technology goals. In SWOT, keep internal and external factors apart, and never list a move as an opportunity.
