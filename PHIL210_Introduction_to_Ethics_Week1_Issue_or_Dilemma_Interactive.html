Week 1 Interactive: Ethical Issues, Dilemmas, and Christian Ethics

Week 1 objective: Tell an ethical issue from an ethical dilemma, and connect Christian ethical principles to the character of God.

Three kinds of situations

  • Ethical issue: an ethical element is present, but no competing principle pulls the other way. Verified theft is simply wrong in most frameworks.
  • Ethical dilemma: two ethical principles compete, so one will be compromised. Example: stealing food (no stealing) to feed one's family (caring for those in your charge).
  • Practical question: a logistical choice or personal preference with no ethical principle at stake.

This course focuses on dilemmas. Christian ethics grounds its principles in the character of God and the teaching of Scripture.

Practice 1: Issue, dilemma, or practical?

Each situation is fictional. Decide what kind of situation it is.

Practice 2: Which attribute of God?

The Christian ethics overview links each principle to an attribute of God. Choose the attribute behind each principle.

Takeaway: Before you analyze a case, ask whether two ethical principles truly compete. If they do, you have a dilemma, and Christian ethics asks you to weigh it in light of who God is: holy, loving, just, merciful, faithful, Creator, and sovereign.
