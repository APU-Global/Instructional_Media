Week 2 Interactive: Interview Approaches

Week 2 objective: Distinguish the Reid, PEACE, and Cognitive approaches to investigative interviewing and choose questions that produce accurate, usable information.

Three approaches at a glance

Reid Technique
Factual analysis, then a behavioral interview (rapport, baseline, behavior-provoking questions), then an accusatory interrogation that interrupts denials and uses minimization and maximization. Aims at a confession.
PEACE Model
Preparation and planning, Engage and explain, Account clarification and challenge, Closure, Evaluation. Seeks relevant facts; challenges contradictions without threats, promises, or minimization.
Cognitive Interview
Open-ended narration, then follow-up questions. The interviewee does about 80 percent of the talking. Reverse-order recall can expose fabricated stories.

Practice 1: Which approach is this?

Each move comes from a fictional interview. Choose the approach it best represents, then select Check my answers.

Practice 2: Which question would you ask?

Fictional scenario: A store clerk saw a man run from the parking lot after a car window was smashed. You are taking her first statement.

Choose the question that will produce the most reliable, usable information at this point in the interview.

Takeaway: Know what each method is for. Reid is built to obtain confessions and carries a false-confession risk. PEACE and the Cognitive Interview seek accurate accounts through open questions, fair challenge of contradictions, and letting the other person talk. Ask open first, probe second, and avoid leading or compound questions.
