Week 7 Interactive: The Health Impact Pyramid

Week 7 objective: Classify public health interventions by tier of the health impact pyramid and explain why lower tiers usually reach more people.

Five tiers, from the base up

  1. Socioeconomic factors: poverty reduction, improved education.
  2. Changing the context so healthy options are the default: clean water and air, safe roads, smoke-free laws, less sodium in packaged foods.
  3. Long-lasting protective interventions, one-time or infrequent: immunization, colonoscopy, smoking cessation programs.
  4. Clinical interventions: ongoing care, such as treating high blood pressure.
  5. Counseling and education, at the top.

Interventions near the base reach broader segments of society and need less individual effort.

Practice: which tier?

Each action is planned by the fictional Cedar County Health Department. Choose its tier, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: Interventions at the base tend to have the greatest impact because they reach everyone and need little individual effort, but they can be the most controversial. The top tiers matter too: comprehensive tobacco control works at every level at once.

Tiers and examples from Frieden, T. R. (2010). A framework for public health action: The health impact pyramid. American Journal of Public Health, 100(4), 590–595. Cedar County is fictional.
