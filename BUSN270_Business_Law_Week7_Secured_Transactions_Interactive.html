Week 7 Interactive: Secured Transactions

Week 7 objective: Explain how a security interest attaches and is perfected, and choose the right perfection method for the collateral.

Two different jobs

Attachment makes a security interest enforceable against the debtor. It needs three things: the creditor gives value, the debtor has rights in the collateral, and the debtor authenticates (signs) a security agreement describing the collateral, or the creditor takes possession of it. Perfection announces the interest to the rest of the world, so it is not subordinated to other secured parties, lien creditors, bankruptcy trustees, or certain buyers.

    Takeaway: Attach first (value, debtor's rights, signed security agreement or possession), then perfect. Filing a financing statement is the general rule: the usual route for inventory a business keeps selling and the route for intangibles such as accounts and software. Possession works for goods and certain paper the creditor (or a field warehouse) can hold. A seller's PMSI in consumer goods is perfected automatically, but filing still protects it against a later consumer buyer.
