Week 5 Interactive: Retirement Planning Lab

Week 5 focus: Discussion 5 asks how large a retirement nest egg you need and how much you must save each month to build it. This lab uses a fictional saver so you can practice the two-step method, see the cost of waiting, and then apply it to your own assumptions in the discussion.

1. The two-step method

STEP 1 · PRESENT VALUE

Find the lump sum needed on the day retirement starts: the present value of the monthly income gap (what you need minus other income such as Social Security) over the years you expect to draw on it.

Lump sum = gap × [1 − (1 + i)^(−N)] ÷ i, with i = annual rate ÷ 12 and N = months in retirement.

STEP 2 · FUTURE VALUE → PAYMENT

Treat the lump sum as a future value and solve for the equal monthly deposit that grows to it by retirement.

Deposit = lump sum × i ÷ [(1 + i)^M − 1], with M = months of saving.

Both formulas treat payments as an ordinary annuity (at the end of each month). If your timing differs, such as deposits at the start of each month, adjust and state the assumption, as Discussion 5 asks.

Takeaway: Draw the timeline, discount the retirement income gap back to the start of retirement, then solve for the deposit that grows to that amount. Every year of delay removes compounding periods and raises the deposit. As Psalm 90:12 puts it, "teach us to number our days."
