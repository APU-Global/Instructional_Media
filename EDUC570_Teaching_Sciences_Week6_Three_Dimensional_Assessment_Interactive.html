Week 6 Interactive: Three-Dimensional Assessment

Week 6 objective: Design a multi-component performance task that gathers evidence of three-dimensional learning, and distinguish formative from summative uses of its results.

Four design criteria for 3D assessment

Drawing on the National Research Council (2014), the framework says assessments of three-dimensional learning should (1) examine students’ practices in the context of core ideas and crosscutting concepts; (2) locate students along a progression of understanding; (3) include an interpretive system for evaluating a range of responses; and (4) contain multiple components. The goal is “not to test what students know, but to see how successfully they can use and apply what they know.”

Part 1: Build a three-dimensional task

The task: “Traits and Environment,” a grade 3 performance task from the course’s NGSS Performance Assessment folder (3-LS3-2: use evidence to support the explanation that traits can be influenced by the environment). Students compare pumpkin plants grown with and without fertilizer and with more or less sunlight. Select the items for your version of the task, then select Check my task. Part 2: Formative or summative use?

The framework says the difference between formative and summative assessment lies in how the information is used. Sort each use.

Takeaway: Assess the way students learn: present a phenomenon, ask students to model, investigate, and explain, and decide in advance how you will read a range of responses. Whether an assessment is formative or summative depends on how you use the information.
