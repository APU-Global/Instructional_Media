Week 4 Interactive: Natural Law Ethics

Week 4 objective: Distinguish Aquinas's four kinds of law and recognize the main criticisms of natural law reasoning.

Key ideas

  • Objective morality: some actions are right or wrong even if a society approves of them, and reason can discover this at least in part.
  • First principle (Aquinas): "Good is to be done and pursued, and evil is to be avoided."
  • Four kinds of law: eternal (God's rational plan), natural (rational creatures' participation in it), human (laws societies make), and divine (law revealed by God).
  • Higher law: a government cannot make an act right merely by passing a law.

Practice 1: Which kind of law?

Choose the kind of law in Aquinas's scheme that each example describes.

Practice 2: Name the criticism

Each objection to a natural law argument illustrates one criticism from the overview. Choose it.

Takeaway: When you apply natural law, name the human good at stake and the principle you are using, then test your conclusion against the strongest objection, starting with the is–ought problem.
