Week 4 Interactive: Ethics on Social Media

Week 4 objective: Identify which ethical guideline a social media situation raises and apply best practices before you post.

The draft ASPPB social media guidelines

The Association of State and Provincial Psychology Boards drafted guidelines for psychology licensing boards. Social media is defined broadly as all forms of social interaction through technology. Select a category to read it.

Confidentiality

Ensure the security of client health and personal information, a duty that grows more complex when technology is the mode of communication.

Informed consent

Seek the client’s full understanding and freely given consent for services and communications delivered through technology.

Risk management

Anticipate and mitigate risk. You are responsible for your own social media presence.

Multiple relationships

Ideally avoid multiple relationships, and avoid overlap between your professional and personal social media presence.

Competence

Practice only within your competence, including competence with the technology and platforms themselves.

Professional conduct

Communicate professionally; casual online messages can reach a broader audience than intended.

Security of information

You are ultimately responsible for your accounts and for safe storage of and access to information.

Practice 2: Before you post

Think of a post you might write as a professional. Check each question you can answer with confidence.

Takeaway: The same ethics apply online: protect confidentiality, get informed consent, keep personal and professional presences separate, stay within your competence, and make your boundaries clear. When in doubt, seek understanding (and supervision) before you post.
