Week 1 Interactive: Contingency Theories in Action

Week 1 objective: Apply leadership theory to workplace situations by choosing the style that path-goal theory and Situational Leadership predict will work best.

Why the situation matters

Trait and behavioral research could not find one best way to lead, so contingency theories ask when a style works. Path-goal theory (House) says the leader’s job is to clear the path from effort to performance to rewards people value, using a directive, supportive, participative, or achievement-oriented style depending on the employees and the work. Situational Leadership (Hersey and Blanchard) matches directing, coaching, supporting, or delegating to each follower’s competence and commitment.

Part 1: Path-goal theory

Every organization is fictional. Choose the style path-goal theory predicts will work best, then select Check my answers.

Part 2: Situational Leadership

Match each follower to the style the model recommends for their competence and commitment.

Takeaway: Ask what the situation needs before you choose a style. Path-goal theory looks at the employees and the work; Situational Leadership looks at each follower’s competence and commitment. Research support for both is partial or mixed, so use them as guides alongside your own judgment and values.
