Trace the Pathway: From Hazard to Health Effect

A hazard harms health only if people are exposed to it. Trace an exposure pathway, interrupt it, weigh field, population, and laboratory evidence, and sort the types of environmental hazards.

Part 1 · Break the chain

An exposure pathway links a source, a route of exposure, and a receiving population. Choose a scenario, then pick an action at each link. If an action interrupts a link, the pathway is broken there. (Illustrative scenarios.)

Choose a scenario to begin.

Part 2 · Weigh the evidence

To link an environment to an illness, public health professionals weigh evidence from three directions. Sort each finding about lead, a well-known chemical hazard found in water, paint, and soil.

Field / environmentalMeasurements of the environment itself: soil, water, air
EpidemiologicalPatterns of health in populations of people
LaboratoryControlled experiments, in animals or cells

Part 3 · Hazards, evidence, and fairness

Hazards are commonly grouped as biological, chemical, and physical. Sort these, then answer three questions.

Who bears the burden? Some groups face higher exposure or greater sensitivity, and children are especially vulnerable to lead. Environmental justice asks who bears the burden of hazards, so consider equity as well as exposure.
