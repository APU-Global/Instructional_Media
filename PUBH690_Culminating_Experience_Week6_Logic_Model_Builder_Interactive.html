Week 6 Interactive: Logic Models and Evaluation Standards

Week 6 objective: Identify the components of a logic model and connect proposal feedback to the standards in CDC's Program Evaluation Framework.

Logic model components (CDC Action Guide, Step 2)

  • Inputs: resources needed for activities, such as personnel, partners, funding, data, and the existing evidence base.
  • Activities: strategies the program implements to effect change.
  • Outputs: products of the activities (described in the narrative).
  • Outcomes: who or what is expected to change, sequenced short-term, intermediate, and long-term.
  • Contextual factors: factors outside the program that may affect its ability to reach its outcomes.

Practice 1: Build the logic model

A fictional proposal would start a church-based walking group program for adults with high blood pressure in one county. Choose the component each piece belongs to.

Practice 2: Which standard does the feedback serve?

Each comment is feedback on a fictional draft proposal. Choose the CDC evaluation standard the suggested change would strengthen most.

Takeaway: Read your logic model as a chain of if-then links from inputs and activities to short-term, intermediate, and long-term outcomes. When you weigh feedback in Discussion 3, ask which standard it serves. Accept suggestions that strengthen relevance, rigor, objectivity, transparency, or ethics, and explain any you decline.

Concepts from Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (2024), CDC's Program Evaluation Framework Action Guide (Evaluation Standards; Step 2: Describe the Program), and CDC Program Evaluation Framework (2024). The program, numbers, and feedback are fictional.
