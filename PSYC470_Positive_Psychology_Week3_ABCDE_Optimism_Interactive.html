Week 3 Interactive: Explanatory Style and ABCDE

Week 3 objective: Recognize the three dimensions of a pessimistic explanatory style and apply each step of the ABCDE method to a setback.

Key terms

A pessimistic explanatory style explains a bad event as caused by internal, stable, and global factors. Seligman calls these dimensions:

  • Personal (internal): "It's because of me."
  • Permanent (stable): "It will always be this way."
  • Pervasive (global): "It ruins everything."

An optimistic explanation sees a setback as external, temporary, and specific. The ABCDE model changes the explanation: Adversity, Belief, Consequences, Disputation, Energization.

Practice 1: Name the explanation

Each thought follows a setback. Choose the pessimistic dimension it shows most clearly, or Optimistic if it is external, temporary, and specific.

Practice 2: Put Jordan's thinking into ABCDE

Jordan is fictional. Label each part of his experience with its ABCDE step.

Takeaway: You cannot always change the adversity, but you can test the belief. Dispute it with evidence, and look for the explanation that is external, temporary, and specific where the facts support it.
