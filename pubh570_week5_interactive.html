Design a Clinic Everyone Can Reach

Inequality shapes who can obtain care, information, and protection, and barriers differ by gender and context. Spend a limited budget on services, see who is reached and who is left out, and consider the people who face several barriers at once.

The challenge

A county sexual and reproductive health clinic wants to reach more of its community. You have 10 budget points. Choose services; each one removes certain barriers. Examples of barriers include cost, transportation, stigma, and safety. Services should be designed to reduce them, and affected communities should be included in planning.

–Average reach
–Lowest reach (who is left out)

Think intersectionally

Factors overlap. Gender interacts with race, income, age, language, and place. Overlapping disadvantages can compound risk, so avoid treating groups as uniform.
Include the community. Listening first reveals barriers a planning team may not guess. Policy can reduce or reinforce inequality, so evaluate interventions for fairness and effectiveness.

Source: PUBH 570 Week 5 Deep Dive. Personas, costs, and reach percentages are invented for practice, to illustrate trade-offs, not to predict real outcomes.
