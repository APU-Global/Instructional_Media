Week 8 Interactive: Multiple Relationships

Week 8 objective: Distinguish multiple relationships that can be managed with safeguards from those to avoid, and apply a risk-managed decision model.

The test for a multiple relationship

What it is. A psychologist in a professional role with a person is at the same time in another role with that person, or in a relationship with someone closely associated with or related to that person (APA Standard 3.05).
Not automatically unethical. Multiple relationships that would not reasonably be expected to cause impairment or harm are not necessarily unethical.
When to refrain. If the relationship could reasonably be expected to impair your objectivity, competence, or effectiveness, or risks exploitation or harm.
Where overlap happens. Rural practice, small communities such as the Deaf community, and shared settings such as recovery meetings make some overlap hard to avoid.

Practice 1: Manage it or avoid it?

All people are fictional. Decide whether each overlap can be managed with safeguards or should be avoided, then select Check my answers.

Practice 2: Weigh an overlap you cannot avoid

Boness (2016) points to Younggren and Gottlieb’s risk-managed model. Choose one “permissible with safeguards” case above and check each factor as you think it through.

Takeaway: Not every overlap is unethical. Ask whether it could impair your objectivity or risk exploitation or harm; if it can be managed, talk openly with the client, protect confidentiality, and document your reasoning. Some lines, such as sponsoring a client, always hold.
