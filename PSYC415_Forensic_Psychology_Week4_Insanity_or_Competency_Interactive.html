Week 4 Interactive: The Insanity Plea and Competency

Week 4 objective: Distinguish insanity (mental state at the time of the offense) from competency to stand trial (present ability), and recognize sound practice in a criminal responsibility evaluation.

Practice 1: Which question is the court asking?

Each referral is fictional. Decide whether it raises insanity or competency to stand trial, then select Check my answers.

Practice 2: Good practice or a problem?

Decide whether each step in a fictional criminal responsibility evaluation is good practice or a problem.

Takeaway: Insanity asks what a person understood when the act happened; competency asks whether the defendant can take part in the trial now. A diagnosis alone does not establish legal insanity. A sound evaluation warns about confidentiality, avoids leading questions, weighs conduct around the offense, and leaves the final legal conclusion to the court.
