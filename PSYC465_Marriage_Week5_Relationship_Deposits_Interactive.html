Week 5 Interactive: Making Positive Relationship Deposits

Week 5 objective: Recognize the responses that build intimacy and apply the five-to-one balance of positive to negative interactions.

The relational bank account

Lambert describes everyday interactions as deposits or withdrawals in a relational bank account. Happier couples have about five positive interactions for every negative one. One of the most frequent chances to make a deposit comes when a partner shares good news (capitalization). There are four ways to respond:

  • Active-constructive: enthusiastic, engaged support.
  • Passive-constructive: understated support.
  • Active-destructive: pointing out something negative about the news.
  • Passive-destructive: ignoring the news or changing the subject.

Practice 1: Which type of response?

Maya tells her husband Chris, "I was chosen to lead the new project at work!" Then Chris tells Maya, "I finished my first 10K run today." Choose the type for each fictional reply.

Practice 2: Deposit or withdrawal?

Decide whether each fictional moment is likely to be a deposit or a withdrawal in the relational bank account.

Practice 3: Check the balance

Count the positive and negative interactions in a fictional couple's evening (or a scenario of your own), then check the ratio.

Takeaway: Conflict is less damaging in a relationship that is otherwise highly positive. Make small deposits every day: respond with enthusiasm, say thank you, forgive, and spend time in engaging ways.

Source: Lambert, N. M. (n.d.). Positive relationships. In R. Biswas-Diener & E. Diener (Eds.), Noba Textbook Series: Psychology. DEF publishers. nobaproject.com. Scenarios are fictional.
