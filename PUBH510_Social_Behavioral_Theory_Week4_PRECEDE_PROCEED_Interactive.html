Week 4 Interactive: From Theory to Practice

Week 4 objective: Use PRECEDE-PROCEED to sort the influences on a health behavior and to plan how an intervention will be evaluated.

Phase 3: three kinds of influence

PredisposingKnowledge, attitudes, beliefs, values, and confidence.
EnablingAvailable resources, accessible services, laws and policies, issue-related skills.
ReinforcingAttitudes of family, peers, teachers, employers, providers, media, and leaders.

Practice 1: predisposing, enabling, or reinforcing?

Fewer two-year-olds in the fictional town of Cedar Falls are up to date on their vaccines. Sort each factor.

Practice 2: process, impact, or outcome?

Cedar Falls launches evening clinic hours and a mobile clinic. Match each evaluation question to its PROCEED phase.

Takeaway: PRECEDE works backward from the result the community wants; PROCEED carries the plan forward and checks it three ways. If the outcome does not move, return to the diagnosis and look for better factors to change.

Phases and factor definitions from Center for Community Health and Development. (n.d.). Chapter 2, Section 2: PRECEDE/PROCEED. Community Tool Box. University of Kansas. Cedar Falls and its residents are fictional.
