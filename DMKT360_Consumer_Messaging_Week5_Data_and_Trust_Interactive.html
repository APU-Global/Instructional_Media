Week 5 Interactive: Consumer Data and Trust

Week 5 objective: Evaluate how an organization uses consumer data in its messaging and where it oversteps.

Why trust decides what data you get

In Twilio's 2016 survey, 9 out of 10 consumers wanted to message businesses, and 85% wanted to reply, not just receive. Every conversation creates data. Consumers share it only when they trust a brand, and trust has three parts: security (protect data from being hacked or stolen), privacy (follow the law on what may be collected and used, with an easy-to-find privacy policy), and transparency (show people how their data is used and how it improves their experience). Phones are personal, so permission and timing matter most on mobile, and personalization should not be too obvious.

Practice 1: which part of trust is at stake?

Northgate Outfitters (fictional) sells outdoor gear online and in stores. Choose the part of trust each situation affects most.

Practice 2: respects trust or oversteps?

Decide whether each Northgate message respects the customer's trust or oversteps it.

Takeaway: Data is a trust, not just an asset. Keep it secure, collect and use it lawfully, be transparent about how it helps the customer, and treat people's data the way you would want yours treated.
