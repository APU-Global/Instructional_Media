Week 3 Interactive: Ethical Frameworks

Week 3 objective: Distinguish duty, consequence, and character-based ethical frameworks and identify the framework behind a clause in a code of ethics.

Four frameworks at a glance

  • Duty (means): learn the basic duties and obey them, whatever the consequences.
  • Kant: act only on a rule that could be universalized, and treat people as ends, never merely as means.
  • Utilitarianism (ends): choose what brings the greatest good to the greatest number.
  • Virtue: develop good character and trust people of good character to act well.

Practice 1: means or ends?

Harbor Lane Outfitters is a fictional outdoor-gear retailer. Decide whether each manager is reasoning from the act itself (means) or from its results (ends).

Practice 2: which framework?

Choose the framework that best describes each line of reasoning.

Practice 3: read the code

Each line comes from a draft code of ethics for Harbor Lane. Which framework does the clause reflect most clearly?

Takeaway: Decide first whether your code centers on right actions, good outcomes, or good character. Duty gives clear guidance but can be rigid; utilitarianism serves the many but can miss injustice to the few; virtue is flexible but less specific. Matthew 7:12 echoes Kant's consistency test: write standards you would want applied to yourself.
