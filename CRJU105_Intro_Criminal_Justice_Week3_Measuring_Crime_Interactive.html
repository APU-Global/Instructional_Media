Week 3 objective: Choose the crime data source that best answers a question and spot common ways crime statistics are misread.

Three kinds of crime data

Official statistics come from agencies such as the police: the FBI's Uniform Crime Reports (UCR), including NIBRS, LEOKA, and hate crime statistics, count crimes reported to police and arrests. Victimization surveys, chiefly the National Crime Victimization Survey (NCVS), ask households whether they were victims, reported or not. Self-report studies, such as Monitoring the Future, ask people what offenses they have committed. Each helps with the dark figure of crime, the crime that never comes to the attention of the system, in a different way.

Takeaway: Match the data to the question. Official statistics are best for crimes people reliably report, such as homicide; the NCVS reveals crime never reported to police; self-report studies reveal hidden and victimless offenses. Before drawing a conclusion, ask what changed: the crime, or the way it was counted?
