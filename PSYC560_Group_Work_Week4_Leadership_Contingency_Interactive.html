Week 4 Interactive: Fiedler’s Contingency Theory and Power

Week 4 focus: Discussion 4 asks you to complete the Least Preferred Coworker (LPC) Scale and use Fiedler’s three situational factors to explain your last leadership experience. Try the scale here, then build situations and see which leader Fiedler’s theory predicts will succeed.

Part 1: Least Preferred Coworker Scale

Think of the person with whom you could work least well (not necessarily the person you like least). Move each slider toward the word that describes that person. Nothing you enter is saved or sent anywhere. Use the course’s LPC worksheet for the score you report in Discussion 4.

Part 3: Which base of power?

Each workplace is fictional. Choose the source of power each person is using, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: Fiedler’s theory measures style with the LPC scale and judges situations by leader-member relations, position power, and task structure: low LPC (task-oriented) leaders are predicted to do best in very favorable or very unfavorable situations, high LPC leaders in the middle. A leader also draws on six bases of power; referent power is more effective than formal power bases. In Discussion 4, use your own score and the three factors to explain a real leadership experience.
