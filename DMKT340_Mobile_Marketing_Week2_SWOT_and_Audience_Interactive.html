Week 2 Interactive: SWOT and Audience

Week 2 objective: Sort a church's situation into strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats, and describe its audience by segmentation type.

How SWOT and segmentation work

Strengths and weaknesses are internal to the organization; opportunities and threats come from its environment. Two common mistakes: listing an internal factor as external, and listing a strategic move (something you would do) as an opportunity. SWOT is a starting point for ideas, not the final strategy. To describe an audience, marketers use demographic (age, income, education, religion, family size), psychographic (activities, interests, opinions, attitudes, values, lifestyles), geographic (where people live), and behavioral (how people behave toward a product) segmentation.

Takeaway: SWOT helps you see the situation honestly: keep internal and external factors apart, keep strategies out of the grid, and pair your SWOT with a clear picture of who you serve and why they care.
