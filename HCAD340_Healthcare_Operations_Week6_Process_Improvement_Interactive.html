Week 6 Interactive: Process Improvement Methods

Week 6 objective: Apply process improvement methodologies to enhance patient care delivery (CLO 5).

Three ideas to apply

Fix the system
Errors in health care most often come from system problems, not individual acts. Look at the process before blaming a person.
Model for Improvement
What do we want to accomplish? How will we know a change is an improvement? What change will result in improvement? Test changes in small Plan-Do-Study-Act (PDSA) cycles.
Six Sigma DMAIC
Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control: a structured, data-driven method for reducing variation and defects.

Practice 1: outcome, process, or balancing measure?

Oak Hill Hospital (fictional) wants to shorten the wait between a discharge order and the patient going home. Classify each measure.

Practice 2: which DMAIC phase?

A hospital (fictional) wants faster CT scans for emergency department patients who are not admitted. Place each activity in its DMAIC phase.

Practice 3: which step of the PDSA cycle?

Oak Hill tests a bedside discharge checklist. Put each step in the cycle.

Takeaway: Start with the system, not the person. Set an aim, choose outcome, process, and balancing measures, and test changes small with PDSA cycles before spreading them. For problems driven by variation, DMAIC moves from Define to Control. Use these methods for Mr. Smith’s case in Discussion 6 and in your Data-Driven Decision Making recommendations.
