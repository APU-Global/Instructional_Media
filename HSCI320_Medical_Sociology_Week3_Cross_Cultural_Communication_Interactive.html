Week 3 Interactive: Cross-Cultural Communication

Week 3 objective: Apply Chapter 3's practice points to patient encounters with language and cultural barriers.

Why it matters

Patients with limited English proficiency (LEP) face higher risks of unnecessary testing, incomplete assessment, misdiagnosis, delayed treatment, poor symptom management, readmission, and severe adverse events. When providers avoid conversations because of time pressure, patients and families lose trust. Chapter 3 recommends professional interpreters over family interpretation, avoiding apps such as Google Translate for difficult care conversations, pairing patients with staff who share their language, reporting unsafe practices, and advocating for more services.

Takeaway: Trust depends on communication patients can actually understand. Use professional interpreters for difficult conversations, save apps for small talk, give LEP patients the same time and attention as others, and report and advocate when language barriers make care unsafe.
