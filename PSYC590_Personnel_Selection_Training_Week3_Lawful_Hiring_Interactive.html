Week 3 Interactive: Lawful, Job-Related Selection

Week 3 focus: Chapter 5 asks two questions of every hiring practice: is it tied to the job, and is it applied the same way to every candidate? Practice both here as you prepare Discussion 3.

Part 1: Screen the interview questions

A fictional hiring team at Cobalt Route Co. drafted these questions for a project coordinator role that requires 50 percent travel. Decide whether each one is job-related or should be avoided.

Part 2: Score a candidate with a weighted selection model

In the statistical approach, the hiring team sets the criteria and weights before interviews, then multiplies each rating (1–5) by its weight. Rate one fictional candidate and compare three decision rules.

Part 3: Name the interview bias

Each fictional interviewer below lets a bias creep in. Choose the bias your text names.

Takeaway: Set job-related criteria and weights first, ask every candidate the same structured questions, avoid questions that reveal a protected characteristic, and use tests that match the job. A scored model limits disparate treatment, but you still need to check the results for disparate impact.

Concepts, lawful and unlawful question examples, the selection models, and bias types from Human Resource Management v1.0 (Saylor Academy, 2012), Sections 5.1 "The Selection Process," 5.3 "Interviewing," and 5.4 "Testing and Selecting," CC BY-NC-SA 3.0. The weights for "Ability to give bad news to a client" (5) and "Dress" (1) follow the text's sample model; the other criteria, weights, organizations, and people are fictional. Laws vary by state and change over time; this activity is practice, not legal advice.
