Week 4 Interactive: Situational Triggers of Aggression

Week 4 objective: Identify situational factors that provoke aggression and match them to evidence-based ways of reducing provocation.

Why this matters

Situational crime prevention starts from two ideas: "opportunity makes the thief," and offenders make choices with limited rationality. It lists five ways to change situations: increase the effort, increase the risks, reduce the rewards, reduce provocations, and remove excuses. The reduce-provocations column speaks directly to this week's question about situational triggers and aggression.

Practice 1: which strategy?

Each measure comes from the Twenty Five Techniques of Situational Prevention. Choose the strategy it belongs to.

Practice 2: fix the trigger

Fictional scenario: Riverside Stadium hosts a heated rivalry game. Security manager Dana Ortiz reviews last season's incident reports.

For each problem, choose the provocation technique that fits best.

Takeaway: Aggression does not come only from inside a person. Frustration, crowding, insults, peer pressure, and imitation can all push behavior, and each can be reduced by changing the situation. NIJ research on online radicalization makes a parallel point about media: exposure matters, but its effect depends on the person and the people around them.
