Week 6 Interactive: Workplace Ethics Pressures

Week 6 objective: Identify the situational pressures that lead professionals to overlook ethical conduct and use questions that bring them to light.

Why do good professionals cut corners?

Behavioral ethics studies why well-intentioned people sometimes do bad things. Unethical behavior is often committed by professionals who know their codes and believe they practice ethically. Open each pressure to read it.

Self-serving bias

We process information and act in ways that serve our own interests, often without awareness. Rewards for unethical acts tend to be more immediate and tangible than rewards for ethical ones.

Incrementalism (the slippery slope)

Small lapses become normal, and similar but larger lapses follow, often unnoticed by the person or by others.

Framing

The way a situation is described changes what seems right. Before the Challenger launch, engineers were asked to take off their “engineer hats” and put on “management hats.”

Obedience to authority

People follow directives from supervisors or other authority figures, even when they know a rule is about to be broken.

Conformity bias

People conform to the behavior of their peer group to belong and avoid exclusion, even against their own judgment.

Overconfidence bias

People overestimate their own moral capacity and assume their behavior is ethical when it may not be.

Setting events

Time pressure, fatigue, and a lack of resources make unethical behavior more likely.

Practice 2: Pause before you act

Cicero (2021) suggests that teams review questions like these after reaching a decision but before acting on it. Think of a recent decision at work, and check each question you have honestly asked.

Takeaway: Knowing the code does not guarantee following it. Self-interest, small steps, framing, authority, conformity, overconfidence, and fatigue are predictable forces. Name them, ask the questions before you act, and remember that faithfulness in little things protects you from larger failures.
