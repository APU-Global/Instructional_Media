Week 2 Interactive: Cybernetics and Feedback

Week 2 objective: Describe a system the way a cybernetician would: its parts, its feedback loops, and the controls that keep it on course.

Three ideas to use

Feedback

Information about the past or present is used to influence the present or future. Cause and effect form a loop, so actions “feed back” into themselves.

The control loop

Set standards, measure actual performance, compare, and take corrective action. Then measure again.

Dampen or amplify

Feedback linkages can dampen a change (pull the system back toward a level) or amplify it (make it grow).

Part 1: Build the system map

Harbor Street Bike Co-op (fictional) fixes donated bikes and sells them at low cost. Label each element as an input, a process, an output, or feedback.

Part 2: Which kind of control?

Control can look back (feedback), happen in real time (concurrent), or act before any output changes (feedforward). Classify each move by the co-op's manager.

Part 3: Dampening or amplifying loop?

Follow each loop around once. Does it pull the system back toward a level (dampening) or make the change keep growing (amplifying)?

Takeaway: To take a cybernetics approach in your discussion, draw the boundary, show what flows in and out, and then draw the feedback loop that tells the system whether it is meeting its standard. Mark whether each loop dampens or amplifies change.
