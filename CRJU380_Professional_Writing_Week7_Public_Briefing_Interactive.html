Week 7 Interactive: Public Briefings and Accountability

Week 7 objective: Evaluate law enforcement public communication for transparency, audience response, and professional delivery.

The difference

Information output
A "just the facts" reading of a police report. It shares, but it does not necessarily communicate or move anyone to act.
Effective communication
Starts with the question "What do I want people to do with this information?" It helps people think, feel, and act, and it practices transparency without compromising an investigation.

Practice 1: sort the draft statement

Fictional scenario: Lieutenant Reyes will brief the Riverton City Council about a series of overnight car break-ins downtown. An arrest is not yet made.

Sort each line from the draft. Output = accurate but only transmits data. Communicates = connects with the audience and asks for a response. Overshares = could compromise the investigation or reveal information prematurely.

Practice 2: delivery check

Lieutenant Reyes rehearses the briefing. For each habit, choose Keep or Change, based on the FBI's public speaking guidelines.

Takeaway: Public trust depends on substance and delivery. Give the facts, then say what they mean for residents and what you need from them. Be open about process while protecting active cases. In the room, respect the audience's time and attention.
