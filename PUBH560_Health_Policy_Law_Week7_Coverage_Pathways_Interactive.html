Week 7 Interactive: Coverage Pathways in U.S. Health Policy

Week 7 objective: Describe how Medicare, Medicaid, and the Affordable Care Act shape access to health coverage.

Key rules (KFF Health Policy 101, 2025)

Medicare (1965, Title XVIII): people 65 or older regardless of income; people under 65 on Social Security Disability Insurance after a two-year waiting period; people with end-stage renal disease or ALS with no waiting period.
Medicaid (1965, Title XIX): low-income people. In states that adopted the ACA expansion, nearly all adults under 65 with incomes up to 138% of the federal poverty level (FPL).
ACA Marketplace: subsidies for people without other affordable coverage; in non-expansion states, from 100% FPL. Adults below 100% FPL in those states are not eligible for tax credits and generally not for Medicaid.
Young adults: the ACA lets them stay on a parent's private plan up to age 26.

Practice: choose the pathway

Each person is fictional and a U.S. citizen. Assume no employer coverage unless stated. Choose the most likely pathway, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: Coverage depends on age, disability, income, and the state you live in. The same income can mean Medicaid in one state and no subsidized option in another, which is why the ACA's 2012 Supreme Court ruling and each state's expansion choice matter for access.
