Week 4 Interactive: Reading Evaluation Data

Week 4 objective: Interpret descriptive and inferential results in an evaluation report and judge whether a summary, including an AI-generated one, is supported, as Discussion 4 asks.

The tools you need

MEAN
Add the values and divide by how many there are.
STANDARD DEVIATION
The typical distance from the mean; about 68% of observations typically fall within one standard deviation.
Z-TEST FOR A SAMPLE MEAN
z = (sample mean − population mean) ÷ (σ ÷ √n). At alpha = 0.05 (two-tailed), the critical value is 1.96.
FOUR CONDITIONS OF CAUSALITY
Logical time ordering, correlation, mechanism, and nonspuriousness.

Part 1: Describe the data

The fictional Westbrook Library literacy program reports these session attendance counts for five branches: 12, 18, 9, 15, 21.

Part 2: Test the difference

Statewide, adult learners score a mean of 60 on a reading assessment, with a standard deviation of 10. A random sample of 25 Westbrook participants averages 64.

Part 3: Judge the claims

An AI tool summarized a fictional evaluation report. Decide what each statement needs.

Takeaway: Describe first, then infer, then ask whether a causal claim meets all four conditions. "Simply reporting analytic results is insufficient to draw evaluative conclusions" (CDC, 2024). Examine every summary against the report: "The one who states his case first seems right, until the other comes and examines him" (Proverbs 18:17, ESV).
