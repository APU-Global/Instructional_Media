Week 6 Interactive: Christian Vocation

Week 6 objective: Distinguish the Christian understanding of vocation from the “higher calling” myth and from a career-only view.

What is vocation?

“Vocation” comes from the Latin word for “calling.” We often think of vocation as career and employment, but Christian vocation affirms that God can use his people for good in any place and space, calling them to serve him with the best they have in any circumstance. A prevailing myth holds that full-time Christian ministry is the only way to fully live out a Christian vocation; in fact, formal ministry is one of countless vocations for which God calls and equips his people.

  • Christian vocation: placing God-given gifts into his service, wherever that may be.
  • The “higher calling” myth: only formal ministry fully counts as serving God.
  • Career-only view: vocation means nothing more than a job or job title.
Takeaway: Christian vocation is a whole-life calling: God calls and equips each person with gifts, talents, and strengths to serve him in any place and circumstance. Formal ministry is one calling among countless others, and a job title is only part of the picture. The Oswald Liturgy puts it as a prayer: “All that I do, Lord, I place into Your hands.”
