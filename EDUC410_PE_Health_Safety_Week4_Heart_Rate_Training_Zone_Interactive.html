Week 4 Interactive: Find Your Training Zone

Week 4 objective: Calculate resting, maximum, and training heart rates the way the Heart Rate Worksheet does, and use a 10-second pulse count to judge exercise intensity.

The worksheet in four steps

1. Resting heart rate. Count your pulse for 60 seconds first thing in the morning, before any activity, at the radial artery (base of the thumb on the wrist) or the carotid artery (side of the neck). A normal resting rate can range from about 40 to 100 beats per minute (bpm), and it usually decreases as cardiovascular fitness improves.
2. Maximum heart rate (MHR). 220 minus your age. For safety, you should never work at your maximum; it is only a reference point.
3. Training zone. MHR × 0.60 (low end) to MHR × 0.80 (high end). Lower zones are recommended for beginners, people with health risks, and during pregnancy.
4. 10-second zone. Divide each end of the zone by 6. During exercise, count your pulse for 10 seconds and compare, so you can check your effort and get back to moving quickly.

Takeaway: Resting heart rate is a snapshot of cardiovascular health, and a training zone of 60% to 80% of maximum heart rate tells you how hard to work.
