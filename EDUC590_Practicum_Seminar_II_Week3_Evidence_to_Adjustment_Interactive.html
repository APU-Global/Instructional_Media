Week 3 Interactive: Monitoring and Adjusting Instruction

Week 3 objective: Distinguish formative from summative assessment and choose an instructional adjustment that the evidence supports.

Evidence first, then decisions

The Guide defines formative assessment as "a process teachers and students use during instruction that provides feedback to adjust ongoing teaching strategies and student learning" (assessment for learning), and summative assessment as evaluating learning at the conclusion of an instructional period against specific criteria (assessment of learning). Rubric 2.5 asks you to use assessment to monitor progress toward the ELA/Literacy and ELD goals and to make adjustments that support students' literacy and language development.

Practice 1: formative or summative?

All lessons are fictional. Decide how each assessment is being used.

Practice 2: what does the evidence say to do next?

Read each result from a fictional learning segment and choose the best next step.

Takeaway: Plan your formative check and your summative assessment with their evidence indicators before you teach. When results come in, decide whether the need is class-wide, limited to a few students, or about language, and note each adjustment for your Part 4 commentary.
