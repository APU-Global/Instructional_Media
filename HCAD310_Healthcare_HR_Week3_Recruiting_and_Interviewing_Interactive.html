Week 3 Interactive: Recruiting and Interviewing

Week 3 objective: Examine the processes of employee recruitment and selection (CLO 2). Use these skills when you review the AI-generated interview questions in this week's assignment.

The role: Patient Access Representative

Bayside Community Hospital (fictional) is hiring a Patient Access Representative to register patients, verify insurance, schedule appointments, and answer questions at a busy front desk. The panel will use a structured interview: the same job-related questions for every candidate, rated against answers planned in advance. Structured interviews can be up to twice as effective at predicting job performance as unstructured ones.

Practice 1: keep, or remove?

Classify each draft question as Behavioural (a past example), Situational (what would you do), or one to Remove because it is not job related.

Practice 2: spot the interviewer bias

Unconscious biases shrink when interviewers know they exist. Name the bias in each situation.

Practice 3: which recruiting source is working?

Change the numbers to compare two sources. The yield ratio is the percentage of applicants who reach the interview stage. Cost per hire adds the month's recruiting costs and divides by the number of hires. The starting values are the textbook's examples.

Takeaway: Set selection criteria from the job analysis before you read a resume, ask every candidate the same job-related questions, and watch for your own biases. Track yield ratios and cost per hire so you invest in the recruiting sources that work, and give candidates a realistic preview of the job.
