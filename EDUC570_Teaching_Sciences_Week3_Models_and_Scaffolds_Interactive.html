Week 3 Interactive: Models and Supports for All

Week 3 objective: Distinguish models from representations and plan scaffolds that help all students make their thinking visible.

Models explain; representations show

A model is for “describing, explaining, and predicting natural phenomena and communicating scientific ideas to others.” In K–2, students learn to distinguish a model from the actual object or event; by grades 3–5 they identify a model’s limitations and revise it based on evidence. Every student can model when scaffolds are contingent, fade over time, and transfer responsibility to the student, without lowering the challenge.

Part 1: Model or representation?

Decide whether each example only shows something or uses it to explain or predict a phenomenon.

Part 2: Planned or just-in-time?

Teachers plan some scaffolds in advance and give others in the moment. Sort each support.

Part 3: What does this part of the model do?

Students in a fictional third-grade class are modeling how a cloud forms. Choose the modeling tool that does each job.

Takeaway: Ask students to draw what they think is happening, not just what something looks like. Plan scaffolds in advance, respond in the moment, and fade both so students take over the modeling. Revising a model is a sign of learning, not a mistake.
