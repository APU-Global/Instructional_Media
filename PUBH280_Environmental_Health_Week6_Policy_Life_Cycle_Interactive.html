Week 6 Interactive: The Policy Life Cycle

Week 6 objective: Match environmental health research questions to the stage of the policy process where they are asked.

Six stages of a policy

Diagnosis: does a health problem exist, how big is it, who carries it, and what causes it? Design: what impacts, tradeoffs, costs, and co-benefits would a proposed policy have? Pilot: what happens when the policy is tested in a limited area or time, and do people accept it? Implementation: how far has the rollout gone, and what is it achieving so far? Operation: once fully in effect, what are its actual impacts, are they equitable, and is it cost-effective? Dismantling: what impact does a past policy still have today?

Practice: which stage is it?

Each city is fictional. Choose the stage for each research question, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: Each stage asks a different question. Diagnosis and design rely on estimates; pilot, implementation, and operation can measure what actually happened. Strong advocacy calls for evaluation at every stage and for checking who benefits.

Stages and research questions from Benavides, J., et al. (2022), "Methods for evaluating environmental health impacts at different stages of the policy process in cities," Current Environmental Health Reports, 9(2), 183–195 (PMC8986968). Cities and scenarios are fictional.
