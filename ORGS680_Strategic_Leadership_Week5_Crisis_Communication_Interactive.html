Week 5 Interactive: Strategic Communication in a Crisis

Week 5 objective: Recommend a crisis communication approach: the plan elements, the facts to gather, and the channel that fits each audience and message.

The scenario

At 6:10 a.m., a fire breaks out in the packing area of Westlake Supply, a fictional regional distributor with 240 employees. Firefighters respond, the building is evacuated, and two workers are taken to the hospital with smoke inhalation. Reporters are calling by 7:00.

A crisis communication plan has four elementsA crisis communication team with contact information, a designated spokesperson, a meeting place, and a media plan with procedures.
Information richnessFace-to-face, telephone, and video are rich channels; handheld devices, blogs, letters, and memos are medium; formal documents and spreadsheets are lean.

Practice 1: Which plan element does the job?

Choose the element of the crisis communication plan that each task belongs to.

Practice 2: Rich or written?

Choose the better channel for each message. Rich channels convey feelings and allow immediate feedback; written channels convey facts and leave a record.

Practice 3: First questions

In the first minutes of a crisis, which questions keep you focused on who, what, and where?

Takeaway: Plan before the crisis, gather the facts fast, and share them honestly through the channel that fits each audience: rich channels for people who are hurting, written ones for the record.
