Week 5 Interactive: Logic Model Components

Week 5 objective: Design a health promotion program by separating its inputs, activities, outputs, and outcomes.

The columns of a logic model

The CDC self-study guide describes a program by its resources, activities, outputs, and outcomes, linked by arrows that show which activities produce which outcomes.

Inputs
People, money, information, and authority needed, usually from outside the program, to mount the activities.
Activities
What the program and its staff do: outreach, training, service delivery, partnerships, communication.
Outputs
Direct, tangible, countable products of activities.
Outcomes
Changes in someone or something other than the program and its staff, from short-term to long-term.

Practice: sort the program parts

A fictional city health department is adapting an evidence-based quit-smoking program for a diverse neighborhood. Choose the column for each item, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: Outputs show the program did its work; outcomes show that something changed for someone else. A good logic model makes the links between them visible, and planning for inputs such as funding, partnerships, and staff is what lets a program last.

Component definitions from Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (2011), Introduction to Program Evaluation for Public Health Programs: A Self-Study Guide, Step 2 "Describe the Program." The program and its numbers are fictional.
