Week 2 Interactive: Allocating Resources by Criteria

Week 2 objective: Explain the allocation of human, financial, and material resources in healthcare settings (CLO 2).

Three ideas to apply

Three levels
Macro: national or regional. Meso: the institution, such as a hospital or care home. Micro: the patient's bedside.
Score against the average
For each indicator, a unit scores 1 below the average range, 2 within about ±20% of the average, and 3 above it.
Hear stakeholders
“Nothing about me, without me”: the people most affected by a decision deserve a voice in it.

Practice 1: which level of decision?

Each organization is fictional. Choose the level at which each decision is made, then select Check my answers.

Practice 2: score each unit

Riverbend Medical Center (fictional) compares clinical visits per equivalent doctor for five units: Cardiology 280, Neurology 380, Orthopedics 520, Pediatrics 610, Oncology 710. Work out the average and the ±20% band, then score each unit.

Show the average and band

Average = (280 + 380 + 520 + 610 + 710) ÷ 5 = 2,500 ÷ 5 = 500. The ±20% band runs from 500 × 0.8 = 400 to 500 × 1.2 = 600.

Practice 3: when lower is better

For some indicators, a low value is good. Riverbend’s claims against clinical activity per doctor: Unit A 0.5, Unit B 1.0, Unit C 1.5 (average 1.0; band 0.8 to 1.2). Here, fewer claims than average scores 3.

Practice 4: sound practice or warning sign?

Decide whether each allocation practice is sound or a warning sign.

Takeaway: Allocation happens at macro, meso, and micro levels. At the hospital (meso) level, clear indicators scored against the average reveal which units need attention and guard against impulsive choices. Remember that indicators are proxies: pair them with validated data, the voices of the people affected, and reserves for the unexpected.
