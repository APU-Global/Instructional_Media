Week 4 Interactive: Multi-Tiered System of Supports

Week 4 objective: Distinguish Tier 1, Tier 2, and Tier 3 supports and recognize when the core program itself needs attention.

The tiers at a glance

Tier 1: core (first teaching)
Differentiated, UDL-based instruction for all students in general education, including integrated and designated ELD for English learners. Should bring at least 80% of students to grade-level expectations.
Tier 2: strategic, targeted
Added to Tier 1 for generally no more than 15% of students, such as 30 minutes of daily small-group instruction for six to eight weeks. Temporary, with frequent progress monitoring.
Tier 3: intensive
For roughly 5% of students who did not benefit enough from Tier 2: e.g., 40 to 60 minutes daily, reduced ratios, or a secondary double block, with continued access to grade-level standards.

If fewer than 80% of students succeed in Tier 1, the school examines its curriculum and teaching practices first.

Practice

Each school and student is fictional. Choose where the support belongs, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: Tiers describe the intensity of instruction, not programs, labels, or staff. Strong UDL-based Tier 1 comes first; Tier 2 and Tier 3 add time and focus, are monitored often, and aim to return students to success in the core.

Tier descriptions and percentages from English Language Arts/English Language Development Framework (California Department of Education, adopted 2014), Chapter 9 "Access and Equity" (Multi-Tiered System of Supports). Schools and students are fictional.
