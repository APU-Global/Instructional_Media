Week 6 Interactive: Persuade, Don't Trick

Week 6 objective: Recognize Cialdini's six principles of persuasion and the eight common fallacies, so your presentations persuade with real reasons, not tricks.

Persuasion and false logic (Chapter 14)

  • Six principles (Cialdini): reciprocity, scarcity, authority, commitment and consistency, consensus, and liking.
  • Eight fallacies: red herring (a diversion), straw man (a weak version of the other side), begging the question (assuming the very point in question), circular argument (a claim used to prove itself), ad populum (everyone believes it), ad hominem (attacking the person), non sequitur (it does not follow), and post hoc ergo propter hoc (after this, therefore because of this).
  • Ethics: no manipulation, deception, bribery, or coercion.

Practice 1: name the principle

A sales team is pitching a fictional office chair, the Nimbus. Choose the principle each line uses, then select Check my answers.

Practice 2: spot the fallacy

Each line comes from a fictional business presentation. Name the fallacy.

Takeaway: Persuasion is not the problem; tricks are. Build your case on honest evidence, name your purpose, and let the five-step checklist guide you: get attention, identify the need, satisfy the need, present a vision or solution, and call for action.
