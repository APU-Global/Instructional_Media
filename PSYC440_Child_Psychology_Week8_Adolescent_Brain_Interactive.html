Week 8 Interactive: The Adolescent Brain

Week 8 objective: Explain how brain development in adolescence relates to emotion, risk-taking, and mental health, and check common claims you may hear in podcasts against research.

The big picture

During adolescence the brain does not get larger; it becomes more interconnected and specialized. The limbic system, which regulates emotion and reward, changes with puberty, while the prefrontal cortex, which handles impulse control and planning, keeps developing into the mid-20s. Laurence Steinberg compares this to engaging a powerful engine before the braking system is in place.

Takeaway: The teen brain is maturing, not broken. Strong reward drives arrive years before full self-control, so adults can scaffold decisions, watch for early signs of anxiety and depression, and help teens build healthy friendships that support rather than deepen distress.
