Week 1 Interactive: Words, Assumptions, and Questions

Week 1 objective: Identify negative prior assumptions about disability and reframe them as respectful, curious questions.

Why words and questions matter

CDC calls attitudinal barriers "the most basic" barrier to inclusion because they feed every other barrier. Two everyday habits help: people-first language, which names the person before the disability, and "I perspective" questions, which turn an assumption into something you can investigate and act on.

Practice 1: Choose the people-first wording

For each situation, choose the wording CDC recommends, then select Check my answers.

Practice 2: Assumption or "I perspective" question?

Discussion 1 asks you to turn assumptions into questions you can act on. Decide what each statement is.

Takeaway: Lead with the person, ask before you label, and turn every "they can't" into "How can I...?" Curiosity is the first step toward a plan that fits.
