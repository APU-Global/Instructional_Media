Week 4 Interactive: Program Logic and SMART Objectives

Week 4 objective: Connect program activities to outputs, outcomes, and goals, and recognize what makes an objective SMART.

Key ideas

If-then logic

If resources are provided, activities can be conducted; if activities are conducted, outputs are produced; if outputs are produced, outcomes can occur; outcomes contribute to the goal.

Assumptions

Conditions outside the program's control that must hold true for each level to lead to the next.

Indicators

Quantitative or qualitative measures of change, each with a baseline at the start and a target.

SMART

Specific, Measurable, Achievable (within budget, time, and scope), Relevant, and Time-bound.

Practice 1: where does it belong in the logic?

A fictional university is planning a peer-support program for student mental health. Choose the level of the logical framework for each statement.

Practice 2: what is missing?

Each draft objective for the same fictional program is weakest on one SMART criterion. Choose that criterion.

Takeaway: A strong objective names who will change, by how much from what baseline, how you will measure it, and by when, and it sits on a clear if-then chain from activities to the goal. That is the standard your own SMART goal should meet.
