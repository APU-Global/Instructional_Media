Week 5 Interactive: Coaching and Mentoring

Week 5 objective: Tell coaching, mentoring, and peer coaching apart, and choose the right tool for a colleague's need.

Three development tools

Coaching
Inquiry-based. Maximizes potential by working on a person's perceptions, self-confidence, and creative drive; typically focuses on immediate performance and skill development. The coachee drives the agenda.
Mentoring
Provides guidance, direction, and career advice, usually from a senior mentor (often outside the protégé's chain of supervision) to a junior protégé; supports long-term career growth.
Peer coaching
Two or more professionals reflect on current practice, share ideas, and solve problems together; each acts as both coach and coachee.
Shared benefits
Better performance, engagement, culture, and retention; mentoring also helps identify and develop future leaders.

Practice: which tool is this?

Each scenario is fictional. Decide which development tool it describes, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: Mentor when someone needs your knowledge and experience for long-term growth; coach when they need to find their own answer and build confidence now; and in your capstone group, do both as peers. As Paul entrusted what he learned to Timothy, good mentoring multiplies leaders (2 Timothy 2:2).
