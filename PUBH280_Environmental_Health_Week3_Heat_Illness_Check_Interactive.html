Week 3 Interactive: Heat and Health

Week 3 objective: Recognize the signs of heat-related illness, a growing health concern in a warming climate, and know when it becomes an emergency.

A spectrum of heat illness

Too much heat is not safe for anyone, and it is riskier for older adults, who adjust less well to sudden temperature changes and are more likely to have chronic conditions or take medicines that affect cooling (NIA, 2022).

  • Heat syncope: sudden dizziness or fainting when active in the heat.
  • Heat cramps: painful muscle spasms; temperature and pulse usually normal.
  • Heat edema: swollen ankles and feet.
  • Heat rash: red clusters of small blisters from heavy sweating.
  • Heat exhaustion: thirst, dizziness, weakness, nausea, heavy sweating, cold and clammy skin; a warning sign.
  • Heat stroke: body temperature above 104°F, confusion, fainting, no sweating, dry flushed skin; a medical emergency.

Takeaway: Heat exhaustion is a warning; heat stroke is an emergency. Risk is highest for older adults with chronic illness, people on certain medicines, and people living without air conditioning or fans, so public health plans cooling centers and outreach before a heat wave.
