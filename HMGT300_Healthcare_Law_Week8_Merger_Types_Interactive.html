Week 8 objective: Classify healthcare mergers as horizontal, vertical, or conglomerate (including market-extension and product-extension deals) as the first step in judging their effect on competition.

Merger types under Section 7 of the Clayton Act

Section 7 bars a merger whose effect "may be substantially to lessen competition" in any line of commerce in any section of the country. A horizontal merger joins competitors. A vertical merger joins a supplier and a customer (a customer buying its supplier is backward integration; a supplier buying its customer is forward integration). A conglomerate merger joins businesses that are not directly related, including market-extension deals (same business, different location) and product-extension deals (a related product). For horizontal deals, courts weigh market shares, concentration, and the trend toward concentration.

Takeaway: Classifying the deal tells you what to examine. Horizontal mergers raise questions about market share and concentration; vertical mergers about foreclosing rivals from suppliers or customers; conglomerate mergers about entrenchment or lost potential competition. Claimed savings are not a legal defense, so ask whether they reach patients.
