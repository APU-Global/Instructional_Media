Week 6 Interactive: Complex Adaptive Systems

Week 6 objective: Distinguish complicated systems from complex adaptive systems (CAS) and describe a CAS in plain language.

A Ferrari and a rainforest

Complicated

An expert mechanic can take a Ferrari apart and put it back together without changing a thing. The whole is the sum of its parts, and at least one right answer exists. Its aim is designed in: it is a mechanical system.

Complex adaptive

A rainforest is in constant flux, and the whole is far more than the sum of its parts. Its many agents adapt in response to changes other agents bring about, and new, often unpredictable patterns emerge, as in ecosystems, cities, economies, and termite mounds.

Part 1: Sort the systems

Decide whether each example is best described as complicated or complex adaptive.

Part 2: Read the neighborhood

The fictional town of Millbrook opens a Saturday farmers’ market. No one plans the layout beyond the first week. Vendors move their stalls toward the busiest paths, shoppers change their routes to find favorite vendors, a coffee cart arrives because crowds linger, and musicians start playing where the coffee cart parks. A year later, the market has a lively “center” that no one designed. When the town tried a strict stall-assignment rule one month, vendors left and crowds thinned.

Is each statement about the Millbrook market true or false?

Takeaway: To explain complex adaptive theory to an older relative, compare something you could take apart and rebuild unchanged (a car) with something that keeps adjusting to itself (a garden, a neighborhood, a family). In a CAS, leaders shape conditions; they cannot script every outcome.
