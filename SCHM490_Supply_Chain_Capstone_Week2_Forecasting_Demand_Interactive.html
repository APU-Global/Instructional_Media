Week 2 Interactive: Forecasting Demand

Week 2 objective: Match a forecasting method to the demand pattern, measure forecast error, and see how collaborative planning, forecasting, and replenishment (CPFR) reduces it.

The scenario

Harbor Paddle Co. (a fictional company) makes touring kayaks and sells them through outdoor retailers. Monthly demand for its best-selling model has been climbing:

Step 1: Forecast month 7 three ways

Enter each forecast for month 7, then select Check.

a. 3-month simple moving average (average of months 4, 5, and 6)

b. Weighted moving average with weights 0.5 (month 6), 0.3 (month 5), and 0.2 (month 4)

c. Exponential smoothing with alpha = 0.3. The month 6 forecast was 140. Use F7 = F6 + α(D6 − F6).

Step 3: Name the demand pattern

Choose the pattern that best describes each fictional product's demand, then select Check my answers.

Step 4: Forecast a seasonal product

Harbor Paddle's spray skirts sold 180, 240, 300, and 280 units in quarters 1 to 4 last year (1,000 in total, an average of 250 per quarter). The seasonal index is a quarter's sales divided by the average.

Step 5: Put CPFR in order

Harbor Paddle and its largest retailer agree to plan together. Use the arrow buttons to put the CPFR steps in order, then select Check order.

    Takeaway: Look at the pattern before you pick a method. Averages smooth out noise in steady demand but trail behind a rising trend; seasonal products need a seasonal index. Track error with MAD, and shrink it at the source by sharing data with trading partners through CPFR.
