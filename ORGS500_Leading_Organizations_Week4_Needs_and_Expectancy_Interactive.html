Week 4 Interactive: McClelland and Vroom

Week 4 objective: Apply McClelland’s acquired-needs theory and Vroom’s expectancy theory to diagnose motivation problems.

Two theories from Discussion 4

McClelland’s acquired-needs theory: people acquire needs for achievement, affiliation, and power, and the dominant need drives behavior. Vroom’s expectancy theory: people put in effort when they believe effort leads to performance (expectancy), performance leads to rewards (instrumentality), and the rewards are worth having (valence). If any link is broken, motivation drops.

Part 1: Which need is dominant?

Each employee is fictional. Choose the need that seems to drive each person.

Part 2: Which expectancy link is broken?

Each team is fictional. Find the link that is keeping people from putting in effort.

Takeaway: Diagnose before you motivate. Learn each person’s dominant need, then check all three links: can they do it, will performance be rewarded, and do they value the reward? The same questions apply to on-site and virtual teams.
