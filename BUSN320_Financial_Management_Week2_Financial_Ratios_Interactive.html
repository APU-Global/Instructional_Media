Week 2 Interactive: Financial Ratios

Week 2 objective: Calculate liquidity, operations, profitability, and leverage ratios from financial statements and interpret what they suggest about a company's financial health.

The case: Harbor Outfitters (fictional)

Harbor Outfitters sells outdoor gear on credit to retailers. Here are two years of simplified data, in thousands of dollars. Use year-end figures and a 360-day year.

Step 1: Calculate the Year 2 ratios

Enter each Year 2 ratio (two decimals are enough; enter percentages as numbers, such as 12.5). Select Check my ratios to see how you did.

Step 2: Compare the years

Ratios raise questions; they do not answer them by themselves. Compare Year 2 with Year 1 (a longitudinal comparison), then choose the best reading of each trend.

Takeaway: Read the four areas together. Harbor's sales fell while inventory, receivables, and debt rose, so liquidity, efficiency, returns, and interest coverage all weakened at once. That pattern is the question an analyst brings to the 10-K: why is the company slipping, and what is management doing about it?
