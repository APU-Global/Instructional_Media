Week 5 Interactive: Capacity, Bottlenecks, and Utilization

Week 5 objective: Calculate the daily capacity of each step in a process, identify the bottleneck, decide where an improvement actually raises output, and compute utilization and efficiency. These are everyday operations skills, like the ones you practice in this week's job simulation.

Key terms

  • Step capacity = minutes available per day ÷ minutes per unit at that step.
  • Bottleneck: the slowest step. Its capacity sets the capacity of the whole process.
  • Utilization = actual output ÷ design capacity. Efficiency = actual output ÷ effective capacity (design capacity minus allowances).

1. Bottleneck lab: a smart doorbell line

A fictional plant assembles smart doorbells in four steps, one worker per step, in a 480-minute shift. Change any step's minutes, or add a second worker to one step (which doubles that step's capacity), and see what happens to daily output.

3. Utilization and efficiency calculator

Design capacity for a week = units per hour × hours per shift × shifts per day × days. Start with the textbook example (230 units per hour, three 8-hour shifts, 7 days, effective capacity 28,000, actual output 25,000), then change the numbers.

Takeaway: A process can only go as fast as its slowest step. Find the bottleneck before you spend money, and track utilization and efficiency to see whether a system is underused or underperforming.
