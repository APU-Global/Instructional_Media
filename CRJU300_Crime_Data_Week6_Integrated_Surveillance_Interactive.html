Week 6 Interactive: Integrated Surveillance Data

Week 6 objective: Evaluate what integrated surveillance and intelligence data can reasonably support, and what conclusions need more evidence.

The key idea

An automated license plate reader (ALPR) captures images of passing vehicles, reads the plate, and can record vehicle type and color, GPS location, date, and time. It compares plates against hot lists of vehicles of interest and can alert officers in real time. What it observes is a vehicle at a camera, not who was driving. Every source in an integrated system observes one narrow thing; treating it as more is how a strong-looking story gets ahead of the evidence.

Case file: Operation Harbor Light (fictional)

Fairmont's real-time unit is reviewing a late-night incident near the waterfront. Read the five data points.

Step 1: What did each source actually observe?

Match each data point to what it really observed. Each answer is used once.

Step 2: Reasonable now, or too soon?

Sort each statement. Reasonable now means the data support it today; Too soon means it needs more evidence first.

Takeaway: Integrated systems are powerful because they connect many sources, but each source still observes only one narrow thing. Not all data is complete or perfectly accurate. Name what each source observed, act on what it supports, and verify the rest. Courts generally treat reading a plate in plain view as no Fourth Amendment search, but they have warned that sustained tracking, or ALPRs combined with other surveillance tools, could become one.
