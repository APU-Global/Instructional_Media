About the course

Discussion of current and future global health challenges. Students will assess the determinants of health and disease from a global diversity perspective and will analyze global health threats including infectious diseases, poverty, conflicts, and healthcare inequity.

The Week 1 overview adds why this matters: ease of travel means diseases can spread quickly, and rapid disease transmission can become a matter of national security. Each week pairs chapters of Lecture Notes Global Health: Issues, Challenges, and Global Action (Armstrong-Mensah, 2017) with articles, reports, and videos. Projected total

Eight discussions, 30 points each

Each week’s discussion has a Review step (the assigned readings and videos), a Respond prompt, and a Discuss step with your colleagues. Respond by Thursday and discuss through Saturday (Week 8: respond by Wednesday, discuss through Friday). Posts and responses made after the academic week do not count toward your grade.

INITIAL POST
Substantive, by the first deadline
Answer every Respond prompt in your own words, supported by scholarly references.
PEER RESPONSES
At least two peers
Expand on their points with scholarly support, offer alternative perspectives, or reflect back their meaning to seek clarification or add context.
SUBSTANTIVE
Avoid simple posts of agreement; if you agree, explain why and further the conversation. Discussions are checked for plagiarism and AI-generated content.

Discussions are graded with the 3-criterion syllabus rubric: Assignment Requirements (10), Integrative Communication (10), and Written Communication (10), for 30 points.

Post readiness check

Before you submit a discussion post, check each statement that is true of your draft. Nothing is saved or sent anywhere.
