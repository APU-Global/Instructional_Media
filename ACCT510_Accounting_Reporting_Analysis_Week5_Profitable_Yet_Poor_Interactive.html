Week 5 Interactive: Reading the Statement of Cash Flows

Week 5 objective: Classify cash flows by activity, compute operating cash flow with the indirect method, and judge whether profit is turning into cash.

Three sections

Operating
The central activity of the business: customers, suppliers, employees.
Investing
Nonoperating flows involving an asset: buying or selling equipment, land, buildings.
Financing
Nonoperating flows involving a liability or equity: loans, stock, dividends.

Takeaway: Profit is not cash. Growth in receivables and inventory can drain a profitable company, and cash raised by issuing shares, borrowing, or selling assets cannot replace operating cash flow for long. Always ask where the cash came from and whether that source will repeat.
