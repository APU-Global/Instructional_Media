Week 4 Interactive: Coding Group Talk in the Transition Stage

Week 4 objective: Recognize dependency, fight, flight, counterpairing, and work in members' statements, and connect them to the stages of group development.

The Group Development Observation System

Wheelan, Davidson, and Tilin (2003) coded every statement in 26 work-group meetings into categories:

  • Dependency: conforming to the group's mood, following the leader, wanting direction from others.
  • Counterdependency: asserting independence from, or rejecting, leadership or authority.
  • Fight: argumentativeness, criticism, or aggression.
  • Flight: avoiding the task and confrontation.
  • Pairing: warmth, friendship, support, or intimacy.
  • Counterpairing: avoiding intimacy and connection; keeping the discussion distant and intellectual.
  • Work: purposeful, goal-directed, task-oriented effort.

Practice 1: Code the statement

Choose the category each statement fits best. Some are the researchers' own examples; others are fictional.

Practice 2: What did the research find?

Decide whether each statement matches Wheelan, Davidson, and Tilin (2003).

Takeaway: Transition-stage behavior is information. Naming what members do, whether fight, flight, or counterpairing, helps a leader respond to the need underneath instead of reacting to the surface.

Source: Wheelan, S. A., Davidson, B., & Tilin, F. (2003). Group development across time: Reality or illusion? Small Group Research, 34(2), 223-245 (course reading). Scenarios are fictional.
