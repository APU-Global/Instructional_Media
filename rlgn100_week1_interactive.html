Describe Before You Evaluate

Studying another tradition well means describing it the way its followers would recognize. Practice separating description from evaluation, recognize a fair account, and rehearse a respectful dialogue.

Part 1 · Descriptive or normative?

Descriptive study explains what a tradition teaches and does. Normative questions ask whether its claims are true. Both are legitimate, but they are different tasks, and good study begins with description. Classify each statement.

Part 2 · Would an insider recognize it?

Insiders know a tradition from lived experience; outsiders bring distance and comparison. Good study listens to insiders before evaluating, and stays aware of its own assumptions and biases. For each topic, choose the description a follower would recognize.

Part 3 · Practice a dialogue

Dialogue is a respectful exchange between people of different faiths. Listen carefully, represent others accurately, and be honest about real differences while keeping respect. This course is taught from a confessional Christian perspective; secure faith can welcome difference, so choose the reply you think fits that approach.

Know your lens

Name your assumptions. Before you study a tradition, write down what you already expect to find and where those expectations came from (family, media, past experience).
Confidence supports respect. Your own faith commitments can coexist with careful, respectful study of others.
