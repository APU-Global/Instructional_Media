Week 2 Interactive: Grading the Evidence for a Child Intervention

Week 2 focus: Judge how strong the evidence is for a childhood intervention, as Discussion 2 and the Annotated Bibliography Comparisons ask when you name a pro, a con, and whether a source is reliable and useful.

Part 1: Which level of support?

Clinical child psychologists grade treatments on five levels. Read each fictional evidence summary and choose its level.

Part 2: Check a study against the methods criteria

A school counselor finds a fictional study of “Calm Kids,” a 10-session group for anxious 8- to 11-year-olds. Decide whether each feature meets the methods criterion it relates to.

Part 3: What did the trauma review find?

Dorsey and colleagues graded treatment families for children and adolescents exposed to trauma. Choose the level each one received.

Takeaway: Strong evidence comes from randomized trials with a manual, a clearly defined population, reliable and valid measures, and enough participants, replicated by independent teams. “Experimental” means not yet tested enough, not “ineffective.” Use these questions to write the pros and cons in your annotations.

Levels and methods criteria from the JCCAP evidence-based treatment evaluation criteria as presented in Dorsey, S., et al. (2017). Evidence base update for psychosocial treatments for children and adolescents exposed to traumatic events. Journal of Clinical Child & Adolescent Psychology, 46(3), 303–330 (PMC5395332). Treatments A–F and “Calm Kids” are fictional.
