Week 4 Interactive: Situational and Path-Goal Leadership

Week 4 objective: Apply situational leadership and path-goal theory by matching a leadership style to each follower's needs.

Two ways to read the follower

Hersey and Blanchard: match style to maturity
  • Telling for M1: low competence, low commitment.
  • Selling for M2: low competence, high commitment.
  • Participating for M3: high competence, low commitment or confidence.
  • Delegating for M4: high competence, high commitment and confidence.
House's path-goal theory: clear the path
  • Directive: helps when roles are ambiguous.
  • Supportive: helps under stress or with boring, unpleasant work.
  • Participative: suits able employees facing decisions that matter to them.
  • Achievement-oriented: suits able, achievement-motivated employees.

Practice 1: Hersey and Blanchard

Read each fictional follower's situation. Choose the style that matches the follower's maturity level, then select Check my answers.

Practice 2: Path-goal theory

Choose the path-goal style the theory predicts will help each fictional employee most.

Takeaway: Both theories start with the follower. As a follower, tell your leader what you need: direction when a task is new, support when work is hard, a voice in decisions you understand. As a leader, diagnose before you act and be ready to change your style.
