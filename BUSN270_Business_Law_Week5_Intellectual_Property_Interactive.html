Week 5 Interactive: Intellectual Property

Week 5 objective: Distinguish patents, copyrights, trademarks, and trade secrets by what they protect and how long protection lasts.

The four protections at a glance

ProtectionWhat it protectsHow long
PatentA new, useful, nonobvious process, machine, manufacture, composition of matter, or improvement; also new ornamental designs (design patent). The inventor must fully disclose the invention.20 years; design patent 14 years
CopyrightThe expression of ideas fixed in a tangible form (books, music, film, art, sound recordings, computer programs), not the ideas themselves.Life of the author plus 70 years; works for hire 95 years from publication or 120 from creation, whichever is first
TrademarkA word, name, symbol, slogan, sound, or shape that identifies a seller's goods and distinguishes them from others'.10 years, renewable while the mark is still used
Trade secretA formula, process, device, or compilation of information (such as a customer list) that gives a business an advantage and is kept secret.As long as the secret is kept

Practice: classify each asset

Each business is fictional. Choose the protection that best fits, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: A patent trades public disclosure for a 20-year monopoly. A trade secret can last indefinitely but is lost once it is revealed. Copyright protects expression, not ideas. A trademark protects the name or symbol customers use to identify a source, and can be renewed for as long as it is used.

Rules, terms, and durations from Government Regulation and the Legal Environment of Business v1.0 (Saylor Academy, 2012), Chapter 13 "Intellectual Property" (13.1 Patents; 13.2 Trade Secrets; 13.3 Copyright; 13.4 Trademarks), CC BY-NC-SA 3.0. Scenarios are fictional.
