Week 1 Interactive: Choosing Your Capstone Segment

Week 1 focus: identify the healthcare segment that fits your interests and goals, and the access or outcome challenge it frames (CLO 1).

The five Capstone segments

From the course's Capstone Segment Options table:

Clinical and Operational Administration
Day-to-day management of hospitals, outpatient, ambulatory, and long-term care: patient care delivery, workflow coordination, operational compliance.
Finance and Revenue Cycle Management
Budgeting, billing, coding, collections, reimbursement strategies, and financial compliance.
Public Health and Community-Based Administration
Public agencies and community organizations: prevention, health equity, education, population-level outcomes.
Health Informatics and Digital Systems
EHRs, data analytics platforms, health information exchanges, and systems optimization for clinical decision-making.
Policy, Regulation, and Strategic Leadership
Healthcare policy, regulatory compliance and accreditation, and high-level strategic decision-making.

Practice 1: which segment frames this challenge?

Each challenge comes from a fictional organization. Choose the segment whose lens fits it best, then select Check my answers.

Practice 2: picture the setting

The course's Segments in Action table links each segment to representative entities. Choose the segment each setting represents.

Practice 3: who, what, or where?

The BSHA Career Paths guide suggests three questions for finding your focus. Choose the question each student's reflection answers.

Takeaway: Your segment is the lens for your whole Capstone. Ask who you want to serve, what work you want to do, and where you want to do it; then pick the segment whose lens fits the access or outcome problem you care about, and the two Choice-Based courses that strengthen it.

Concepts from Azusa Pacific University Global, Capstone Segment Options and Segments in Action (course documents, n.d.) and BSHA Career Paths (n.d.). All organizations named in the practice items are fictional.
