About the course

Examination of the activities of government and the consequences of those actions. Through the application of analytical techniques, students gain an understanding of the nature of public policy and various ways in which it may be approached to include addressing complex, real-world policy matters involving multiple constituent groups with diverse interests, institutional complexity, and ethical controversy.

Why do governments do what they do? Throughout the course you analyze, create, and plan the implementation of public policy, and you address ethical controversy through a Christian worldview. Select a card to see each course outcome in detail.

Each of the eight discussions is worth 50 points (Week 8: respond by Wednesday, discuss through Friday). Each prompt asks you to review the week’s resources and then discuss several questions.

INITIAL POST
Answer every prompt
Address all of the week’s Discuss questions, drawing on the assigned reading and resources.
PEER INTERACTION
At least two peers
Interact with at least two classmates with enough detail to show thoughtful reflection and insight from the learning materials.
WRITING
Clear, scholarly writing
Use vocabulary suited to the discipline, check grammar and spelling, and apply APA formatting.

Discussions are graded with the 3-criterion syllabus rubric: Assignment Requirements (20), Integrative Communication (20), and Written Communication (10), for 50 points.
