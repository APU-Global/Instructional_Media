1. Build a curve from expectations

In the expectations-plus-premium view, an n-year yield = the average of the expected 1-year rates over the n years + a term premium that grows with maturity. 3. The system behind the curve

Takeaway: A yield curve is the market's forecast of short-term rates plus a premium for tying money up longer. Normal curves slope upward; a flat or inverted curve points to falling rates and is consistent with recession. In 2008 the bigger problem was trust: credit markets froze and investors fled to Treasuries. As you write Discussion 1, use current Treasury or Federal Reserve data for today's curve.
