Elasticity and Revenue

Elasticity tells us how strongly buyers respond when price changes. Change a price, measure the response with the midpoint method, and see why elasticity predicts what happens to total revenue.

Part 1 · Raise or cut the price?

You currently charge $10 and sell 50 units, so revenue is $500. Pick a product type, then move your new price. Price elasticity is the percentage change in quantity demanded divided by the percentage change in price, calculated with the midpoint method and reported as an absolute value.

Part 2 · Is demand elastic or inelastic?

Availability of substitutes, necessity, share of income, and time horizon all affect how responsive buyers are, and narrowly defined goods tend to be more elastic. Classify each situation.

Part 3 · Advise the manager

The rule. If demand is elastic, raising price lowers total revenue. If demand is inelastic, raising price raises it. At unit elasticity, revenue is at its maximum.
