Week 2 Interactive: Comparing Prevalence Rates

Week objective: Calculate and interpret crude and age-adjusted prevalence before comparing two populations.

Crude rates describe; adjusted rates compare

  • Crude prevalence counts every case in the whole population. It shows the real local burden but carries the population's makeup with it.
  • Age adjustment applies each population's age-specific rates to one standard age distribution, removing differences that come only from age.
  • Read the indicator: data source, year, crude or age-adjusted, and the confidence limits.

Practice 2: interpret the comparison

Use your results and the course dataset.

Takeaway: A higher crude rate does not prove higher risk. Ask who lives in each population, adjust or stratify (by age, sex, or other factors), and check each estimate's confidence limits before you design a program around the difference.

Methods from National Center for Health Statistics, Age Adjustment (Health, United States, 2026); CDC Chronic Disease Indicators (2026); CDC About Underage Drinking (2025); state values from the course U.S. Chronic Disease Dataset (YRBSS 2015). City data are fictional.
