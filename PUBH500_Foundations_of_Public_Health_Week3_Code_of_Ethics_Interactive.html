A code for agencies, not only individuals

In medicine most ethical issues arise between a provider and a patient; in public health they arise between an agency and the population it serves. The American Public Health Association adopted a 12-principle Public Health Code of Ethics in 2002. Nine principles state obligations of public health institutions. Practice 2: the Guatemala experiments

From 1946 to 1948, U.S. Public Health Service investigators intentionally infected at least 1,308 people in Guatemala without informed consent. Choose the best answer.

Takeaway: Ethics in public health asks what an agency owes the people it serves: respect for individual rights, community input and consent, honest information, protection of the vulnerable, and confidentiality. In Guatemala every one of these was ignored, which is why consent is the line research must never cross.
