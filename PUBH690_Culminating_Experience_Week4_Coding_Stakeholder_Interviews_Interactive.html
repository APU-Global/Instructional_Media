Week 4 Interactive: Analyzing Qualitative Data

Week 4 objective: Apply CDC's four steps for analyzing qualitative data and code interview excerpts into themes.

Four steps from CDC Evaluation Brief 19

  • Review: read and re-read the notes or transcripts and write down first impressions.
  • Organize: group the data so it is easier to navigate, for example by date, by data collection type, or by question asked.
  • Code: identify and label themes, the common trends or ideas that appear repeatedly.
  • Interpret: list key themes, compare responses from participants with differing characteristics, and consider how themes connect.

Practice 1: Which step is this?

A fictional MPH student, Dana, is analyzing four stakeholder interviews about a free blood pressure screening program that few residents of a small town use. Match each action to its step.

Practice 2: Code the excerpts

Assign each fictional excerpt from Dana's interviews to the best theme.

Takeaway: Follow the steps in order: review, organize, code, and interpret. Remember the brief's cautions: interpretation introduces bias, findings are subjective, and themes from a few interviews will not likely generalize. In Part 2, set each theme beside the literature before you build your recommendation on it.

Concepts from Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (2018), Analyzing Qualitative Data for Evaluation (Evaluation Brief No. 19). Interviews and quotations are fictional.
