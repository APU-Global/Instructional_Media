Week 2 Interactive: Order of Operations, One Step at a Time

Week 2 objective: Use the order of operations agreement to simplify expressions, and explain why a common shortcut gives the wrong answer.

The agreement

  1. Work inside the innermost parentheses or other grouping symbols first.
  2. Evaluate exponents.
  3. Multiply and divide from left to right.
  4. Add and subtract from left to right.

Multiplication and division share a rank, and so do addition and subtraction: neither of the pair goes first automatically.

Step through it

Choose the correct next line at each step. A wrong choice explains the mistake so you can try again.

Which student is right?

Two students simplified each expression. Choose the student whose work follows the agreement.

Takeaway: The order of operations is an agreement that gives every expression one value. Watch the two places students slip most: working multiplication before division (or addition before subtraction) instead of left to right, and confusing −3² (which is −9) with (−3)² (which is 9).
