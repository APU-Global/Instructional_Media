Week 6 objective: Find the efficient level of emissions, see why uniform cuts cost more than needed, and match pollution-control tools to their descriptions.

Part 2: Equal cuts, unequal costs

Two fictional plants each emit 400 tons a month. The agency orders each to cut to 200 tons. For the older North Plant, cutting its last required ton costs $900. The newer South Plant could cut one more ton for $200.


Part 3: Name the tool

Governments use several tools to control pollution. Each example is fictional. Which tool is it?

Takeaway: The goal of environmental policy is not zero pollution but the level where its marginal benefit equals its marginal cost, reached at the lowest cost. Emissions taxes and tradable permits do that and keep rewarding cleaner technology.
