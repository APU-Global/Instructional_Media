Week 5 Interactive: Improvement Plans

Week 5 objective: Weigh the costs of conformance and nonconformance and follow the DMAIC steps of a Six Sigma improvement project.

Four costs of quality

PREVENTION
Money spent to keep defects from happening: improvement initiatives, training, better equipment, quality procedures, proactive design changes.
APPRAISAL
Money spent checking and testing during production: inspectors' wages, testing labs and equipment, gauging, process control.
INTERNAL FAILURE
Defects caught before the product leaves: rework and scrap, plus re-made or expedited orders to stay on time.
EXTERNAL FAILURE
Defects that reach the customer: replacements, expedited shipping, lawsuits, recalls, and lost future business.

Prevention and appraisal are costs of conformance; internal and external failure are costs of nonconformance.

Practice 1: Classify the cost

Each cost comes from Lakeshore Controls, a fictional maker of thermostat parts. Choose its category.

Practice 2: Which DMAIC step?

Lakeshore launches a Six Sigma project on cracked thermostat housings. Match each activity to its DMAIC step.

Takeaway: Spending on prevention and appraisal is the price of conforming; internal and external failure are the price of not conforming, and external failures such as recalls and lost customers are the hardest to predict. A disciplined DMAIC project finds the root cause and then controls the fix so the savings last.
