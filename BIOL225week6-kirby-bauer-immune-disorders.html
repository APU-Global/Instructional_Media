Standardized test conditions (CLSI M02 disk diffusion)
  • Medium: Mueller-Hinton agar poured about 4 mm deep in a 150 mm plate.
  • Inoculum: a suspension adjusted to a 0.5 McFarland turbidity standard (about 1-2 x 108 CFU/mL), swabbed in three directions to make an even lawn.
  • Disks: applied within 15 minutes of inoculation, pressed flat, and spaced at least 24 mm apart (center to center).
  • Incubation: 35 ± 2 °C in ambient air for 16-18 h (EUCAST specifies 18 ± 2 h at 35 ± 1 °C).
  • Reading: measure each zone diameter, including the 6 mm disk, to the nearest millimeter and compare it with the breakpoints for that drug and organism group.
  • Quality control: a reference strain such as Escherichia coli ATCC 25922 confirms that the medium, disks and technique perform correctly.

A larger zone does not automatically mean a better treatment option. Zone size depends on how well each drug diffuses through agar, so zones of different drugs cannot be compared directly. Each zone is compared only with the breakpoints for that drug and organism. Laboratory susceptibility (S, I, R) predicts, but does not guarantee, clinical success: the infection site, dose, drug safety and patient factors also matter. Minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) testing gives a more quantitative measure.

Key terms

  • Zone of inhibition: the clear area around an antibiotic disk where bacterial growth is prevented, measured in millimeters.
  • Minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC): the lowest concentration of an antimicrobial that prevents visible growth of an organism under standardized conditions.
  • Clinical breakpoint: a standardized zone diameter or MIC value used to place an organism in a susceptibility category for a specific drug and organism group.
  • Susceptible (S): the drug is likely to be effective at the standard dose for the infection site.
  • Intermediate (I): the result is uncertain; the drug may work at higher doses or where it concentrates in the body, but response may be less reliable. EUCAST calls this category susceptible, increased exposure.
  • Susceptible, dose-dependent (SDD): a CLSI category used for some drug and organism pairs, in which success depends on the dosing regimen.
  • Resistant (R): the drug is unlikely to be effective at usual doses, so treatment failure is more likely.

Immune Disorders: what went wrong?

Immunodeficiency = immune function that is absent, reduced or impaired. Autoimmunity = loss of tolerance, so the system targets self. Allergy = a hypersensitivity response (usually IgE-mediated) to a harmless antigen. Transplant reactions = rejection (the host attacks the graft) or graft-versus-host disease (the graft attacks the host).

Simplified educational model: real diseases can overlap these categories. For example, some inborn errors of immunity cause both frequent infections and autoimmunity. See Baloh and Chong (2024) and Wang et al. (2024) in the Deep Dive for current clinical detail.
