Week 5 Interactive: Financial Impacts on Public Sector Bargaining

Week 5 objective: Judge whether a public-private pay comparison is complete, and estimate whether new tax revenue covers the cost of a raise.

Three funding reminders

Limited revenueSales tax, property tax, fines and forfeitures, and fees fund most local contracts.
Total compensationCompare salary, pension, and Social Security coverage together.
Long-term costsUnfunded pension liabilities total at least $1.6 trillion nationally.

Practice 1: fair comparison or incomplete?

A fictional council member wants to know whether the city’s public works crews are paid more than the community average. Is each comparison fair or incomplete?

Practice 2: can the revenue cover the raise?

Marlow Springs, a fictional city, is negotiating with its public works union. Change the numbers to see whether a sales tax increase would cover the raise. Annual raise cost = payroll × raise; new revenue = taxable sales × tax increase.

Try it: a quarter-point increase on $1.2 billion of taxable sales raises $3 million a year, the figure in Gary Clifford’s Week 3 podcast. Now set the tax increase to 0.05. Remember that residents shop in the city too, and that pension costs keep growing after the raise is signed.

Takeaway: Count the cost before you commit, as Luke 14:28 says: know which taxes fund the contract, whether the base can grow, and what pension costs will follow the raise.
