Week 5 Interactive: Leading Technology Change

Week 5 objective: Discuss challenges associated with technology integration from a leadership perspective (CLO 4).

Four ways to go live

Direct cutover
Everyone switches on one date. Fastest and least expensive, but the riskiest.
Pilot
A small group uses the new system first, so support can focus on fewer people.
Parallel
Old and new systems run together for a time. Least risky, but the most expensive.
Phased
New functions turn on as old ones are turned off, moving gradually to the new system.

Practice 1: which go-live approach fits?

Each organization is fictional. Choose the approach that best matches the leaders’ situation.

Five factors that help adoption

Advantage
People see the change as better than the old way.
Compatibility
It fits users’ needs, values, and culture.
Simplicity
It is user-friendly and easy to understand.
Testability
Users can try it before committing.
Observability
It produces results people can see and measure.

Practice 2: which adoption factor is the leader using?

Choose the factor each action strengthens.

Psychological safety

Psychological safety means staff can raise concerns, ask questions, offer ideas, or admit mistakes without fear of punishment or humiliation. It begins with leaders, for example through open meetings, formal and informal feedback, and anonymous feedback.

Practice 3: does it build psychological safety?

Decide whether each leader action builds or undermines psychological safety during a new system rollout.

Takeaway: Resistance to a new system is a normal response to loss. Leaders help people adopt change by choosing a go-live approach that fits the risk, making the new system clearly better, compatible, simple, testable, and observable, and creating a team where people can speak up without fear.
