Week 5 Interactive: Morphology

Week 5 objective: Identify morphemes, roots, and affixes, and tell derivational from inflectional morphology.

Key terms

Morpheme: the smallest pairing of form and meaning or grammatical functionRoot / base: what affixes attach to; a root is the smallest baseDerivational: builds a new word, often a new category (-ness, -able)Inflectional: grammatical information, same category (-s, -ed)

Practice 1: Split the word into morphemes

Choose the analysis that finds every morpheme, the smallest pairing of form with meaning or grammatical function.

Practice 2: Derivational or inflectional?

Look at the underlined affix. Derivational affixes build new words and often change the category; inflectional affixes add grammatical information and never change the category.

Practice 3: Free or bound?

A free morpheme can stand alone as a word. A bound morpheme must attach to something.

Practice 4: Which order did the word use?

Derivational affixes are choosy about their base, so the order of attachment matters.

Takeaway: Words have structure. When students know that tri- means three or that -able means "able to be," they can work out words they have never seen. Teach the pieces, not just the words.
