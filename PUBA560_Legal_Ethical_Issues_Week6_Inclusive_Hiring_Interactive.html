Week 6 Interactive: Recruiting and Retaining a Diverse Workforce

Week 6 objective: Apply equal-opportunity tools to public hiring and recognize how workplace culture can include or exclude people that a policy protects.

Part 1: The four-fifths rule

A selection rate for any group that is less than four-fifths (80%) of the rate for the group with the highest rate could be regarded as adverse impact: a practice that looks neutral but has a discriminatory effect on a protected group. Work out each fictional case. Enter percentages as whole numbers (for example, 25 for 25%).

Part 2: Inclusion risk or inclusive practice?

The Lakeview County Parks Department (fictional) has a written anti-discrimination policy. Decide whether each practice creates an inclusion risk or is an inclusive practice, then select Check Part 2.

Part 3: Build the diversity plan in order

Select the three steps in the order they should happen.

    Takeaway: Compliance sets the floor: EEOC laws, reasonable accommodation, and checks like the four-fifths rule. Inclusion asks how people actually experience the workplace. A written policy matters, but what counts is how the organization acts on it, from where networking happens to how jobs are advertised.
