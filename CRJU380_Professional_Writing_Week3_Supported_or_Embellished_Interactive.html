Week 3 Interactive: From Notes to Testimony

Week 3 objective: Check an AI-assisted report draft against the record, separating supported facts from embellishment and from sentences that hide who acted.

The record

An officer typed these notes, then asked an AI tool to "write the narrative so it sounds professional and compelling." Everything in the report must trace back to this record.

FIELD NOTES - Ofc. T. Grant #412 - Fictional incident
1942 hrs  Dispatched: shoplifting, Corner Mart, 18 Elm St.
1949 hrs  Arrived. Clerk Dana Ortiz pointed to male seated on bench outside.
          Ortiz: "He put two energy drinks in his jacket and walked out without paying."
1951 hrs  Spoke w/ male, ID'd by license as Marcus Hale, DOB 03/14/1998.
          Hale: "I forgot they were in my pocket."
          2 cans (energy drink) in Hale's jacket pocket. Ortiz: total $6.58.
1955 hrs  Reviewed store video w/ Ortiz: Hale places 2 cans in jacket, exits past register.
2003 hrs  I issued Hale a citation for petty theft. Cans returned to Ortiz.
          Hale released at scene.

Practice: audit the AI draft

Rate each sentence of the AI draft. Supported means every detail appears in the notes. Embellished means it adds motive, emotion, or detail no one recorded. Hides the actor means it is accurate but in passive voice, so it does not say who did it.

Takeaway: Fluent is not the same as true. Before an AI-assisted or human draft becomes a report, trace every sentence to the record: who saw or said it, whether it is a fact or a characterization, and who performed each action. Write to inform, not to impress.

Guidance from Reynolds, J. (2017), "Writing Clear, Effective Police Reports"; (2018), "Focus on Report Writing: Policies and Practices"; and (2019), "Focus on Report Writing: Old Habits Can Hold Officers Back," FBI Law Enforcement Bulletin. The incident, names, and notes are fictional.
