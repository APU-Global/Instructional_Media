Week 1 objective: Sort spending into the parts of GDP, calculate GDP from its components, and judge when GDP overstates or understates the gain in well-being.

The formula

Gross domestic product (GDP) is the value of all final goods and services produced within a country in a given year. From the demand side, GDP = C + I + G + (X − M): consumption, business investment (new buildings, equipment, housing, and inventories, not stocks or bonds), government purchases of goods and services, and net exports (exports minus imports). Intermediate goods, used goods, transfer payments, and non-market or underground activity are left out.

Takeaway: Add only final goods and services: GDP = C + I + G + (X − M). Investment is the most volatile part, so it often drives a downturn. GDP is a rough but useful gauge; it leaves out leisure, health, the environment, unpaid work, and how income is shared, so read it next to other indicators.
