Week 7 Interactive: Cultural Competence for Partnerships

Week 7 objective: Distinguish the main strategies used to build health workforce cultural competence, a foundation for community partnerships.

Three strategies

Jongen et al. (2018) found two main workforce strategies. Cultural competence training takes either a categorical approach (teaching the beliefs and behaviors of particular groups; it can lead to stereotyping, but can be appropriate when it covers local populations with local cultural experts) or a cross-cultural approach (teaching general skills for any cross-cultural situation, such as asking how a person understands a problem and negotiating shared decisions). Professional development uses other training and mentoring or supervision.

Practice: which approach is it?

Each program is fictional. Choose the approach, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: Both training approaches improved practitioner knowledge, attitudes, and skills in many studies, but evidence of better health outcomes is limited. The surest way to avoid generalizations is to learn directly from the people you serve.

Approaches and findings from Jongen, C., McCalman, J., & Bainbridge, R. (2018), "Health workforce cultural competency interventions: A systematic scoping review," BMC Health Services Research, 18, 232 (PMC5879833). Programs are fictional.
