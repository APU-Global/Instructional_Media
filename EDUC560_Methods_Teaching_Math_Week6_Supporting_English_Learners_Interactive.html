Week 6 Interactive: Supporting English Learners

Week 6 objective: Choose research-based strategies that support English learners in a math lesson, and name them precisely in your lesson plan.

Change the access, not the expectation

English learners do not learn differently than their peers, but they have particular needs. Every math teacher teaches the language of mathematics. Teachers can listen for students' mathematical ideas (including gestures, drawings, and home language) rather than focusing on correcting vocabulary, and build in productive language with sentence frames, wait time, routines such as QSSSA (Question, Signal, Stem, Share, Assess), and multiple modalities.

Practice: which support fits best?

Each student is fictional. Choose the support that best meets the need described, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: In your lesson plan, name each support precisely, say what you expect it to change, and write a next step in advance (reteach with a second representation, preview in the home language, or a small-group revisit based on formative evidence). Keep the mathematics rigorous for every student.

Strategies from Emily Kaplan, "6 Essential Strategies for Teaching English Language Learners," Edutopia (2019), and the California Department of Education, Mathematics Framework for California Public Schools (2024), Chapter 2 (listening for ideas, revoicing, the "36 Fences" sentence frame).
