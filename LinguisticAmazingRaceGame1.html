📚 Stack Details

Stack 1: Earth Science & Ecosystems

Content-Area Focus: Earth Science, Biomes, and Natural Systems

The Vocabulary Stack:
  • Photosynthesis (photosynthesis) — Closed, open, and closed syllable combinations with Greek roots (photo + synthesis).
  • Biodiversity (biodiversity) — Open and closed syllables featuring multiple affixes.
  • Atmosphere (atmosphere) — Closed, closed, and vowel-consonant-e syllable chunks.
  • Precipitation (precipitation) — Latin-derived root with a multisyllabic nominal suffix (-tion).
Background Knowledge Schema Hook:
For "Biodiversity": Show a side-by-side picture of a barren parking lot versus a vibrant rainforest canopy. Ask candidates to count how many different types of plants and animals they spot to anchor the concept of biological variety before breaking down the word parts.
Stack 2: U.S. History & Government

Content-Area Focus: Civics, American Revolution, and Constitution

The Vocabulary Stack:
  1. Government (government) — Closed syllables with an embedded base word and nominal suffix.
  2. Independence (independence) — Complex prefix, closed base, and abstract suffix (-ence).
  3. Constitution (constitution) — Latin root structure with open and closed alternating syllable patterns.
  4. Amendment (amendment) — Open prefix followed by a closed base and suffix.
Background Knowledge Schema Hook:
For "Amendment": Display a torn sticky note with a rule crossed out and rewritten better. Ask teams: "Why do rules or games sometimes need a permanent change or patch as we grow?" to bridge into the historical concept of constitutional changes.
Stack 3: Westward Expansion & Geography

Content-Area Focus: American Expansion, Migration, and Topography

The Vocabulary Stack:
  • Expedition (expedition) — Multi-syllable Latinate structure with an assimilated prefix (ex-).
  • Territory (territory) — R-controlled closed syllables paired with an open final syllable.
  • Environment (environment) — Nasal blend structures and closed syllable frames.
  • Topography (topography) — Greek combining forms (topo + graphy) mapping physical land features.
Background Knowledge Schema Hook:
For "Expedition": Flash a quick image of a backpacker looking at a physical compass and a blank paper map. Ask candidates to describe the feeling of stepping into entirely uncharted territory before decoding the word syllables.
Stack 4: Industrial Revolution & Technology

Content-Area Focus: Innovation, Economics, and Mechanization

The Vocabulary Stack:
  1. Industrialization (industrialization) — A massive multisyllabic word combining multiple derivational suffixes.
  2. Mechanization (mechanization) — Greek-derived root (mech) paired with modern process suffixes.
  3. Urbanization (urbanization) — Latin root (urbs, city) tracing the shift of populations from farms to cities.
  4. Transportation (transportation) — Latin prefix (trans-, across) combined with root and suffix units.
Background Knowledge Schema Hook:
For "Urbanization": Show an old black-and-white photograph of a quiet horse-and-buggy dirt road transitioning into a bustling modern city skyline with streetcars, prompting teams to discuss how a landscape fills up with people and factories.

Early Writing & Peer-Editing

📚 Pit Stop 4 Resources: Writing Samples

These three authentic early elementary writing samples provide diverse student profiles and error patterns for candidate teams to evaluate during Pit Stop 4. Each sample balances developmental spelling and syntax variations with rich student voice, offering ideal practice for applying gracious peer-editing protocols and critical literacy frameworks.

Example: George Washington Narrative

Authentic Early Writing Sample:

"When George Washington crosses the river, he was very brave because the water was cold and dark."

Gracious Peer-Editing Protocol & Critical Literacy Framework:

Warm Glow: Praise the student's historical imagination and strong narrative voice in capturing Washington's courage.

Constructive Grow: Gently look at verb tense consistency (crosses vs. was).

Faith Integration / Critical Literacy Reflection: Instead of marking the paper with red ink, our feedback honors the student's voice (Imago Dei) by asking guiding questions that empower them to refine their historical narrative while celebrating their perspective as active historians.

Sample A: The Emerging Historian (Late First / Early Second Grade)

Writing Excerpt:

"Harriet Tubman was very bratt. She help pepol escape on the undrground rord."

(Harriet Tubman was very brave. She helped people escape on the underground railroad.)

Diagnostic & Pedagogical Profile:

Orthographic & Mechanical Features: Demonstrates strong initial and final consonant salience, but struggles with short vowel confusion (bratt for brave via early vowel-name miscue or spelling sound strategy), omitted silent markers, and compound word boundary spacing (undrground rord).

Critical Literacy & Voice: Exhibits powerful historical empathy and deep admiration for courage, making it an ideal candidate for a "Warm Glow, Constructive Grow" feedback cycle that preserves the child's bold historical perspective while gently guiding phonological spelling patterns.

Sample B: The Multilingual Narrator (Second Grade / English Learner Profile)

Writing Excerpt:

"In the moxrn, the family go to the market for buy fresh fruit. It was very happy day."

(In the morning, the family went to the market to buy fresh fruit. It was a very happy day.)

Diagnostic & Pedagogical Profile:

Orthographic & Mechanical Features: Shows interference from home-language phonology or direct translation patterns in verb tense consistency (go instead of went, missing articles like a before very happy day), alongside inventive phonetic spelling for complex consonant blends (moxrn for morning).

Critical Literacy & Voice: Captures vibrant community life and cultural traditions of open-air markets. The feedback protocol must leverage translanguaging assets, honoring the student's rich descriptive imagery while supporting English syntax acquisition without punitive correction.

Sample C: The Dyslexic Storyteller (Third Grade / Phonological Processing Profile)

Writing Excerpt:

"The brave soljer protmected his friends when the storm hit the camp."

(The brave soldier protected his friends when the storm hit the camp.)

Diagnostic & Pedagogical Profile:

Orthographic & Mechanical Features: Displays classic phonological transposition and sequencing errors typical of dyslexia profiles (writing soljer for soldier and protmected for protected, transposing medial nasal/stop clusters). However, high-frequency sight words (when, the, camp) and base verb structures are accurately retrieved.

Critical Literacy & Voice: Demonstrates strong thematic comprehension of loyalty and protection during hardship. Feedback must use multisensory cueing strategies and focus heavily on affirming the student's narrative arc and moral clarity before addressing phonological sequencing traps.
