Week 2 Interactive: Informing Publics

Week 2 objective: Use the situational theory of publics to decide who needs awareness, who needs an invitation to act, and who carries your message.

Key ideas from the reading

  • Nonpublics do not face the problem; latent publics face it but do not recognize it; aware publics recognize it; active publics do something about it.
  • People act when involvement and problem recognition are high and constraint recognition (believing nothing can be done) is low.
  • Strategy by stakeholder: advocates get action-oriented messages; dormant supporters need awareness and understanding; adversarial stakeholders need conflict resolution; apathetic stakeholders need the issue made salient and an invitation to collaborate.
  • Priority (key) publics are those whose cooperation the goal requires; intervening publics, usually the media, pass messages on; influentials shape how others interpret the message.

Practice 1: What kind of public?

Ridgeview County's health coverage office announces that enrollment rules change next year. Classify each resident, then select Check my answers.

Practice 2: Which strategy fits?

Choose the communication strategy the reading recommends for each stakeholder group.

Practice 3: Who carries the message?

Classify each group by the role it plays in the county's communication strategy.

Takeaway: Do not send one message to everyone. Find out who is latent, aware, or active; give dormant supporters awareness, adversaries dialogue, and the apathetic a reason to care; and reach priority publics through the media and influentials they trust.
