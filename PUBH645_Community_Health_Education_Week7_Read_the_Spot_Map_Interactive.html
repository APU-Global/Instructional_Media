Week 7 Interactive: Spot Maps and Prevention

Week objective: Interpret a spot map of two waterborne diseases and recommend prevention strategies matched to each exposure route.

How to read a spot map

  • Plot each case where it occurred and label potential exposures.
  • Match the pattern to the route: Legionella spreads through inhaled mist; cholera through contaminated water and food.
  • Check the population: clusters can mirror where people live; mark dense and unpopulated areas.

The map

Fictional town of Riverbend. Red dots are cholera cases; blue dots are Legionnaires' disease cases.

Text version of the map
  • Old Mill Road neighborhood (private well): 9 cholera cases, 0 Legionnaires' cases.
  • Within a few blocks of the Harbor Office Building cooling tower: 11 Legionnaires' cases (4 of them in Riverside Apartments, a dense complex), 0 cholera cases.
  • Eastside (city water): 1 cholera case, 1 Legionnaires' case.
  • Central Park (no residents): no cases.

Practice: interpret the map

Answer each question from the map and this week's readings.

Takeaway: A spot map links cases to places: plot them accurately, label possible exposures, and remember that clusters can mirror where people live. Then match prevention to the route: water management for mist-producing systems, treated water for drinking supplies, and testing and case management for the people already exposed.
