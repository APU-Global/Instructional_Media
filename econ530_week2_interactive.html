Count Only the Costs That Change

A good decision compares what changes between the alternatives. Weigh added benefit against added cost, sort relevant costs from sunk ones, and see how a sunk cost can talk you into the wrong choice.

Part 1 · Think at the margin

Part 2 · Relevant or not?

Northwind Bakery is deciding whether to keep running its own oven line or buy loaves from a contract baker. Which of these costs differ between the two alternatives? Only relevant costs matter, and opportunity costs count even when no cash changes hands.

Part 3 · Make or buy?

Use only the relevant costs. Making costs $1.00 per loaf in ingredients and energy, plus $95,000 of avoidable costs per year ($75,000 for wages and the maintenance contract, and $20,000 of rent forgone on warehouse space that would be rented out if the line stopped). Buying costs $2.10 per loaf.

Ethics belong in the analysis. The numbers above leave out the two bakers whose jobs would end. Decisions about what to measure and report reflect values; a Christian worldview asks managers to count the people affected, not just the dollars, and to be transparent about how they weighed them.
