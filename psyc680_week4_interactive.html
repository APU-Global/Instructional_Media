Valid, Reliable, or Neither?

Reading research critically means asking whether a measure hits what it claims to measure, whether it does so consistently, and what could limit the conclusions. Explore both ideas on a dartboard, match the types, and spot limitations and bias.

Part 1 · The dartboard

The bullseye is what a study intends to measure. Each dart is one measurement. Validity asks whether the measure hits the target; reliability asks whether results are consistent. Move the sliders or try a preset.

How spread out the results are (low scatter = consistent)
How far the average is from the bullseye (low = accurate)

Part 3 · Limitation or bias?

Every study has limitations, and authors do not always state them all. Decide which concern each snapshot raises.

Ethics and diversity. Ethical research protects participants and respects diversity, so consider who is represented in a study’s sample. A Christian worldview emphasizes respect for the dignity of persons.
