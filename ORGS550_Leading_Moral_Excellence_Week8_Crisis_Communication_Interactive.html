Week 8 Interactive: Ethical Crisis Leadership

Week 8 objective: Apply the CDC's six Crisis and Emergency Risk Communication (CERC) principles and recognize what leaders do in each phase of a crisis.

Six principles

  • Be first: the first source of information often becomes the preferred source.
  • Be right: share what is known, what is not known, and what is being done to fill the gaps.
  • Be credible: honesty and truthfulness should not be compromised.
  • Express empathy: acknowledge suffering in words.
  • Promote action: meaningful things to do calm anxiety and restore some control.
  • Show respect: respectful communication matters most when people feel vulnerable.

Practice 1: Which principle is the leader applying?

A fire has damaged the main warehouse of Ashford Supply, a fictional distributor, and two employees were injured. Choose the CERC principle each statement from the CEO best shows.

Practice 2: Which phase of the crisis?

The CERC rhythm runs through four phases. Choose the phase where each action belongs.

Practice 3: Helpful habit or trap?

The CERC wallet card lists top tips for communicating in a crisis. Decide whether each statement follows the tips or falls into a trap.

Takeaway: Ethical crisis leadership uses the head (be first, be right, be credible) and the heart (express empathy, promote action, show respect) together, and treats the crisis as a cycle: prepare before it comes and learn after it passes.
