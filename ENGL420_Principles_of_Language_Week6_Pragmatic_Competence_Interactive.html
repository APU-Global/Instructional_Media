Week 6 Interactive: Pragmatic Competence

Week 6 objective: Explain how listeners infer meaning from context using the Cooperative Principle, implicature, and speech acts.

Grice's four maxims

Quality: say what you believe is trueQuantity: as informative as needed, no more, no lessRelation: be relevantManner: be clear, brief, and orderly

The Cooperative Principle describes what speakers expect of each other. What counts as cooperative or polite also varies from community to community.

Practice 1: Which maxim is at work?

Each conversation is fictional. Choose the maxim that explains the inference or that is being broken.

Practice 2: Entailment or implicature?

An entailment is true whenever the sentence is true, in any context. An implicature depends on context and can be cancelled.

Practice 3: What is the speaker doing?

The same words can perform different acts. Choose the illocutionary act: what the speaker does by saying it.

Takeaway: Listeners assume speakers are cooperating, so they read meaning into what is said and what is left out. Pragmatic competence means noticing those layers and fitting your words to each listener, and remembering that the norms differ across communities.
