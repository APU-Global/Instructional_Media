Week 3 Interactive: Elements of a Contract

Week 3 objective: Identify the elements a valid, enforceable contract needs and spot the one that is missing.

The five questions

A contract is a legally enforceable promise. Whether one was formed depends on whether (1) the parties reached an agreement (offer and acceptance, judged by an objective standard); (2) consideration was present (something bargained for in return); (3) the agreement is legal; (4) both parties had capacity; and (5) the agreement is in the proper form (in writing, where the Statute of Frauds requires it).

Practice: did a valid contract form?

Each business is fictional. Choose Valid contract or the element that is missing, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: Before you rely on a deal, run the checklist: agreement (a definite, communicated offer and an acceptance), consideration, legality, capacity, and the right form. Most contracts need no writing, but land, promises that cannot be performed within a year, and sales of goods for $500 or more do.
