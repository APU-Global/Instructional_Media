Week 6 Interactive: Screening Across the Lifespan

Week 6 objective: Identify when a screening tool, its informant, its norm group, or its procedure does not fit the person being screened.

Four fit checks

1. Built for whom? The PHQ-9 was validated with adults 18 or older in primary care and obstetrics-gynecology clinics; the PSC-17 is a pediatric checklist completed by a parent. 2. Who answers? Self-report or an informant. 3. Which norm group? Percentiles compare a person with a reference group: the GAD-7 report uses age-related peers, and the NFFPS-30 uses an Australian sample matched by age and gender. 4. Language and procedure: a person not fluent in the test's language cannot show his or her real standing, a word-for-word translation is not automatically culturally equivalent, and procedures should be as clear and brief as possible.

Part 1: Find the fit problems

Each fictional scenario has zero, one, or more problems. Select every fit check that fails, then check your answers.

Part 2: Same score, different meaning

Choose the best answer.

Takeaway: Before trusting a screening result, run the four checks: who the tool was built for, who answered, which norm group the report used, and whether language and procedure let the person show their real standing.
