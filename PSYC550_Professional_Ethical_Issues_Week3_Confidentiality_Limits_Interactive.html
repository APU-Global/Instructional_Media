Week 3 Interactive: Limits of Confidentiality

Week 3 objective: Recognize when confidentiality holds and when a duty to protect or a mandated report requires disclosure.

What you need to know

Why confidentiality matters. It builds the trust that lets clients share their most private concerns. It rests on autonomy (the client decides who has access to their information), beneficence, and nonmaleficence.
Duty to warn and protect. Tarasoff I (1974): a duty to warn potential victims when a patient presents a serious threat of violence. Tarasoff II (1976): a duty to take reasonable steps to protect them, such as warning the victim or contacting law enforcement. “The protective privilege ends where the public peril begins.”
Mandated reporting. Laws in many places require therapists to report suspected child abuse. How much discretion a professional keeps depends on the jurisdiction.
Not every hard disclosure is a limit. Most ethical codes allow disclosure for imminent risk of serious harm to self or others; outside such limits, the client’s confidentiality holds.

Takeaway: Confidentiality is the default and the foundation of trust, but it is not absolute. Know the duty to warn and protect, know your jurisdiction’s reporting law (and your telehealth client’s), tell clients the limits at the start, and consult and document whenever you breach.
