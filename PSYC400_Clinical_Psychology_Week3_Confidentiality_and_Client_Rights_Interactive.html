Week 3 Interactive: Applying Legal and Ethical Standards

Week 3 objective: Apply confidentiality, the duty to warn, and client rights to realistic situations.

Key standards from Module 15

  • Confidentiality (an ethical principle): information about a client is not shared without consent. Privileged communication is the legal version; exceptions include a client younger than 16 and a client who is a danger to self or others.
  • Duty to warn: under Tarasoff (1976), confidentiality ends when there is a danger to the public. Under Thompson v. County of Alameda (1980), there is no duty to warn when a threat is nonspecific.
  • Client rights: to treatment, to refuse treatment, to less restrictive treatment, and to live in the community.

Practice 1: Keep confidential, or does a limit apply?

Each client is a fictional adult unless stated. Decide whether the therapist keeps the information confidential or whether a limit on confidentiality applies.

Practice 2: Which client right is at stake?

Match each fictional situation to the right it involves.

Takeaway: Confidentiality is the default; danger to self or a specific threat to others changes it. Clients keep the right to choose: to seek, refuse, and receive the least restrictive care.

Source: Bridley, A., & Daffin, L. W., Jr. (2022). Fundamentals of psychological disorders (3rd ed.), 15.2 Patients' rights and 15.3 The therapist-client relationship. LibreTexts. All scenarios are fictional; real cases require supervision and local law.
