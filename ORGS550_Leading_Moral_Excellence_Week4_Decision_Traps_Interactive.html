Week 4 Interactive: Ethical Decision-Making and Influence

Week 4 objective: Identify common decision-making traps and name the influence tactics leaders use, so you can build them into your own hybrid model for moral excellence.

Before you decide

Set your criteria before you search for alternatives. Then watch for traps: anchoring (relying too heavily on one piece of information), framing (being swayed by how a problem is presented), escalation of commitment (staying on a failing course because of what is already invested), and overconfidence (overestimating your ability to predict events). Many people also satisfice: they accept the first option that meets minimum criteria. A quick ethics test asks: Is it fair? How will I feel about myself? Does it break rules or laws? How would it look on the news?

Practice 2: Which influence tactic?

Takeaway: A hybrid model for moral excellence needs both halves: decisions that set ethical criteria first and guard against anchoring, framing, escalation, and overconfidence, and influence that leans on honest reasons, shared values, and consultation rather than pressure.
