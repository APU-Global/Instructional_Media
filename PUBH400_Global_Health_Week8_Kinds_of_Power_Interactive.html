Week 8 Interactive: Eight Kinds of Power in Global Health

Week 8 objective: Describe the roles of key global health actors and how they influence global health decisions.

Moon's eight kinds of power

Moon (2019) argues that influence in global health takes many forms, and that actors often seen as weak can still shape outcomes.

PhysicalUsing or threatening force.
EconomicUsing money or goods.
StructuralUsing a position in society's structures, such as the state.
InstitutionalUsing rules and decision-making procedures.
MoralShaping what others believe is right or wrong.
DiscursiveShaping the language used to frame an issue.
ExpertShaping what counts as legitimate knowledge.
NetworkUsing personal relationships built on trust and history.

Practice: which kind of power?

Each scenario is fictional. Decide which kind of power the actor mainly uses. Choose an answer for each item, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: Power in global health is not only money or force. Rules, moral voice, framing, expertise, and relationships all shape decisions, so even actors with few resources, such as NGOs using moral, expert, and discursive power, can change outcomes.

Typology and examples from Moon, S. (2019), “Power in global governance: An expanded typology from global health,” Globalization and Health, 15(Suppl 1), 74 (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6881906). Every scenario is fictional.
