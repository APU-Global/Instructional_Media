Week 5 Interactive: Mapping Interest Group Influence

Week 5 objective: Analyze special interest groups the way a public manager must: who they represent, why people join, and how they try to influence policy.

Part 1: What type of group is it?

Organized interests in the fictional Valley County. Choose each group’s type.

Part 2: Direct or indirect lobbying?

Direct lobbying pressures officials; indirect lobbying works on public opinion. Classify each tactic.

Part 3: Why do members join?

Interest group entrepreneurs use three incentives. Choose the incentive each appeal uses.

Takeaway: Business, labor, government, and citizen groups all seek to shape public policy, and the groups that organize are heard most. Public managers meet lobbying in hearings, rulemaking, and the courts as well as in the press. Disclosure rules help the public see who is seeking influence, and fair leaders listen to every side without partiality.

Group types, incentives, the free-rider problem, direct and indirect lobbying, and campaign finance rules from Reti, S. (n.d.), Introduction to California Government and Politics, Chapter 7 "Interest Groups" (LibreTexts, CC BY). Valley County and its groups are fictional.
