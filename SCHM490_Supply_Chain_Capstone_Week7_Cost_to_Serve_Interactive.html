Step 2: Keep or drop a customer?

Lark Wholesale Bakery (fictional) supplies three café chains. Its report shows the Riverside account losing money, and a manager wants to drop it. Allocated fixed costs are shared company costs that will continue even if Riverside leaves; direct fixed costs (a delivery van and driver used only for Riverside) will end.

Step 3: Sort the cost-to-serve activities

Activity-based costing groups customer costs into four categories. Choose the category for each Lark activity, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: Customers judge a service at every contact point, so measure tangibles, reliability, convenience, responsiveness, time, courtesy, consistency, and assurance. When a customer looks unprofitable, separate the costs that would end from the allocated costs that stay: a "losing" account can still add profit. Activity-based cost-to-serve data shows where to improve, and the decision is not only financial.
