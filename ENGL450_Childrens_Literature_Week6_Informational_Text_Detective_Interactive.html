Week 6 Interactive: Informational Text Detective

Week 6 objective: Apply the Common Core Reading Standards for Informational Text to nonfiction: identify a text's structure (RI.4.5) and tell firsthand from secondhand accounts (RI.4.6).

Why informational text?

The Common Core State Standards follow the National Assessment of Educational Progress (NAEP) in balancing literature with informational text in grades K–5. NAEP's 2009 reading framework sets this mix of passages:

Grade 4
50% literary · 50% informational
Grade 8
45% literary · 55% informational
Grade 12
30% literary · 70% informational

The percentages describe all of a student's reading across the school day, not only English class.

Practice 1: What is the text structure? (RI.4.5)

Grade 4 students "describe the overall structure (e.g., chronology, comparison, cause/effect, problem/solution)" of a text. The passages were written for this activity.

Practice 2: Firsthand or secondhand? (RI.4.6)

Grade 4 students "compare and contrast a firsthand and secondhand account of the same event or topic." Is each account firsthand (from someone who was there) or secondhand (reported by someone who was not)?

Quick check

Two questions about the balance of literary and informational reading.

Takeaway: Nonfiction has its own architecture. When children can name a text's structure and its point of view, they read informational books, and the biographies you will meet this week, with sharper comprehension.

Source: California Department of Education (2013), California Common Core State Standards: English Language Arts & Literacy in History/Social Studies, Science, and Technical Subjects, Introduction and Reading Standards for Informational Text K–5. Passages are original to this activity.
