Week 3 Interactive: The Power of the Purse

Week 3 objective: Connect fictional government actions to the economic powers in Article I, Section 8, classify each power, and identify the constitutional check that limits it.

Part 1: Which clause applies?

Each action is fictional. Choose the Article I, Section 8 power that authorizes it.

Part 2: What kind of power?

Enumerated powers are listed; implied powers extend them; concurrent powers are shared with the states.

Part 3: What limits the purse?

Each fictional proposal runs into a constitutional control. Which one?

Takeaway: Article I, Section 8 makes Congress the nation's economic authority: taxing, borrowing, commerce, currency, and patents. The same Constitution limits that authority through uniformity rules, time limits on army funding, two chambers, the veto, and judicial review.

Clause text from the U.S. Constitution, Article I, Section 8 (as reproduced in Kantack, America: The User's Manual, 4th ed., Appendix B, LibreTexts). Enumerated, implied, and concurrent powers; separation of powers, checks and balances, and bicameralism from American Government and Politics in the Information Age, Sections 2.3 and 3.1 (LibreTexts). All scenarios are fictional.
