Week 2 Interactive: Eliciting Student Thinking

Week 2 objective: Recognize higher-cognitive-demand prompts and the questioning moves that elicit students’ ideas.

Two tools for eliciting student thinking

Webb’s Depth of Knowledge (DOK) describes four levels of cognitive demand. CA NGSS performance expectations ask students to work at Levels 3 and 4, so Levels 1 and 2 are a foundation, “never a final instructional goal.” The Ambitious Science Teaching primer on eliciting students’ ideas moves the conversation through three phases, in order: observations, hypotheses about “what might be going on,” and pressing for possible explanations. It also names moves that shut students out of the conversation.

Part 1: What level is this prompt?

Each prompt is from a fictional elementary classroom. Choose its DOK level, then select Check my answers.

Part 2: Which phase is this?

Decide which phase of the eliciting conversation each teacher move belongs to, or whether to avoid it.

Takeaway: Plan the question, then plan the conversation. Aim the main task at DOK 3 or 4, start with observations, invite hypotheses, and press for what students cannot see. Probe instead of confirming right answers, and let students turn and talk before sharing.
