Week 6 Interactive: The Hierarchy of Defense Mechanisms

Week 6 objective: Recognize common defense mechanisms in everyday behavior and place defenses on the hierarchy from high adaptive to action level.

Seven levels, most to least adaptive

Defense mechanisms are unconscious strategies used to cope with anxiety and protect a positive self-image. Following Vaillant, the Defense Mechanisms Rating Scales rank them in seven levels:

  1. High adaptive (affiliation, altruism, anticipation, humor, self-assertion, self-observation, sublimation, suppression)
  2. Obsessional (intellectualization, isolation of affect, undoing)
  3. Neurotic (displacement, dissociation, reaction formation, repression)
  4. Minor image-distorting (devaluation, idealization, omnipotence)
  5. Disavowal (denial, projection, rationalization, autistic fantasy)
  6. Major image-distorting (projective identification, splitting)
  7. Action (acting out, help-rejecting complaining, passive aggression)

Practice 1: Name that defense

Each person is fictional. Choose the Freudian defense mechanism the behavior shows.

Practice 2: Place it on the ladder

Choose the level of the hierarchy where each defense belongs.

Takeaway: Everyone uses defenses. Growth means noticing the lower-level ones you rely on and practicing the high adaptive ones, which in research go with less distress and depression.
