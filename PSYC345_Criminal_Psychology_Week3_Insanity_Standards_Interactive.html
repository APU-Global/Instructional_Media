Week 3 Interactive: Insanity Standards and GBMI

Week 3 objective: Distinguish the major insanity tests and the guilty but mentally ill (GBMI) verdict, and weigh whether GBMI delivers the treatment it promises.

Why this matters

In law, "insanity" describes a state of nonresponsibility, not the presence of psychosis, and mental disease alone does not excuse a crime. Each insanity test asks a different question about the defendant's mind at the time of the act. GBMI was added as a middle ground: guilty, but with treatment. Critics doubt that prisons can deliver that treatment.

Takeaway: Each insanity test asks a different question: knowing, control, causation, or both knowing and control. GBMI keeps the guilty label and promises treatment, but national survey data show that many incarcerated people in serious distress are not being treated. Ask whether a verdict's promise matches what the system delivers.
