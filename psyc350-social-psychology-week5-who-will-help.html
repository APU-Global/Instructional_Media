Who Will Help?

A person has collapsed. Do you help?

Most of us believe we would act. Yet decades of research show helping depends heavily on the situation, especially on who else is watching.

You will (1) explore how the number of bystanders changes helping, then (2) diagnose where five different people’s helping broke down using Latané and Darley’s five steps.

Latané and Darley’s five steps to helping

Breaking the freeze

Tilt each step toward helping

  • Notice: look up and look around in public places.
  • Interpret: ask “Are you okay?” rather than reading the crowd’s calm.
  • Take responsibility: point to one person: “You in the red jacket, call 911.”
  • Know how: learn CPR and where the nearest AED is.
  • Decide: remember that an unneeded call costs far less than a missed one.
Reflect: In the parable of the Good Samaritan (Luke 10:30–35), two travelers passed by. Which step might each have struggled with? What did the Samaritan do differently?
