Erikson’s Social-Emotional Lens

Erik Erikson’s theory of psychosocial development proposes that individuals pass through eight distinct stages from infancy to late adulthood. At each stage, a person faces a central psychosocial crisis between two opposing psychological states. Successfully navigating each crisis fosters a core psychological virtue.

How to use this interactive

Select any stage on the timeline below to explore the core crisis, the developed virtue, and a real-world example.

Stage 1

Trust vs. Mistrust

Infancy (0–18 months)
Core Virtue: Hope

The Crisis

Infants must learn whether their basic physical and emotional needs will be met consistently by primary caregivers.

Example

"An infant whose cries are reliably met with warm feeding and comfort learns to trust the world as a safe place, whereas neglect leads to chronic anxiety and insecurity."
