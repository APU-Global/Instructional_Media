Week 6 Interactive: Mergers and Monopolization

Week 6 objective: Classify mergers and distinguish Clayton Act Section 7 merger review from Sherman Act Section 2 monopolization.

Two laws, two tests

Clayton Act Section 7 bars acquisitions whose effect "may be substantially to lessen competition, or to tend to create a monopoly" in any line of commerce in any section of the country. The test is probable harm, not actual harm. Sherman Act Section 2 forbids monopolizing or attempting to monopolize. A monopoly is not unlawful in itself; what is unlawful is gaining or keeping monopoly power by illegitimate means.

Part 1: What kind of merger is it?

Horizontal = competitors. Vertical = supplier and customer. Conglomerate = businesses not directly related (pure, product-extension, or market-extension). All companies are fictional.

Part 2: Which rule applies?

Decide whether each situation raises a Clayton Act Section 7 problem, a Sherman Act Section 2 problem, or no violation.

Takeaway: Name the merger first: horizontal mergers are judged by market shares, concentration, and trends; vertical mergers by whether they foreclose competition in a substantial share of a market; conglomerate mergers by specific effects such as entrenching an oligopoly or eliminating a potential competitor. Section 7 asks whether competition may be lessened, which makes it easier to stop a merger than to prove monopolization under Section 2.
