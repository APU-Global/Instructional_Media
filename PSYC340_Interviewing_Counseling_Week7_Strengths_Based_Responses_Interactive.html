Week 7 Interactive: Strength-Based Counseling

Week 7 objective: Choose strength-based responses that validate distress first and build on the client’s own strengths, and recognize responses that are mistuned to the client, as Discussion 7 and Partner Roleplay #4 ask.

Quick guide

  • Strength-based methods acknowledge, validate, and foster a client’s strengths, capabilities, and readiness to change, while still addressing problems.
  • Leave space to discuss distress when it is needed.
  • Define strengths with the client, with cultural sensitivity.
  • Build on problem-solving strategies the client already uses.
  • Watch for the trap: “cheering up” a client in despair can leave them feeling invalidated and alone.

Score: 0

Takeaway: Strength-based counseling does not mean only positive talk. Validate distress first, then explore strengths with the client, build on strategies they already use, and keep both problems and resources in view.

Sources: Flückiger, Munder, Del Re, & Solomonov (2023), “Strength-based methods: A narrative review and comparative multilevel meta-analysis of positive interventions in clinical settings,” Psychotherapy Research, 33(7), 856–872 (PMC10440292). Clients are fictional and for practice only.
