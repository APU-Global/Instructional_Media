Week 2 Interactive: Choosing an Evaluation Design

Week 2 objective: Read research design notation and choose an evaluation design that fits the question, the context, and ethical limits, as Discussion 2 asks.

Design notation at a glance

Franco et al. write each group on one line, with time running left to right.

R / NR
Random assignment / nonrandom assignment into the group.
O
An observation (a pretest or posttest measure).
X
The treatment: the program being evaluated.
ONE LINE = ONE GROUP
Two lines mean two groups, such as a program group and a control or comparison group.

Part 1: Name the design

Each fictional evaluation is written in notation. Identify the design.

Part 2: Which design fits the question?

The CDC framework says no design is best in all circumstances. Choose the design that best fits each evaluation question.

Part 3: Spot the threat

Each fictional result could be misleading. Choose the most likely threat to the conclusion.

Takeaway: Start from the question. Causal questions need a credible comparison; implementation questions may not. Name the threats, respect ethical limits, and choose a design you can carry out well. "The plans of the diligent lead surely to abundance" (Proverbs 21:5, ESV).

Design notation, experimental and nonexperimental designs, and threats from Franco, J., et al. (n.d.). Introduction to political science research methods, Chapter 6.2 (LibreTexts). Experimental, quasi-experimental, and observational designs and design fit from Kidder, D. P., et al. (2024). CDC Program Evaluation Framework, 2024. MMWR Recommendations and Reports, 73(6). Scripture verified on esv.org. All places and programs are fictional.
