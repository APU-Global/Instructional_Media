Week 2 Interactive: Where Crime Numbers Come From

Week 2 objective: Explain how criminal justice data is collected and what each major data source captures and misses.

Two ways to count crime

POLICE-REPORTED DATA
FBI UCR Program, NIBRS, and BJS LEARCAT

Built from crimes that law enforcement agencies record and report. Detailed and local, but they count only what reaches police. The Bureau of Justice Statistics found that less than half of violent and property crimes were reported to police in 2022.

VICTIM SURVEY
National Crime Victimization Survey (NCVS)

Each year the U.S. Census Bureau interviews about 240,000 persons age 12 or older in about 150,000 households about nonfatal personal crimes (rape or sexual assault, robbery, aggravated and simple assault, personal larceny) and household property crimes (burglary/trespassing, motor vehicle theft, other theft), whether or not they were reported to police.

Takeaway: Before you trust a crime trend, ask who collected the data, how, and who is missing. Police-reported data miss crimes victims never report; the NCVS misses homicides, crimes against businesses, and people outside its household sample. Comparing sources gives a fuller picture than any one alone.
