Week 2 Interactive: Choosing a Theoretical Lens

Week 2 objective: Apply developmental theories to real-life adolescent experiences using each theory's own terms.

Four lenses from Chapter 2

Chapter 2 compares theories to pairs of glasses: each brings some details into focus and leaves others blurry. Look for the clue that points to one lens.

  • Erikson: identity versus role confusion; exploring beliefs, roles, and goals; fidelity when commitments feel true to oneself.
  • Piaget and Elkind: formal operational (abstract, hypothetical) thinking and adolescent egocentrism: the imaginary audience and the personal fable.
  • Bandura: observational learning, vicarious reinforcement (learning from others' rewards and punishments), and self-efficacy.
  • Bronfenbrenner: nested systems: microsystem, mesosystem (links between settings), exosystem (settings the teen never enters), macrosystem, chronosystem.

Practice: name the lens

Each adolescent is fictional. Choose the theory that best explains the behavior, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: Name the clue, then name the concept. Discussion 2 asks you to use the theory's own terms, such as role confusion, imaginary audience, vicarious reinforcement, or exosystem, and to say why that lens fits better than the others.
