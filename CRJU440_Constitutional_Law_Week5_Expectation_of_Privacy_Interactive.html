Practice 1: Search or not a search?

Each scenario is fictional. Decide whether the government conduct is a Fourth Amendment search.

Practice 2: Warrant needed?

Decide whether the search is allowed without a warrant or whether officers need one.

Takeaway: Ask two questions: did the government physically intrude to gather information, and did it invade a reasonable expectation of privacy? If either answer is yes, it is a search, and officers need a warrant unless an exception such as consent, exigency, or a search incident to arrest applies. Digital data, from phones to location records, generally needs a warrant.
