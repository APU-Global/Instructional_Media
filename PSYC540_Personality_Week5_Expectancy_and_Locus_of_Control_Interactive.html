Week 5 Interactive: Rotter's Social Learning Theory

Week 5 objective: Apply Rotter's constructs of expectancy, reinforcement value, the psychological situation, and locus of control to motivation.

Rotter's model in brief

  • Behavior potential: how likely a behavior is, compared with other options, given a possible reinforcement.
  • Expectancy: the person's own estimate of the probability that reinforcement will follow the behavior.
  • Reinforcement value: how much the person prefers that reinforcer.
  • Psychological situation: the cues in the setting that the person attends to.
  • Locus of control: a generalized expectancy. Internal: outcomes result from my own behavior. External: outcomes result from luck, fate, chance, or powerful others.

Rotter's formula treats behavior potential as a function of expectancy and reinforcement value: people tend to choose the option they realistically expect to bring the most favorable outcome.

Part 1: What is holding the behavior back?

Each person is fictional. Choose the Rotter variable that best explains why the behavior is unlikely, or choose the last option if the behavior is likely.

Part 2: Internal or external?

These statements are written for practice in the style of a forced-choice scale. Choose the locus of control each one expresses.

Takeaway: In Rotter's model, behavior depends on what people expect will follow and how much they value it, within the cues of the situation. To motivate someone, find the weak link: strengthen the perceived connection between effort and outcome, or connect the outcome to something the person truly values.
