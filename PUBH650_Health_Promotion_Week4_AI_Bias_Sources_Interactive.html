Week 4 Interactive: Sources of AI Bias

Week 4 objective: Analyze ethical considerations in AI-supported health promotion by identifying sources of bias.

Five external sources of AI bias

AI bias means a system was designed in a way that makes its decisions or use unfair. Beyond the algorithm itself, Dankwa-Mullan (2024) names five external sources:

Exclusion
Groups are left out of the data: missing data, underrepresentation, or tools built without considering barriers to access.
Environment
The social and physical context is not represented: income, education, living conditions, urban versus rural setting.
Empathy
Human experience that is hard to quantify is missed: patient preferences, values, and personal stories.
Expertise
Varying expertise among the people involved: inconsistent data labeling, models that do not generalize, uneven familiarity among users.
Evidence
How evidence is produced and used: research funding priorities, publication bias toward positive results, translation into guidelines.

Practice: diagnose the problem

A fictional city health department is testing FamFuel, an AI app that gives families meal and activity suggestions to prevent childhood obesity. Choose the main source of bias in each problem, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: Bias can enter an AI tool long before the algorithm runs. Recommended fixes include collecting data from diverse groups, explainable models, diverse expert teams, training for users, and regular equity audits that check whether every group benefits.

Definitions and strategies from Dankwa-Mullan, I. (2024), "Health Equity and Ethical Considerations in Using Artificial Intelligence in Public Health and Medicine," Preventing Chronic Disease, 21, 240245 (CDC). FamFuel and its city are fictional.
