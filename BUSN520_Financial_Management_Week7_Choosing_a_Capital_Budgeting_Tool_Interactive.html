Week 7 focus: Discussion 7 asks you to analyze the tools used to evaluate an investment and justify which one you would use. Test a project with net present value (NPV), internal rate of return (IRR), and payback, then match each tool to the question it answers.

Cash flows come at the end of each year. The required rate of return (hurdle rate) is usually based on the firm's cost of capital, and riskier projects get a higher rate.

3. Practice

A $30,000 machine saves $9,000 at the end of each year for 5 years, with no salvage value. The required rate is 8%.

Takeaway: NPV tells you how much value a project adds at your required rate; IRR gives its time-adjusted return to compare with the hurdle rate; payback tells you how fast the cash comes back but ignores the time value of money and everything after payback. Use cash flows, include inflation, weigh qualitative factors, and count the cost before you build (Luke 14:28).
