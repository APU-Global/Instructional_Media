Week 6 Interactive: Relevant vs. Irrelevant Data

Week 6 objective: Decide which data is relevant by starting from the goal and the question to be answered, and recognize data that is only nice to know.

Start from the goal

"A problem half defined is a problem half solved." Set the goal first, then choose KPIs that show progress toward it. Our eMarketing text warns that analyzing metrics that are not indicators of success "will detract from timely optimization," and that measuring raw hits is a fairly meaningless task. Its examples: a hospitality booking site that wants more bookings and lower marketing costs might track conversion rate, cost per visitor, and average order value; a news site that wants more readers and longer visits might track length of visit, average time on site, and percentage of returning visitors.

Practice 1: Pinecrest Cabins (online booking site)

Goal: increase bookings and decrease marketing expenses. For each item, choose whether it belongs on the boss's dashboard.

Practice 2: The Valley Ledger (community news site)

Goal: increase readership and the time visitors spend on the site.

Practice 3: Is this a good reason to collect data?

Research consultant Alex Caffarini lists poor reasons companies gather information. Decide whether each request is a good reason or a poor reason.

Takeaway: Relevance starts with the question. Define the problem, tie each metric to a goal, look at overall metrics before details, and leave out what is only nice to know. Then report it honestly, with a just weight.
