Week 5 Interactive: Relapse Warning Signs

Objective: Recognize relapse warning signs and match each to a level of warning-sign management.

Relapse is a process

SAMHSA’s relapse prevention manual describes relapse as a process that is usually preceded by observable warning signs. Clients build a personal list of three kinds of signs:

  • Irrational thoughts that justify or excuse use.
  • Unmanageable feelings such as rising anger, loneliness, or anxiety.
  • Self-defeating behaviors that pull a person back toward people, places, and habits linked to use.

Signs are then managed at three levels: the situational-behavioral level (avoid or change triggering situations), the cognitive-affective level (challenge irrational thoughts and deal with feelings), and the core-issue level (work on the deeper addictive and psychological issues that create the signs).

Takeaway: The drink or drug is the end of a relapse, not the beginning. Name the warning signs early, plan a response for each, and involve the people who know the client best.
