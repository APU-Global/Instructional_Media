Week 3 Interactive: When Worldviews Clash

Week 3 objective: Distinguish facts from values in a disagreement and recognize responses that show active listening.

Facts, values, and listening

Following Thomas Kuhn, Smith distinguishes facts, information about the world, from values, beliefs about the way the world is or ought to be. A disagreement about facts may yield to evidence; a disagreement about values calls for understanding. Open RN describes three kinds of listening: competitive listening focuses mostly on sharing our own point of view, passive listening assumes we understand without checking, and active listening shows interest and verifies understanding, for example by restating what the person said. Acceptance is not the same as agreement.

Practice 1: Fact or value?

Decide whether each statement is mainly a fact (information about the world) or a value (a belief about how things are or ought to be).

Practice 2: What kind of listening?

A fictional friend, Dana, says: “I don't think God has anything to do with my life.” Identify the kind of listening each response shows.

Takeaway: Ask whether a clash is about facts or values, and listen to understand before you answer. Gentleness and respect keep the conversation open.
