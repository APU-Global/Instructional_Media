Week 1 Interactive: The Social-Ecological Model

Week 1 objective: Use the social-ecological model and the language of social determinants to explain why an outbreak spreads in a community, not only in individuals.

The five levels in this course

From the course’s Social Ecological Model presentation:

IndividualKnowledge, attitudes, behavior, self-efficacy, age, literacy.
InterpersonalFamily, friends, peers, co-workers, religious networks, customs.
OrganizationalSchools, hospitals, government and other institutions with rules for how they operate.
CommunityRelationships among organizations and networks; community social norms.
Policy / enabling environmentLocal, state, and national laws; allocation of resources; missing policies.

Practice 1: which level?

Each statement describes the fictional town of Lake Arden during a whooping cough (pertussis) outbreak. Choose the level of the model where the factor mainly sits, then select Check my answers.

Practice 2: name the idea

Match each description to the term it defines.

Takeaway: An outbreak is never only an individual problem. Lake Arden’s cases trace back to waning immunity, household contact, school and camp rules, poor coordination among agencies, and missing resources. Each level points to a different action, and lasting change needs action at several levels at once.
