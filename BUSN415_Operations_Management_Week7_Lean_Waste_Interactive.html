Week 7 Interactive: Lean Waste and Flow

Week 7 objective: Identify forms of waste in a service process, match the five lean principles, and explain why flow exposes problems.

Why this matters

In lean thinking, waste is any activity that consumes resources but creates no value for the customer. Lean began in the Toyota Production System, and its ideas now reach offices and other nonmanufacturing processes as well as factories. Naming each waste is the first step toward removing it.

Practice 1: name the waste

Maple Street Clinic (fictional) wants to shorten patient visits. Choose the form of waste each observation shows.

Practice 2: match the lean principle

Choose the principle from Womack and Jones that each clinic action applies.

Practice 3: true or false?

Decide whether each statement is true or false according to this week's sources.

Takeaway: Start from what the customer values, walk the process, and label every non-value-adding step with its waste type. Smaller batches and less inventory make problems visible, and continuous improvement keeps removing them.
