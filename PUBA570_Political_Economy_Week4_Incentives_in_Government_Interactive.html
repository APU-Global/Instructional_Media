Week 4 Interactive: Two Lenses on a Policy Decision

Week 4 objective: Tell public interest reasoning from public choice reasoning, trace who gains and who pays in a policy decision, and name its trade-offs.

Part 1: Which lens is at work?

Each statement explains a public decision. Is it public interest reasoning or public choice reasoning?

Part 2: Who gains, who pays?

A fictional state protects two local producers with an import cap. Follow the incentives.

Part 3: Sort the consequences

Label each consequence of Ardent's import cap.

Takeaway: Ask two questions of any policy decision: what would be efficient, and what did the decision makers and organized groups gain? Concentrated benefits and dispersed costs explain many choices; a Christian worldview adds a third question: whose interests were left out?

Firms seeking protection from competition and the case for and against intervention from Sell, An Introduction to Politics, Sections 8.4 and 8.6 (LibreTexts). The State of Ardent and its figures are fictional.
