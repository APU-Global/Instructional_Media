Week 6 Interactive: Raise It or Let It Go?

Week 6 objective: Apply three questions from Difficult Conversations to decide when a conversation makes sense and when another response is wiser.

Three questions before you talk

1. Is the real conflict inside you?
Your own feelings and identity issues may be driving the problem more than the other person is.
2. Is there a better way than talking?
Sometimes a change in your own behavior, or another action, does more than words.
3. Do you have purposes that make sense?
Purposes that backfire: changing the other person, venting or telling them off, and hit-and-runs.

If you decide to let go

Select each liberating assumption to see what it means.

Takeaway: There is no single "right choice," only a clear-headed one. Ask whether the conflict is inside you, whether action beats talk, and whether your purpose makes sense. If you raise it, aim for mutual understanding and start with their story. If you let it go, these assumptions can help: do your best, accept their limits, don't let the conflict define you, and keep caring.

Questions, purposes, and assumptions from Stone, Patton, and Heen, Difficult Conversations: How to Discuss What Matters Most, Chapter 7 "What's Your Purpose? When to Raise It and When to Let Go," the Week 6 course reading. Scenarios are fictional.
