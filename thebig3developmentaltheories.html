The Big 3 Developmental Theories: A Side-by-Side Guide

Comprehensive comparative overview of Piaget, Erikson, and Kohlberg across developmental dimensions and key life stages.

1. Theoretical Overview & Domain Comparison

Side-by-side framework comparison across core domains

Theorist & Domain What it Measures Core Question Theoretical & Biological Driver
Piaget
(Cognitive)
 Thinking & Logic: How our understanding of concepts evolves. "How do I process the world?" Cognitive Maturation & Assimilation: Brain development paired with active environmental exploration.
Erikson
(Psychosocial)
 Identity & Relationships: How we resolve social conflicts. "Who am I, and where do I fit?" Epigenetic Principle & Aging: Biological maturity combined with growing social expectations.
Kohlberg
(Moral)
 Ethics & Judgment: How we evaluate right vs. wrong. "Why should I follow rules?" Cognitive Reasoning & Perspective-Taking: Maturation of logical abstract thinking.

2. How Each Theory Views the Same Life Stage

Vertical age progression mapping biological development alongside cognitive, psychosocial, and moral theories

0-6

Infancy & Early Childhood (Ages 0–6)

Biological Hardware: Rapid creation of brain connections; the prefrontal cortex (brake pedal) is completely undeveloped.
Piaget (Cognitive)
  • Sensorimotor (Ages 0–2): Explores the world through senses and motor actions; develops object permanence.
  • Preoperational (Ages 2–7): Uses symbolic thought and language, but exhibits egocentric thinking (struggles to see perspectives other than their own).
Erikson (Psychosocial)
  • Trust vs. Mistrust (Ages 0-1.5): Learns whether the world is safe based on caregiver responsiveness to biological needs.
  • Autonomy vs. Shame/Doubt (Ages 1.5 - 3): Builds independence, self-control, and physical autonomy.
  • Initiative vs. Guilt (Ages 3–6): Asserts power and leadership through direct play and social initiation.
Kohlberg (Moral)
  • Premoral / Early Pre-conventional Level (Ages 4–6): Moral reasoning begins to emerge; actions are judged purely by avoiding immediate physical punishment.
  • (Note: Infants 0–3 lack the cognitive development for moral reasoning).
7-11

Middle Childhood (Ages 7–11)

Biological Hardware: Gradual pruning of unused brain connections; prefrontal cortex strengthens, allowing for increased focus and impulse control.
Piaget (Cognitive)

Concrete Operational Stage (Ages 7–11): Thinks logically about concrete, hands-on events; masters conservation, classification, and operational logic, but struggles with purely abstract hypothetical ideas.

Erikson (Psychosocial)

Industry vs. Inferiority (Ages 6–12): Focuses on mastering academic, social, and physical skills; develops a sense of pride in accomplishments or feels inadequate if compared unfavorably to peers.

Kohlberg (Moral)

Pre-conventional Level (Ages 7–11): Determines right vs. wrong based on concrete consequences—avoiding punishment and seeking tangible rewards ("What's in it for me?").

12-18

Adolescence (Ages 12–18)

Biological Hardware: Peak emotional limbic system (gas pedal) paired with an unfinished prefrontal cortex (brake pedal for impulse control at 60%).
Piaget (Cognitive)

Formal Operational Stage (Ages 12+): Unlocks the ability to think abstractly, hypothesize, test, and debate concepts.

Erikson (Psychosocial)

Identity vs. Role Confusion (Ages 12-18): Tests different social personas and values to establish a stable personal identity..

Kohlberg (Moral)

Conventional Level (Ages 12-18+): Follows rules primarily to gain social approval (Stage 3) and maintain social order/laws (Stage 4).

19+

Adulthood (Ages 19+)

Biological Hardware: Prefrontal cortex reaches full structural maturity (around age 25).
Piaget (Cognitive)

Formal Operational Stage continues (some theorists add Post-Formal thought for adults).

Erikson (Psychosocial)
  • Intimacy vs. Isolation (Ages 19–40): Building deep, committed relationships.
  • Generativity vs. Stagnation (Ages 40-65): Shifts focus outward toward guiding and supporting the next generation.
  • Integrity vs. Despair (Ages 65+): Reflecting back on life with a sense of fulfillment or regret.
Kohlberg (Moral)
  • Conventional Level for most adults: Focusing on societal laws.
  • Post-conventional Level: Some adults will not get to this level of making moral judgments based on personal ethical principles, even if they conflict with written laws.
