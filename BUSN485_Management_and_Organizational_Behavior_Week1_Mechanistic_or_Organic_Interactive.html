Week 1 Interactive: Organizational Structure

Week 1 objective: Describe an organization's structure with the four building blocks and decide whether it is mechanistic or organic.

The four building blocks

Centralization: how far decision-making authority sits at higher levels. Formalization: how far policies, procedures, job descriptions, and rules are written down. Hierarchical levels: tall structures have many layers and small spans of control; flat structures have few layers and wide spans. Departmentalization: jobs grouped by function (functional) or by product, service, customer, or geography (divisional). Mechanistic structures are highly formalized and centralized; organic structures are flexible, decentralized, and low in formalization.

Practice 1: Which building block?

Each organization is fictional. Choose the building block each description is mainly about, then select Check my answers.

Practice 2: Mechanistic or organic?

Decide which configuration each fictional organization resembles.

Takeaway: Describe a structure by its four building blocks, then name the configuration. Mechanistic designs buy efficiency and suit stable environments; organic designs buy flexibility, satisfaction, and innovation. Many real organizations mix the two.
