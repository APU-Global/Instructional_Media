Surveillance, Forecasts, and Trust

AI can process very large amounts of health data to detect outbreaks and forecast their spread, but every data source has blind spots, forecasts are uncertain, and public trust depends on transparency and fairness.

Part 1 · Whose data are we seeing?

Public health surveillance is the ongoing collection and analysis of health data to detect outbreaks and guide action. It now draws on varied sources, and each has strengths and blind spots. Turn data sources on and off to see which people each one reaches. (Illustrative ratings for discussion.)

Part 2 · Forecasts are uncertain

Models can forecast the spread of disease, but forecasts are uncertain. Here a simple model follows an outbreak in a city of 100,000 people. R is the average number of people one infectious person infects early in an outbreak. Because R is only estimated, try different values and ranges.
