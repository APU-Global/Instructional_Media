Week 8 Interactive: Personality Stability and Change

Week 8 objective: Distinguish kinds of personality stability and the person-environment processes that maintain them.

Key terms

  • Heterotypic stability: the same underlying attribute expressed in different behavior at different ages.
  • Absolute stability: consistency in the level of a trait over time, for one person or for a group's average.
  • Differential stability: consistency in a person's rank order relative to peers, measured by test-retest correlations.
  • Attraction: people are drawn to settings that fit their personalities. Selection: gatekeepers choose people because of their personalities. Attrition: poor fits leave or are asked to leave. Manipulation: people reshape their settings to match their personalities.

Part 1: Which kind of stability?

Each person and study is fictional. Choose the kind of stability each example shows.

Part 2: Which process?

Choose the process that matches each fictional example.

Takeaway: Ask what kind of stability a claim is about: the same attribute in a new form, the level of a trait, or a person's rank order. Attraction, selection, attrition, and manipulation help keep personalities consistent, yet stability is never perfect, so change remains possible at every age.

Concepts from Donnellan, M. B. (n.d.), "Personality Stability and Change," in R. Biswas-Diener & E. Diener (Eds.), Noba Textbook Series: Psychology, DEF Publishers (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). Scenarios are fictional.
