Week 7 Interactive: Communicating Evidence

Week 7 objective: Present evidence so decision-makers can understand and use it.

Evidence that is not understood is not used

Managers name lack of time, limited understanding of research, and unreadable articles as barriers to using evidence (Barends et al., 2017). A scoping review of strategies for communicating scientific evidence to managers and the public found that some formats appear to help: natural frequencies instead of percentages, absolute instead of relative risk, numbers instead of vague words, and plain-language summaries (Riera et al., 2023).

Practice 1: which version communicates better?

You are presenting the results of a pilot at Northfield Clinics (fictional). For each pair, choose the version research suggests decision-makers understand better.

Practice 2: recompute the numbers

Northfield’s turnover fell from 20% to 15% of employees in one year. Choose the correct statement.

Takeaway: Lead with the key message, show absolute numbers and natural frequencies, state how strong the evidence is, and say what it does show. Use this checklist for your Part 5 after-action review.
