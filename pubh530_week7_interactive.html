Prevent Injuries, Engage Stakeholders

Many unintentional injuries are preventable, and strong recommendations are built with the people who will carry them out. Fill in a Haddon matrix to find points for action, map the stakeholders in a community plan, and practice presenting with clarity.

Part 1 · Find the points for action

The Haddon matrix considers the person, the agent, and the environment before, during, and after an event, and helps you identify points for action. Scenario: traffic crashes, a common cause of unintentional injury. Select a measure, then select the cell where it belongs.

Select a measure to begin.

Part 2 · Map the stakeholders

Stakeholders include those affected by an issue and those able to influence it. Map their interest and influence, and engage them early and honestly. Scenario: a town plans safety changes along a busy road where several crashes have occurred. Choose the best place for each stakeholder; they will appear on the map.

← Lower interestHigher interest →

Placements depend on the situation, and they change. A council member’s interest may rise sharply when residents speak up, which is one more reason to engage early.
