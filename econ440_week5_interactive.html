Exchange Rates and Currency Risk

An exchange rate is the price of one currency in terms of another, and its movements change the prices of imports and exports. See how, then test whether hedging a currency risk is worth its cost.

Part 1 · What a currency move does to prices

The exchange rate is quoted as dollars per euro. When it falls, the dollar buys more euros: the dollar has strengthened. Move the rate and watch both sides of trade.

Part 3 · Read the balance of payments

The balance of payments records a country’s transactions with the rest of the world, including the current account and the capital account. Trade deficits and surpluses are one part of the picture. Where does each item appear?

A central bank raises interest rates, making the country’s assets more attractive to foreign investors. What tends to happen to its currency?
Policies interact. Fiscal and monetary policy affect trade: interest rates influence exchange rates and capital flows, and domestic goals can conflict with trade goals.
