The three C's

The criminal justice system works at local, state, and federal levels through three main parts. Cops respond, investigate, arrest, and book suspects, but do not decide guilt or punishment. Courts decide what charges to file, handle pleas and trials, and sentence those found guilty. Corrections carries out the sentence, from fines and probation to jail, prison, and parole. Along the way, discretion, the power to make decisions within legal guidelines, filters cases out like a funnel.

    Takeaway: Most cases never travel the whole path. Police discretion, unreported crime, and plea bargains narrow the funnel at every stage. Know who decides what: police arrest, prosecutors charge, judges sentence, and corrections carries out the sentence. Good policy balances crime control and due process.
