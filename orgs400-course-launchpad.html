    WEEK 1

    Principles of Innovation

    Discussion 1
    Interactive · Smart Failure Sorter

    Leaders make failure cheap, fast, and informative. Is each scenario a smart failure or a careless one?

    Why test small, test early?

    Week 1 success tips

    • Read first. Keeley, Ten Types of Innovation, Chapters 1–2 define innovation and the ten types you will use all term.
    • Answer both halves. Discussion 1 asks how you define innovation and whether it matters. Use the Fast Company article for both.
    • Define it by results. Innovation creates new value for customers; it does not require a gadget.
    • Watch the talk. Eddie Obeng’s “Smart Failure” (12:29) fits this week’s big idea.
    WEEK 3

    Types of Innovation: External & Combining

    Innovation Proposal · Part 1
    Interactive · Ten Types Combo Wheel

    Select two or more types to build a combination. Select again to remove.

    Select a type to explore it

    Keeley groups the ten types into Configuration, Offering, and Experience. This week focuses on Experience and on combining types.

    Combo strength: none yet

    The more types an innovation touches, the harder it is for competitors to copy.

    Week 3 success tips

    • Read Chapters 9–14 (Experience types and combining) before posting.
    • Watch “How to Be a Disruptor” (2:03) before you share your project idea in Discussion 3.
    • Proposal Part 1: choose an organization and a specific innovation, then explain why it is needed and how it creates competitive advantage.
    • Pick a topic you can keep digging into. It carries through all four proposal parts.
    WEEK 5

    Innovation Tactics

    Innovation Proposal · Part 2
    Interactive · Declare Your Intent

    Week 5 success tips

    • Read Chapters 18–20 to declare your intent and choose tactics from the playbook.
    • Not everything is disruptive. In Discussion 5, decide whether core and adjacent innovation still count as innovation.
    • Use Porter’s Five Forces as a Part 2 checklist: new entrants, substitutes, buyer power, supplier power, rivalry.
    • Mine the failure stories. Look for patterns in the start-up failures to strengthen your risk analysis.
    WEEK 7

    Implementation & Change Management

    Discussion 7
    Interactive · Change Model Planner

    Week 7 success tips

    • Pick the model that fits your organization’s scale and culture, then tie an action item to every stage or step.
    • Watch Jason Clarke (18:04), then recall a time you or a teammate resisted a change for Discussion 7.
    • Treat resistance as feedback: listen first, explain why it matters and what stays the same, and celebrate early wins.
