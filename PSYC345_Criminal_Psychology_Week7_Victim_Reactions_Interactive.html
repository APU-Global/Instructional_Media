Week 7 Interactive: Psychological Effects on Victims

Week 7 objective: Describe the emotional, physical, and relational effects of violent victimization and compare how often victims experience them.

Why this matters

The Bureau of Justice Statistics found that 68% of victims of serious violent crime in 2009-12 experienced socio-emotional problems: moderate to severe distress, problems at work or school, or problems with family and friends. Knowing the most common reactions helps you describe victims' symptoms in this week's discussion and think about what recovery requires.

Practice 1: sort the reaction

Choose the kind of effect each reaction represents.

Practice 2: which is more common?

For each pair, choose the option that was more common in the BJS data (victims of violence, 2009-12).

Takeaway: Crime leaves wounds that are emotional, physical, and relational, and they often last a month or more. Many distressed victims never reach police or victim services. Recovery is possible, but it depends on support actually reaching the people who need it.

Facts from Langton, L., & Truman, J. (2014), Socio-emotional Impact of Violent Crime, Bureau of Justice Statistics. Percentages describe victims of nonfatal violent crime in the National Crime Victimization Survey, 2009-12.
