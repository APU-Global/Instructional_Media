Week 2 Interactive: Phonemic Awareness and Scaffolding

Week 2 objective: Recognize the phonemic awareness tasks that prepare students for decoding, and judge whether a lesson gives the right balance of challenge and support.

Two ideas to keep in mind

Phonemic awareness is the ability to hear, identify, and manipulate individual sounds (phonemes) in spoken words. It is auditory and does not involve print. Five common tasks are blending (combine sounds into a word), segmenting (break a word into its sounds), deletion (remove a sound), substitution (replace a sound), and isolation (identify one sound in a word).

Scaffolding is temporary support that does not lower the intellectual challenge of a task. The ELA/ELD Framework's Four Zones of Teaching and Learning cross challenge with support: high challenge and high support is the learning/engagement zone; high challenge and low support is the frustration/anxiety zone; low challenge and high support is the comfort zone; low challenge and low support is the boredom zone.

Practice 2: Which zone is this lesson in?

Takeaway: Phonemic awareness tasks are spoken, not written, and they prepare children to map sounds to letters. When you plan a phonics or letter-formation lesson, keep the challenge high and plan the support (modeling, guided practice, feedback) that lets every student meet it, then fade that support as students gain independence.
