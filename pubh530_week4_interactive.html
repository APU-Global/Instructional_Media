Risk Assessment: From Hazard to Decision

Risk assessment gives public health a structured way to move from a hazard to a decision. Put the four steps in order, explore how dose and exposure combine to create risk, and see why people’s perception of risk matters as much as the calculation.

Part 1 · Put the steps in order

Risk assessment commonly has four steps. Select the steps in the order they are carried out. Each one relies on data and stated assumptions.

Start with the first step.

Part 2 · Dose and exposure

The dose often determines the harm, and who is exposed, and how much, depends on the person. Here a hypothetical chemical (“Chemical X”) is in a town’s drinking water. A reference dose is a daily dose per kilogram of body weight below which harm is not expected. Move the slider and compare an adult and a child drinking the same water. Simplified screening model: dose = concentration × water drunk per day ÷ body weight. Assumed intakes: adult 70 kg drinking 2 L/day; child 15 kg drinking 1 L/day. Reference dose for the hypothetical Chemical X: 0.005 mg/kg/day. A ratio of 1 or more is a signal to look closer, not a measure of illness. Real assessments state their assumptions and uncertainty.

Part 3 · Perceived risk and calculated risk

People judge risk using values, experience, and trust, so perceived risk may differ from calculated risk. Decide what is shaping each response, then choose how to communicate.
