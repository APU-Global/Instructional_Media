Week 7 Interactive: Measuring Success

Week 7 objective: Write measurable, time-bound performance measures and distinguish measures of change activities from measures of outcomes, and recognize which influence tactics win commitment to a measure.

Goals, objectives, and measures

Goals are general statements. Objectives are specific and time bound. Measures are the indicators used to assess whether an objective was achieved.

Model from Discussion 7: Wait staff members will be able to enter 100% of orders taken during a specified month within 10 minutes of taking the order, using a point-of-service tablet and specialized software. It names who, what, how much, by when, and with what.

Practice 1: what does this measure need?

Maple & Main Bakery, a fictional business, is moving to online ordering. Decide whether each proposed measure is complete or what it is missing.

Practice 2: activity or outcome?

Discussion 7 asks two questions about a change: were the planned activities completed, and what were their outcomes? Choose which question each metric answers.

Practice 3: compliance or commitment?

A measure only works if people support it. This week's textbook chapter, "Power and Politics," says an influence attempt can meet resistance, compliance, or commitment. Name the tactic each Maple & Main leader is using.

Takeaway: Write measures that state who, what, how much, and by when, and keep to the vital few. Track both whether planned activities happened and what outcomes followed. Break the change into phases so there are small wins to recognize, and when people fall short for lack of skill, respond with support and training rather than abandoning the goal. Explain the purpose behind a measure: pressure and position power rarely win commitment.
