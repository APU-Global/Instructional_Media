Week 5 Interactive: Adverse Childhood Experiences and Adult Criminality

Week 5 objective: Classify adverse childhood experiences (ACE) and interpret the findings of this week's podcast reading on ACE and adult criminality.

Why this matters

Your Risk and Criminality Podcast reading, Reavis and colleagues (2013), gave the ACE Questionnaire to 151 adult male offenders in court-ordered treatment and compared them with a normative sample. Knowing the ACE categories and reading the results carefully will help you discuss risk factors without overstating what the study proves.

Practice 1: abuse, neglect, or household dysfunction?

The ACE Questionnaire groups childhood experiences into three categories. Choose the category for each experience.

Practice 2: what does the study show?

Decide whether each statement is true or false according to the reading.

Takeaway: Offenders in this study reported far more childhood adversity than the normative sample, for example psychological abuse (52.3% vs. 7.6%) and criminal behavior in the household (20.5% vs. 4.1%). That is an association, not a sentence: protective factors in the family, school, peer, and community domains can buffer risk. Use both ideas in your podcast.

Facts from Reavis, J. A., Looman, J., Franco, K. A., & Rojas, B. (2013), "Adverse Childhood Experiences and Adult Criminality," The Permanente Journal, and Development Services Group (2015), Protective Factors Against Delinquency, Office of Juvenile Justice and Delinquency Prevention.
