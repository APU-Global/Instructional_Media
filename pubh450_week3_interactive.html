Is the Algorithm Fair?

AI can reproduce the biases in its training data, and unequal data can lead to unequal outcomes. Run a small fairness audit yourself, apply the core principles of health ethics, and review privacy and accountability.

Part 1 · Audit a screening tool

A screening tool flags people whose marker level is above a cut-off as likely to have a condition. The tool learns its cut-off from training data. Part 2 · Which principle is at issue?

Health ethics is often organized around four principles. They can be applied to AI decisions, and they sometimes conflict and require judgment.

AutonomyPeople make informed decisions about their own care
BeneficenceActing to do good and help
NonmaleficenceAvoiding harm (“first, do no harm”)
JusticeFair distribution of benefits and burdens

Part 3 · Privacy, bias, and accountability

Guiding question: How can the principle “first, do no harm” guide the use of AI in health? The course approaches this through systems thinking and a Christian ethical framework, aiming for more just and responsible use of AI.
