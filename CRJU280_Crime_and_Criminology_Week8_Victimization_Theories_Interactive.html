Week 8 Interactive: Explain Risk, Not Blame

Week 8 objective: Match situations to lifestyle-exposure, routine activities, and victim precipitation theories, and choose language that explains risk without blaming victims.

Routine activities: three elements

Cohen and Felson held that crime becomes likely when three elements meet in the same time and place. Select each element.

Select a card to see the details.

Practice 1: Which theory?

Each situation is fictional. Choose the theory that best helps explain it.

Practice 2: Blaming or explaining?

Advocates explain risk without blaming victims. Decide whether each fictional statement blames the victim or explains risk neutrally.

Takeaway: Victimization theories describe situations and risk, not fault. Use them to plan prevention and support: add capable guardians, reduce exposure, and remember that the offender is always responsible for the crime. Victim-blaming adds to the harm and can discourage people from coming forward.
