Week 4 Interactive: Confidentiality and Its Limits

Week 4 objective: Apply confidentiality, mandated reporting, and duty-to-warn standards to situations with adolescent clients.

The decision points

Teens name confidentiality concerns as the top reason they might skip care, so explain its limits early and revisit them often. In every scenario below you are a licensed counselor in California.

  • Keep confidential: the default. Keep working with the teen and encourage them to involve a trusted adult.
  • Break confidentiality for the teen's safety: serious risk of harm to self, such as suicidal thoughts with a plan.
  • Mandated child abuse report: you know or reasonably suspect abuse. No consent is needed, and a good-faith report is protected. Coerced sexual activity is always reportable; some age gaps are reportable by age alone.
  • Duty to warn or protect: a client communicates a serious threat of violence against an identifiable person (Tarasoff). In California the duty is discharged by reasonable efforts to communicate the threat to the victim and law enforcement.
  • Clinical judgment: some partner-age combinations are not reportable by age alone; report if you reasonably suspect coercion.

Practice only, not legal advice. These answers follow the California toolkit (2010) and NASW summary assigned this week. Laws differ by state and change over time, so always check current law where you practice.
