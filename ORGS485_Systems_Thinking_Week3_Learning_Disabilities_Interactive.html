Week 3 Interactive: Senge and the Learning Organization

Week 3 objective: Recognize the organizational learning disabilities, or delusions, that Peter Senge says a learning organization must face, and see how systems thinking answers them.

The four blind spots

Mental models

Deeply held assumptions and images shape how we see the world and act, often without our awareness. Change-capable organizations notice their shared models and revise them when they stop working.

The boiled frog

We react to sudden threats but miss gradual ones: slowly eroding market share, steady quality decline, the quiet loss of key people.

Learning from experience

“We learn best from experience but we never directly experience the consequences of many of our most important decisions.” When results are delayed or distant, trial and error stops teaching.

Individual initiative

When results lag, people “work harder.” Often the system, not the individual, is the problem, so look into the structures that shape individual actions.

Takeaway: A learning organization is skilled at creating, acquiring, and transferring knowledge and at changing its behavior to reflect new insight. That starts by admitting its blind spots and looking beneath events to the structures that produce them.
