Week 2 Interactive: Analyzing a Community Problem

Week 2 objective: Analyze your capstone community issue before proposing solutions, as Discussion 2 and Capstone Project Part 1 ask: justify the problem with evidence, state it fairly, and identify what keeps it from changing.

Six criteria for a community problem

Berkowitz (Community Tool Box, Chapter 3, Section 5) suggests these criteria for deciding whether an issue deserves attention.

FREQUENCY
It occurs too frequently.
DURATION
It has lasted for a while.
SCOPE
It affects many people.
SEVERITY
It is disruptive to personal or community life.
EQUITY
It deprives people of legal or moral rights.
PERCEPTION
It is perceived as a problem.

Part 1: Which criterion does the evidence support?

Each piece of evidence comes from the fictional city of Riverbend. Choose the criterion it best supports.

Part 2: Frame it without blame

Berkowitz advises stating a problem without implying a solution or blaming anyone. Choose the best-framed statement.

Part 3: Driving or restraining force?

In a force field analysis, restraining forces keep a problem from changing and driving forces push it toward change. Riverbend's issue: pedestrians are injured too often at crossings on its main arterial road.

Takeaway: Analyze before you act: justify the problem with evidence, frame it fairly, and know the forces for and against change. "If one gives an answer before he hears, it is his folly and shame" (Proverbs 18:13, ESV).

Criteria, framing, and force field analysis from Berkowitz, B. (n.d.). Section 5. Analyzing community problems. In Community Tool Box, Center for Community Health and Development, University of Kansas (Chapter 3). Scripture verified on esv.org. Riverbend and all figures are fictional.
