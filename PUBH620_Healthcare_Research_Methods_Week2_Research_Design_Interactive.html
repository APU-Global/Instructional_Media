Week 2 Interactive: Choosing a Research Design

Week 2 objective: Select and justify a research design that fits a public health research question, as you will in Part 2 of your proposal.

Two decisions every design makes

First, time: a cross-sectional study collects data once, a snapshot; longitudinal designs (trend, panel, cohort) observe over time. Second, control: a true experiment compares an experimental group and a control group, measured before and after. When random assignment is not possible or ethical, researchers use weaker but still useful designs.

Practice 1: snapshot, series, or experiment?

Each study is fictional. Choose the design it uses.

Practice 2: when randomizing is not possible

A fictional county wants to evaluate a smoking cessation program. Choose the design each plan describes.

Takeaway: A design is a trade-off. Choose the strongest design your question, ethics, time, and budget allow, then be honest about what it can and cannot show about cause: a causal claim needs a plausible link, a cause that comes before the effect, and no third variable that explains both.
