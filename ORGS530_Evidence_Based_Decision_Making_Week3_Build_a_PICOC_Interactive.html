Week 3 Interactive: Evidence From the Scientific Literature

Week 3 objective: Use PICOC to structure a search and appraise the studies it returns.

Structured questions make searches reproducible

A search tool is an organizing framework that lists terms by the main concepts in the question (Methley et al., 2014). Your course uses PICOC: Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome, Context. Recording each element and every search makes your search systematic, transparent, and reproducible.

Practice 1: build a PICOC

Question: “Among frontline supervisors in mid-size regional hospitals in the United States, does using the Balanced Scorecard, compared with budget-only reviews, improve unit performance?” Assign each phrase to its PICOC element.

Practice 2: search and appraise

Methley et al. (2014) ran the same search terms through three tools: PICO, PICOS (PICO plus study design), and SPIDER. Choose the best answer.

Takeaway: Every element you add narrows the search: you save screening time but risk missing studies. Then appraise what you find: levels of evidence rank risk of bias, not quality guaranteed.

Search tool comparison from Methley, A. M., Campbell, S., Chew-Graham, C., McNally, R., & Cheraghi-Sohi, S. (2014). PICO, PICOS and SPIDER: A comparison study of specificity and sensitivity in three search tools for qualitative systematic reviews. BMC Health Services Research, 14, 579. Levels of evidence from Burns, P. B., Rohrich, R. J., & Chung, K. C. (2011). The levels of evidence and their role in evidence-based medicine. Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery, 128(1), 305–310.
