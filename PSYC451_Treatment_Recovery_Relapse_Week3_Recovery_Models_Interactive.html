Week 3 Interactive: Comparing Recovery Models

Objective: Distinguish major recovery models and consider which one best fits a person’s values, needs, and barriers.

A short menu of paths

Florin and Trytek stress that recovery has many paths, but effective options form a “relatively brief menu”:

  • 12-step fellowships (AA, NA): admit powerlessness, rely on a higher power, make amends, and take “one day at a time.”
  • Celebrate Recovery: a Christ-centered 12-step program for all kinds of “hurts, hang-ups, and habits.”
  • SMART Recovery: meetings that use cognitive-behavioral techniques to cope and resist urges.
  • Women for Sobriety: a program for women whose steps remove the word “powerless.”
  • Cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT): anticipate problems, self-monitor cravings, and practice coping skills.
  • Motivational enhancement therapy (MET): brief sessions that help resolve ambivalence about change.
  • Community reinforcement approach (CRA): family, social, recreational, and vocational rewards make a sober life more rewarding than use.
