Week 7 Interactive: Rights-Based Global Health

Week 7 objective: Identify which element of the right to health a problem threatens and which human rights principle a practice puts into action, so you can recommend ethical conduct in global public health work.

The right to health, in WHO’s words

Every human being has “the right to the highest attainable standard of physical and mental health.” It includes freedoms, such as being free from non-consensual medical treatment and experimentation, and entitlements, such as access to quality health services without discrimination.

Availability
Enough functioning facilities, goods, and services for all.
Accessibility
Non-discrimination, physical, economic, and information access.
Acceptability
Medical ethics, culturally appropriate, gender-sensitive, consent.
Quality
Safe, effective, timely, and scientifically approved care.

Takeaway: Ethics in global health is not only about avoiding harm. Check every program and study against availability, accessibility, acceptability, and quality, and build in non-discrimination, participation, and accountability. The Tuskegee study, which enrolled men without informed consent and withheld treatment, shows what happens when these checks are missing.
