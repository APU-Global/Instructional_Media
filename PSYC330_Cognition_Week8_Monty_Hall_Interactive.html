Why switching wins 2 times in 3

Suppose you pick Door A. The prize is behind A in 1 of 3 cases; staying finds it only then. In the other 2 of 3 cases the prize is behind B or C, and the host must open the other empty door, so switching finds the prize. The host’s choice is not random: it depends on your pick, and that is information. In a 100-door version, your first pick is right only 1 time in 100, which makes the advantage of switching easier to see.

Takeaway: Across cultures, 79–87% of people stay with their first door, although switching finds the prize twice as often. Equiprobability thinking, an illusion of control, and anticipated regret pull us toward the wrong choice. For important decisions, slow down and use deliberate System 2 thinking: lay out the possibilities, look at frequencies, and consider other outcomes.
