Week 2 Interactive: Protecting Patient Data

Week 2 objective: Apply ethical principles in data privacy, security, and patient information management (CLO 1).

Three ideas to apply

Privacy
A person’s right to control the collection, use, disclosure, and retention of their personal information.
Confidentiality
A provider’s obligation to protect the secrecy of personal information.
Security
The tools and techniques that protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information.

Practice 1: which duty is at stake?

Each organization is fictional. Choose the best label for each situation, then select Check my answers.

The CIA triad

Confidentiality
Only people who are allowed to see information can access it.
Integrity
Information has not been altered and truly represents what is intended.
Availability
Authorized people can reach information in an appropriate timeframe.

Practice 2: which security goal is threatened?

Choose the goal each event puts at risk.

The circle of care

Access should follow a person’s role in the patient’s care: in a hospital, the attending physician and the team assigned to the patient. A privacy breach occurs when health information is collected, used, or disclosed without authorization, including theft, loss, or unauthorized access.

Practice 3: within the circle of care, or a breach?

Decide whether each action is appropriate access or a privacy breach.

Takeaway: Privacy is the patient’s right, confidentiality is your duty, and security is the toolkit that protects confidentiality, integrity, and availability. Before you look at or share a record, ask whether your role in this patient’s care requires it. Use these ideas as you identify ethical risks in this week’s assignment.
