About the course

Major concepts, methods, and issues involved in the profession of health education/health promotion are presented in this course. The course goal is to provide a solid grounding and appreciation for the history, science, and foundations of health education/health promotion. Each week pairs a health behavior or health promotion topic with public health data you analyze and discuss, and you evaluate social, behavioral, cultural, and faith-based factors throughout.

Projected total

Respond by Thursday. Discuss through Saturday.

Each of the eight discussions is worth 30 points (in Week 8: respond by Wednesday, discuss through Friday). Most use a public health dataset, a case, or a map from that week.

INITIAL POST
Respond to the prompt with a substantive post by the first deadline, addressing every part of the prompt.
RESPONSES TO PEERS
Respond to peers’ posts by the second deadline. The rubric looks for interaction with at least two peers.
SCHOLARLY SUPPORT
Extend the discussion in your own words, supported by scholarly references. Posts made after the academic week do not count toward your grade.

Discussions are graded with the syllabus discussion rubric: Assignment Requirements (10), Integrative Communication (10), and Written Communication (10), for 30 points.

Welcome aboard: Community health education starts with the data: who is affected, by what risk factors, and why rates differ between places and groups. This course moves from the foundations of health promotion through tobacco, eating, physical activity, mental health, and preventive medicine to national and global initiatives, and asks you to turn what you find into a health education action plan for a community you care about. Start your public health issue in Week 1, pair with a peer early for the case studies, and ask questions in Ask the Prof whenever you need help.
