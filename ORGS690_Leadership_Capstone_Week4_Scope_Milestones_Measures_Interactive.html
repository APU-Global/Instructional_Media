Week 4 Interactive: Defining and Planning Your Program

Week 4 objective: Apply scope, milestones, measures, and change control to the Statement of Work and timeline in Capstone Project Part 2.

Four planning tools

A scope document (your Statement of Work) says what the project includes and, just as importantly, what it does not. A milestone is an important point in the project with no duration or resources of its own. A measure is an indicator of whether an objective is achieved; keep a few, tied to the drivers of success. Finally, a small schedule slip that does not move the end date is a deviation; adding or removing work is a scope change that should be requested in writing and logged.

Activity 1: Sort the plan

A fictional student is planning "Lead Forward," a volunteer leadership academy at Eastside Food Bank. Sort each item from the plan.

Activity 2: Deviation or scope change?

Things change during every project. Decide whether each situation is a deviation (adjust and record it) or a scope change (request it in writing, explain its value, and log it).

Takeaway: Write your Statement of Work so a reader knows what is in and what is out, break the work into pieces you can manage, mark milestones, choose a few research-based measures, and keep a change log. Counting the cost before you build is wise stewardship (Luke 14:28).
