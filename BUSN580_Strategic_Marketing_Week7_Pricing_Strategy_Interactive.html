Week 7 Interactive: Pricing and Negotiation

Week 7 objective: Identify common pricing strategies, recognize illegal pricing practices, and calculate a breakeven point.

Three pricing reminders

Fit the mixPrice should match the product, promotion, and distribution image.
Perceived valueCustomers buy when the value they perceive is worth the price.
Easy to copyPrices can be changed and matched quickly, so price alone rarely sustains an advantage.

Practice 1: name the pricing strategy

Each fictional company is pricing a product. Which strategy is it using?

Practice 2: where is the legal line?

Decide whether each pricing practice is generally legal or crosses a legal line.

Practice 3: find the breakeven point

Fernwood Pantry, a fictional specialty-food maker, is pricing a new sauce. Breakeven units = fixed costs ÷ (price − variable cost per unit). Change the numbers to see how price and costs move the breakeven point.

Try it: raise the price to $16. Breakeven falls from 6,000 to 4,000 units, but will customers who see the sauce as a good value still buy it?

Takeaway: Choose a pricing objective first, ground the price in perceived value, elasticity, competition, and cost, and pick the strategy on purpose. Whatever you choose, keep pricing honest: Proverbs 11:1 calls a false balance an abomination to the Lord and a just weight his delight.
