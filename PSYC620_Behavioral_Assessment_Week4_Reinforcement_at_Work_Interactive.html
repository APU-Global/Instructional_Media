Week 4 Interactive: Name That Contingency at Work

Week 4 objective: Tell positive and negative reinforcement apart from punishment in workplace examples, and recognize the reinforcement schedules that shape work behavior.

Three steps to name any contingency

1. Given or removed?
Something added is positive. Something taken away is negative. Neither word means good or bad.
2. Up or down?
If the behavior becomes more likely, it is reinforcement. If less likely, it is punishment.
3. Put it together
Positive reinforcement, negative reinforcement, positive punishment, or negative punishment.

Practice 1: Which contingency is at work?

Each scene takes place at Harbor Point Health, a fictional clinic. Read what happens after the behavior and whether the behavior goes up or down, then choose the contingency.

Practice 2: Which schedule is it?

Reinforcement at work often arrives on a schedule. Choose the schedule each fictional example describes.

Practice 3: Design one of each

Harbor Point wants front-desk staff to verify each patient's insurance before the visit. Choose the option that is a true negative reinforcement plan for increasing that behavior.

Takeaway: Reinforcement always makes behavior more likely; punishment makes it less likely. Negative reinforcement strengthens a behavior by removing something unpleasant, through escape or avoidance. At work, reinforcers work best when they are contingent, immediate, large enough to matter, and suited to the person.

Concepts from Daffin, L. W., Jr. (n.d.), Principles of Learning and Behavior (2nd ed.), LibreTexts: 6.2 Basics of Operant Conditioning, 6.3 Factors on Operant Learning, and 6.4 Schedules of Reinforcement. Harbor Point Health and all scenes are fictional. Supports Discussion 4.
