About the course

Provides critical analysis and applications of research, theory, and interventions for child, adolescent, and family issues with an emphasis on empirically-supported practices. The course allows much independent reading and research across three populations (children, adolescents, and families), so you can narrow your findings to your own field of interest, and you integrate biblical and psychological perspectives throughout.

Projected total

Respond by Thursday. Discuss through Saturday.

Each of the eight discussions is worth 30 points. In Week 8, respond by Wednesday and discuss through Friday. Here are the course’s discussion requirements.

INITIAL POST
250–300 words
Answer every part of the week’s prompt, with 1–2 APA-formatted sources.
RESPONSES TO PEERS
150 words minimum each
Respond to a minimum of three posts in an exchange of ideas that continues through the second deadline.
SUBSTANTIVE POSTS
Offer further resources, questions of clarification, or context and insight. If you agree, explain why and further the conversation. Posts made after the academic week do not count.

Discussions are graded with the syllabus discussion rubric: Engagement (5), Critical Thinking (10), Contributions to Discussion (10), and Communication and Collaboration (5), for 30 points.
