What Does a Shipment Really Cost?

Sound transportation decisions begin with understanding what a shipment actually costs. Sort the cost drivers, then watch how volume changes the cost of every trip.

Part 1 · Sort the cost drivers

Carriers and shippers analyze fixed, variable, and joint costs. Fixed costs do not change with volume in the short run; variable costs change with distance, weight, or number of trips; a joint cost arises when providing one service automatically creates another.

Part 2 · Cost per trip on a common basis

Your company runs its own truck. Adjust the volume and distance, then compare your cost per trip with a for-hire carrier's price. Compare rates on a common basis, such as cost per trip or per unit.

Two ways to price

Cost-of-service pricing
The rate is built up from the carrier's cost of providing the service, plus a margin.
Value-of-service pricing
The rate reflects what the service is worth to the shipper, such as speed or reliability, not only what it costs the carrier.

When you compare options, make your assumptions visible and consider service quality along with price.
