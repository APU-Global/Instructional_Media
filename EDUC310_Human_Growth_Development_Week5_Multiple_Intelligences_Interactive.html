Week 5 Interactive: Multiple Intelligences

Week 5 objective: Connect classroom activities to Gardner’s intelligences and separate research-supported practice from myths.

Gardner’s idea in brief

Howard Gardner proposed that intelligence is not a single IQ. Your textbook lists nine domains: logical-mathematical, linguistic, spatial, musical, bodily-kinesthetic, naturalistic, interpersonal, intrapersonal, and existential. Everyone has all of them at varying levels. Multiple intelligences are not learning styles, and students should not be labeled as one type of learner.

Practice 1: which intelligence does it draw on?

Each lesson idea is fictional. Choose the intelligence each activity draws on most.

Practice 2: supported or not?

Decide whether each statement is supported by this week’s readings.

Takeaway: Use multiple intelligences as a toolbox for teaching, not a label for students. Offer many ways into the content and many ways to show learning, and build each choice on what you know about the student’s strengths and needs.
