Week 7 Interactive: Collecting and Describing Data Fairly

Week 7 objective: Identify sampling methods and sources of bias in a study, and summarize a data set with a five-number summary.

Key ideas

The population is the group a study intends to describe; the sample is the part actually studied. A sampling method is biased if every member of the population does not have an equal chance of being in the sample. Bias can also come from who funds a study, how questions are worded, who refuses to answer, and factors that are mixed up with the treatment (confounding).

Practice 3: five-number summary

Fifteen students' scores on a fictional exam, already in order:

52, 61, 64, 68, 70, 72, 75, 77, 78, 81, 84, 86, 88, 92, 97

Use the textbook's locator method. For Q1, compute L = 0.25n; if L is a decimal, round up and take that data value; if L is a whole number, average the values in positions L and L + 1. Use 0.75n for Q3. The median is the middle value.

Mean or median? Suppose the lowest score, 52, is replaced by a 12. Which measure of center changes?

Takeaway: Before trusting a statistic, ask who was sampled, how, and who paid or asked the questions. When describing data, the median and the five-number summary are not pulled by an extreme value the way the mean is.
