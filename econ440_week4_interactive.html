Who Pays for a Trade Barrier?

A tariff raises the domestic price and generates government revenue; it protects domestic producers but harms consumers. Impose a tariff or a quota, then see who gains, who loses, and how much society loses overall.

Part 1 · Impose a barrier on imports

A small country imports a good. At the world price it buys more than it makes, and the difference is imported. Choose an instrument and set its size. Barriers typically create deadweight loss.

Part 3 · Ask the questions

Who pays and who benefits? Protected industries gain; consumers lose, and the loss is larger than the gain. Barriers can also provoke retaliation that hurts exporters.
Use real cases. Evaluate policies with data, such as the steel and aluminum tariffs, and look for effects on prices, jobs in industries that use the protected good, and trading partners’ responses.
