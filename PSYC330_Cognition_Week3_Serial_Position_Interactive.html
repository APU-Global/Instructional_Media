Week 3 Interactive: The Serial Position Effect

Week 3 objective: Explain why we remember the first and last items in a series best, and choose strategies that protect the middle.

Step 1: Study a list

You will see 12 words, one at a time. Read each word once, then select Next word. Do not go back or write anything down.

Ready when you are.

Step 2: Recall

Type every word you remember, in any order, separated by spaces or commas. Then select Score my recall.

Step 3: Apply it

Each situation is fictional. Choose the best answer, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: The first items in a list get extra rehearsal and move into long-term memory (primacy); the last items are still in short-term memory when you recall (recency). The middle gets the least rehearsal, so give it extra attention: space your study, overlearn, vary the order, organize items into categories, and relate them to yourself.

Concepts from Introduction to Psychology v1.0 (Saylor Academy), Sections 8.1 "Memories as Types and Stages" and 8.2 "How We Remember: Cues to Improving Memory." Word list and scenarios are fictional. Your own recall results vary from trial to trial; the curve is clearest when many people's results are averaged.
