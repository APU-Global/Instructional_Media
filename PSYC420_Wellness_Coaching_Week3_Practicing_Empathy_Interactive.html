Week 3 Interactive: Practicing Empathy

Week 3 objective: Distinguish sympathy, empathy, and compassion and recognize the responses that help a person feel understood.

Three responses that are often confused

In interviews with 53 adults with advanced cancer, patients described these three responses very differently and disliked sympathy most.

SympathyA pity-based reaction to someone's misfortune. Patients experienced it as distant, shallow, and lacking understanding.
EmpathyUnderstanding and accurately acknowledging another person's feelings, leading to an attuned response. It moves toward the suffering.
CompassionEmpathy plus action: a response, motivated by love, that seeks to relieve suffering through relational understanding and action, often through small kindnesses.
What helpedEmpathy and compassion left patients feeling heard, understood, and validated.

Practice: name the response

Marco (fictional) lost his job last month and says he lies awake at night worrying about his family. Choose the best label for each response, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: Sympathy pities from a distance, empathy comes close to understand, and compassion understands and then acts. In coaching, confirm the feeling first, then ask how you can help.

Definitions and findings from Sinclair, S., et al. (2017), “Sympathy, empathy, and compassion: A grounded theory study of palliative care patients’ understandings, experiences, and preferences,” Palliative Medicine, 31(5), 437–447 (open access, PMC5405806). Marco and every scenario are fictional.
