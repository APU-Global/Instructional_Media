Week 3 Interactive: Stages of Spelling Development

Week 3 objective: Use students' spelling attempts as evidence of where they are, so instruction neither frustrates nor bores them.

Five stages (names vary)

  1. Prephonetic (emergent): marks or letters with little, if any, relationship to sounds.
  2. Phonetic (letter name-alphabetic): letters represent sounds; invented spellings such as duk for duck are common.
  3. Within word pattern: moving from one-to-one letter-sound matches to spelling-sound patterns, such as digraphs and long-vowel spellings, in single-syllable words.
  4. Syllables and affixes: spelling words of more than one syllable; doubling, dropping, or changing letters when adding endings and affixes.
  5. Derivational relations: using morphology to spell by meaning, keeping Greek and Latin roots consistent even when sounds change.

Practice

Each sample comes from a fictional student. Choose the stage the attempt best shows, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: Spelling is a developmental process, not only memorization (though irregular words must be memorized). Read drafts as evidence, teach encoding and decoding together, and differentiate so every child is challenged at the right stage.

Stages and examples from English Language Arts/English Language Development Framework (California Department of Education, adopted 2014), Chapter 4 "Grades Two and Three" (Spelling; Figure 4.8, Stages of Spelling Development) and Chapter 3 (Using Language Conventions). Student samples are fictional.
