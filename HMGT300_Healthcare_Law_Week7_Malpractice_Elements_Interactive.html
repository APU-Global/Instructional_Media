Week 7 Interactive: The Four Elements of Malpractice

Week 7 objective: Apply the four elements of malpractice (duty, breach, causation, damages) to healthcare incidents and see why every error still deserves reporting.

The four elements

Medical negligence that injures a patient becomes medical malpractice. The plaintiff must prove four elements: (1) Duty: a relationship, such as physician-patient, created an obligation to act reasonably; (2) Breach: the provider departed from the standard of care; (3) Causation: but for the breach, the foreseeable injury would not have occurred; and (4) Damages: the patient suffered actual loss. If any element is missing, the claim fails, but the event may still need to be reported and fixed.

Takeaway: A malpractice claim needs duty, breach, causation, and damages. Near misses and harmless breaches will not support a lawsuit, but they are exactly the events a Just Culture reports without blame, traces to a root cause, and fixes. Keep the record accurate: falsifying documentation can bring False Claims Act liability.
