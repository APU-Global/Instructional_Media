Week 5 Interactive: Trauma-Informed Screening

Week 5 objective: Explain what the PCL-5 and MDQ measure, apply their screening rules, and choose trauma-informed ways to talk about results.

What the PCL-5 measures

The PCL-5 has 20 items rated 0 (Not at all) to 4 (Extremely), total 0-80. Its subscales follow the four DSM-5 clusters: B Re-experiencing (items 1-5), C Avoidance (6-7), D Negative alterations in cognition and mood (8-14), and E Hyper-arousal (15-20). A rating of 2 (Moderately) or higher counts as an endorsed symptom. The DSM-5 rule for a provisional result needs at least 1 B, 1 C, 2 D, and 2 E symptoms; a total of 33 or more is treated as clinically significant. A screener is a smoke alarm, not the fire investigator: diagnosis needs a structured interview by a licensed professional.

Takeaway: A trauma screener asks about painful experiences, so how it is given matters as much as the score. Explain the purpose, honor the person's choices, describe results as flags, and refer to a qualified clinician.
