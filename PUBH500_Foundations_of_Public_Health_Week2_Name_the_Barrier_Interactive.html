Week 2 Interactive: Barriers to Children’s Physical Activity

Week 2 objective: Identify family-level and community-level barriers to children’s physical activity and match each to a solution that community members proposed.

What the Colorado study found

Finkelstein, Petersen, and Schottenfeld (2017) held 20 focus groups with 128 parents and 42 youth in five low-income Colorado communities. They found 12 barrier themes: 3 in the family context (parents’ work schedules, a child’s lack of interest or apprehension, and youth’s school and family commitments) and 9 in the community context (including affordability, traffic safety, unsafe or illicit activity in public spaces, access to high-quality facilities, transportation, and lack of information). Most proposed solutions were community-level.

Practice 1: name the barrier

Each statement comes from a fictional family in the fictional town of Pine Mesa. Choose the barrier theme it shows, then select Check my answers.

Practice 2: family or community?

Decide whether each barrier from the study sits mainly in the family context or the community context.

Takeaway: Nine of the twelve barriers were community-level, while 81% of parents already said regular exercise was important for their child. A task force that only urges families to be active misses most of the problem: fix the conditions (cost, safety, access, transportation, information) and ask the community first.

Findings, themes, and proposed solutions from Finkelstein, D. M., Petersen, D. M., & Schottenfeld, L. S. (2017). Promoting children’s physical activity in low-income communities in Colorado: What are the barriers and opportunities? Preventing Chronic Disease, 14, E134. The statements and the town of Pine Mesa are fictional.
