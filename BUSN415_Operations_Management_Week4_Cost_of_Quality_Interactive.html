Week 4 Interactive: Cost of Quality and SPC

Week 4 objective: Classify prevention, appraisal, internal failure, and external failure costs, and use a control chart to separate common-cause variation from special causes.

Why this matters

Organizations that deliver high quality incur fewer losses from rework, inspections, and scrap. Quality costs fall into four categories: prevention and appraisal (costs of conformance) and internal and external failure (costs of nonconformance). Control charts, first devised by Walter Shewhart in 1924, help find problems while work is still in process.

Takeaway: Prevention and appraisal are investments; internal and external failures are losses, and failures that reach customers add replacements, recalls, liability, and lost goodwill. A control chart is an appraisal tool: points outside the limits flag special causes so you can fix them before they become failure costs.
