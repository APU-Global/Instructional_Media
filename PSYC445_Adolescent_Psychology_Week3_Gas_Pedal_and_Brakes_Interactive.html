Week 3 Interactive: The Dual Systems Model

Week 3 objective: Connect adolescent brain development to behavior by identifying which system a cue points to.

Two systems on different timelines

Steinberg's Dual Systems Model, as Chapters 2 and 3 explain it, describes a race between two brain networks. Teens often know the risks; what lags is control in emotionally charged moments.

  • Gas pedal (socioemotional system): the limbic system, including the ventral striatum and amygdala, surges at puberty and makes rewards, thrills, and peer approval feel intense.
  • Brakes (cognitive control system): the prefrontal cortex handles planning, weighing future consequences, and self-control, and matures gradually into the mid-twenties.
  • Cold cognition: in calm settings without peers, teens can reason about risk about as well as adults. Hot cognition (excitement, stakes, peers) lets the gas override the brakes.
