Week 1 Interactive: The Three Financial Statements

Week 1 objective: Interpret healthcare financial statements and the types of information provided by a balance sheet, income statement, and cash flow statement.

Three statements, three questions

The income statement (a nonprofit's statement of activities) lists revenues earned and expenses incurred over a period. The balance sheet (statement of financial position) lists assets and liabilities at one date; assets = liabilities + equity, and a nonprofit calls its equity net assets. The statement of cash flows explains how cash changed, in three sections: operating, investing, and financing. The organizations below are fictional: Riverbend Community Clinic (nonprofit) and Summit Surgical Center (for-profit).

Part 2: Which cash flow section?

Operating = the organization's central, daily activities. Investing = buying or selling assets outside daily operations. Financing = borrowing, repaying debt, and owner transactions.

Enter whole dollars (no $ sign needed).

Takeaway: The income statement shows performance over a period, the balance sheet shows position on one date, and the statement of cash flows sorts cash into operating, investing, and financing. Nonprofits use different names (statement of activities, statement of financial position, net assets), but the logic is the same: assets always equal liabilities plus equity or net assets.
