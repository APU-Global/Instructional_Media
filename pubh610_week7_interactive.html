Conflict Is Not the Problem

Unmanaged conflict is. Explore the five conflict-management styles, choose one on purpose for different situations, and practice negotiating from interests instead of fixed positions.

Part 1 · Five conflict styles

People tend to use competing, collaborating, compromising, avoiding, or accommodating approaches. No style is best in every situation, and knowing your default helps you choose deliberately. Select a style on the grid.

Part 3 · Negotiate interests, not positions

Negotiation seeks agreements that meet underlying interests. Two programs both want the one meeting room on Tuesday at 2 p.m. First, sort what each side says.

Parent-support group
Position: “We need the room Tuesday at 2.”
Diabetes education class
Position: “No, that time is ours.”

Now find an option that meets both interests

The support group needs a quiet, private space when school gets out. The class needs a room with a kitchen demonstration area and enough chairs for 20 people.

Look past positions. Ask “why does that matter to you?” Listen as much as you speak, paraphrase to confirm understanding, and ask open questions before offering solutions. Look for options that create value for both sides.
