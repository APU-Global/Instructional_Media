Week 6 Interactive: Responding to the Great Depression

Week 6 objective: Explain how Herbert Hoover and Franklin Roosevelt answered the Great Depression differently, and classify New Deal programs by their main goal.

Three goals

In a 1934 fireside chat, Roosevelt described his program as "measures of recovery and relief and reform." Relief meant immediate help and jobs for people in need; recovery meant restoring farms and industry; reform meant lasting changes in law and in government's role so a collapse would be less likely and its victims better protected. Some programs did more than one thing, so choose each one's main goal.

        Takeaway: Hoover trusted voluntary cooperation ("Associationalism") and feared that direct federal aid would weaken the work ethic; Roosevelt used federal power for relief, recovery, and reform. When you analyze a Depression-era source for Discussion 6, ask which of these views it reflects, and who was helped or left out: the Social Security Act and the larger New Deal excluded large groups of Americans.
