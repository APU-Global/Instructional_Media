Week 7 Interactive: Moral Reasoning and Identity

Week 7 objective: Classify adolescent moral reasoning by Kohlberg’s stages and identity choices by Marcia’s statuses.

The dilemma

Jordan, a fictional 15-year-old, finds a photo of tomorrow’s test answer key in a group chat. His best friend begs him to share it. Kohlberg cared less about what a person decides than why: the reasoning reveals the stage. Preconventional (Stages 1-2) focuses on consequences and self-interest; conventional (Stages 3-4) on approval and social order; postconventional (Stages 5-6) on social contracts and universal principles.

Practice 1: what stage is the reasoning?

Each statement is a possible reason Jordan might give. Choose the stage it reflects.

Practice 2: identity status

Marcia described identity by exploration and commitment. Each teen is fictional. Choose the status.

Takeaway: Questions and testing are normal in adolescence. Adults help most when they model moral character, discuss dilemmas that expose weak reasoning, give teens real responsibility, and support exploration that leads to commitments the teen owns.

Stages and examples from Lifespan Development (Lumen Learning, n.d.), Module 6 “Moral Development” and Module 7 “Moral Reasoning During Adolescence” and “Identity Formation”; Moral Education K-12 (n.d.), “Lawrence Kohlberg: Stage Based Moral Development.” Jordan and the other teens are fictional.
