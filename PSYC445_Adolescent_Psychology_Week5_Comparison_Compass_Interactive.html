Week 5 Interactive: Social Comparison in Adolescence

Week 5 objective: Explain how social comparison on social media shapes adolescent self-esteem and identity.

Three directions of comparison

Festinger's social comparison theory says people judge their abilities and opinions by looking at others. In adolescence peers become the main mirror, and social media multiplies the mirrors.

  • Upward: comparing yourself to someone who seems better off. It can inspire goals but more often widens the gap between your actual self and your ideal self, especially about appearance.
  • Downward: comparing yourself to someone who seems worse off. It briefly protects self-esteem but can feed judgmental, competitive dynamics.
  • Lateral: comparing yourself to equals. It is the most common type and builds belonging, but over-reliance can pressure teens to conform.

Practice: read the compass

Each teen is fictional. Choose the direction of comparison, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: The direction of comparison shapes the effect. Frequent upward comparison, especially about appearance, is linked to lower self-esteem and body dissatisfaction. Teens do better when their worth does not hang on the feed.
