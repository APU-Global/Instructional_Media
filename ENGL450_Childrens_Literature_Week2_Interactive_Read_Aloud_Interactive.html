Week 2 Interactive: Plan an Interactive Read-Aloud

Week 2 objective: Plan a read-aloud that invites every child to respond, and choose books in culturally responsive ways, so the children's responses can become evidence for your discussion.

What the research-based guidance says

Interactive read-alouds (Collier, 2023)
  • Plan about four stops in a 10-minute read-aloud; more feels choppy.
  • At each stop: stop and jot, turn and talk, gesture, or act it out.
  • Preview the text, tie stops to the standard you teach, and note them on sticky notes; offer sentence stems.
  • Ask, "Why am I choosing this text for these students?" Texts offer windows and doors into others' experiences and reflections of students' own.
Culturally responsive teaching (Steps to Success)
  • Learn students' funds of knowledge: ask questions, use interest inventories, meet families, attend community events.
  • Reflect on how your own background and biases shape expectations.
  • Beware the "apprenticeship of observation": copying the teaching you saw as a student.

Takeaway: A read-aloud becomes interactive when you plan a few purposeful stops. Mix ways to respond, so children who prefer to draw, talk, move, or gesture all have a way in, and choose books with your students' families and communities in mind.
