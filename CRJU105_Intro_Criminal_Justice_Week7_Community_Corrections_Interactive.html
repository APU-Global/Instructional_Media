Supervision without prison walls

Most people under correctional control are not in jails or prisons. Diversion moves a person out of the formal process. Probation suspends a jail or prison sentence in exchange for conditions supervised in the community. Intermediate sanctions such as intensive supervision probation, drug courts, and halfway houses add control or treatment. Restorative justice brings victims, offenders, and community members together to repair harm. Parole releases people under conditions after they have served part of a prison sentence.

Takeaway: Community corrections is not one program but a continuum, from a warning at first contact to supervision after prison. Research suggests that control alone does little to reduce reoffending, while options that pair accountability with treatment and repair, such as drug courts, effective halfway houses, and restorative justice, show more promise.
