Week 7 Interactive: Academic Vocabulary and Language Functions

Week 7 objective: Classify vocabulary so you can decide which words to teach explicitly, and recognize the language functions that graphic organizers and sentence frames support.

Three categories of vocabulary

The ELA/ELD Framework (Figure 2.13) sorts vocabulary into three categories:

  • Conversational (Tier One): words of everyday use, such as happy, dog, run, or water.
  • General academic (Tier Two): words far more likely to appear in text than in everyday speech, useful across many kinds of texts, often with precise or nuanced meanings, such as develop, technique, disrupt, or essential.
  • Domain-specific (Tier Three): words specific to a field of study and key to a new concept, such as equation, place value, landform, or thermometer.

Tier Two words affect meaning but are rarely defined in the text, so some consider them most in need of attention. Tier Three words are usually taught in the context of the discipline.

Practice 2: Which language function?

AVID names five language functions common across content areas, each signaled by typical words and supported by a graphic organizer and sentence frames. Read each student sentence from fictional grade 4 lessons and choose its function.

Takeaway: Choose the words first: content-specific words with the concepts they name, and general academic words that appear in every subject. Then give students the structures to use them: signal words, a graphic organizer, and sentence frames for the language function the task requires.
