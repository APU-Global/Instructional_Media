Week 6 Interactive: Universal Design for Learning in Literacy

Week 6 objective: Identify how engagement, representation, and action and expression each remove barriers in a foundational literacy lesson.

The three UDL principles

Universal Design for Learning (UDL) plans for learner variability at the point of first teaching, instead of adding fixes later. The ELA/ELD Framework summarizes three principles:

  • Engagement (the why): options for recruiting interest, sustaining effort and persistence, and self-regulation.
  • Representation (the what): options for perception; language, mathematical expressions, and symbols; and comprehension.
  • Action and expression (the how): options for physical action, expression and communication, and executive functions.

Practice: Which principle does it apply?

Each modification belongs to a fictional second-grade lesson on reading words with the vowel team ee. Choose the principle it mainly applies, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: UDL is not a special education add-on. When you plan the why, the what, and the how of a lesson for the full range of learners from the start, more students succeed in first teaching and fewer need catch-up instruction later.
