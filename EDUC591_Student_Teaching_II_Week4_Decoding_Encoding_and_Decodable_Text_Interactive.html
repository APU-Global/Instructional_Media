Week 4 Interactive: Phonics and Decodable Text

Week 4 objective: Distinguish decoding from encoding, judge whether text is decodable for a group, and check a phonics lesson against the SB 488 definition.

Lesson Plan 2 in brief

The California Dyslexia Guidelines call phonics "a two-way street": decoding maps letters to sounds (reading), and encoding maps sounds to letters (spelling). Decodable books are "books with words that can be mostly sounded out" with the patterns students have learned. SB 488, quoted in the CalTPA Guide, defines "direct, systematic, explicit phonics" as phonemic awareness, spelling patterns, the direct instruction of sound/symbol codes, and practice in connected text.

Practice 1: decoding or encoding?

Each classroom moment is fictional. Decide which direction of the code students are practicing.

Practice 2: is it decodable for this group?

A fictional first-grade group has learned short a, i, and o in CVC words, the digraphs sh and ch, and the high-frequency words the, is, a, and to. Is each sentence decodable for them now?

Practice 3: what is missing from this phonics lesson?

Check each fictional lesson against the SB 488 definition of direct, systematic, explicit phonics.

Takeaway: In Lesson Plan 2, have students read and spell the same pattern, teach the code directly, and finish in connected, decodable text built from patterns they have learned.
