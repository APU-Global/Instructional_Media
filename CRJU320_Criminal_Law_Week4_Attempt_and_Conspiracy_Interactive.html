Four tests for the attempt act

Thoughts and mere preparation are not criminal. Jurisdictions use one of four tests to decide when a defendant has gone far enough to commit attempt. Attempt also requires the specific intent, or purpose, to commit the crime. Select a test.

Takeaway: The law punishes unfinished crimes to prevent harm and deter repeat tries. Planning alone is not attempt, but a substantial step that corroborates criminal purpose is. Conspiracy is complete much earlier, at the agreement (plus an overt act where required), and under the Pinkerton rule conspirators can answer for foreseeable crimes committed in furtherance of the plan.
