Week 2 Interactive: Six Ethical Principles

Week 2 objective: Identify which foundational ethical principle a healthcare decision protects or puts at risk, as a first step toward resolving ethical conflicts.

The six principles

Autonomy: the right to self-determination; patients may refuse care. Beneficence: acting to benefit others and remove harm, often beyond what the law requires. Nonmaleficence: a duty to do no harm, balancing avoidable harm against the good achieved. Justice: fairness, equality, and equity for all. Fidelity: keeping promises and providing competent care. Veracity: telling the truth, as in informed consent.

Practice: name the principle

Each organization and person is fictional. Choose the principle that the decision most directly protects or puts at risk, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: Naming the principle is the first step in any ethical conflict. When two principles collide, such as autonomy and beneficence when a patient refuses care, gather the facts, decide what the law requires, name the values at stake, hear each stakeholder, and weigh the possible courses of action before choosing.
