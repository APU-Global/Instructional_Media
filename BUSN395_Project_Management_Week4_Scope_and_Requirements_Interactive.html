Week 4 Interactive: Project Scope and Scheduling

Week 4 objective: Define requirements precisely, hold the line on what is in and out of scope, and connect the work in the right order.

The case: Maple Street Clinic Online Booking

A fictional family clinic hires you to replace phone-only scheduling with online booking. Scope statement deliverables: an online booking page, text-message reminders, a staff schedule view, and training for front-desk staff. Non-goals (out of scope): a mobile app, online bill payment, and video visits.

Step 1: Name the requirement type

Chapter 9 sorts requirements into six categories. Functional requirements say what the deliverable should do; non-functional requirements constrain it (performance such as response time or availability); technical requirements say how it will be built; business requirements state the sponsor's reasons; user requirements describe the user's experience; regulatory requirements come from laws and licenses.

Step 2: In scope or out of scope?

Compare each request with the scope statement above. Anything outside it needs a change request through change control, not a quiet yes.

Step 3: Does the WBS branch pass the 100 percent rule?

Each child level must represent 100 percent of the work of its parent. Compare each branch with what the scope says it must include.

Step 4: Pick the dependency

Chapter 10 describes four predecessor types: finish-to-start (FS), start-to-start (SS), finish-to-finish (FF), and start-to-finish (SF).

Takeaway: Clear scope means measurable requirements of the right type, a written list of non-goals, a WBS that captures 100 percent of the work, and dependencies that put that work in the right order. When a request falls outside the scope, send it through change control.

Concepts from Watt, A. (2014), Project Management (2nd ed.), BCcampus, CC BY 4.0: Chapter 9 "Scope Planning" (six requirement types and the ATM examples, scope statement and non-goals, the WBS and the 100 percent rule) and Chapter 10 "Project Schedule Planning" (FS, SS, FF, and SF predecessors; change requests), with one note from Chapter 2 "Project Management Overview" (added scope and cost). The Maple Street Clinic case is fictional.
