Week 2 Interactive: Defining Community Needs

Week 2 objective: Use Bradshaw's four categories of need and a problem tree to define a community health problem before choosing a priority population.

Key ideas

Felt need

What the community itself thinks should be a priority.

Expressed need

What people show through their actions, such as long waiting lists for a service.

Normative need

How current conditions compare with a professional or expert standard.

Comparative need

How the situation compares with that of other people or places. When all four types are present, a "real" need is indicated.

Practice 1: which kind of need?

Each finding comes from the fictional town of Maple Creek. Choose the type of need it shows, then select Check my answers.

Practice 2: build the problem tree

Core problem: many older adults in Maple Creek have uncontrolled type 2 diabetes. Ask "What causes that?" Causes go below the core problem; effects go above it.

Takeaway: A defensible priority starts with an honest picture of need. When felt, expressed, normative, and comparative evidence all point the same way, and the problem tree traces root causes rather than symptoms, you can make a strong case for who should be served first.
