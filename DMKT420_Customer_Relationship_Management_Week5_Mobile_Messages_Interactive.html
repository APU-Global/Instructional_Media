Week 5 Interactive: Mobile CRM Messages

Week 5 objective: Evaluate CRM text messages for permission, timing, and clarity, and draft a follow-up text that builds the customer up.

Personal device, personal standards

People see their phones as very personal, so texts need permission (opt-in, with an easy opt-out) and value. Timing matters more than in email because texts arrive immediately. Citing SMS Global, Stokes suggests general marketing texts between 10:30 and 11:30 a.m. or 2:30 and 3:30 p.m., appointment reminders 24 hours out and/or directly after the appointment is made, and weekend-sale reminders on Thursday or the morning of the event, then testing with your own customers. A text holds 160 characters, should name the sender, include an opt-out, and link only to a mobile-friendly page.

Practice 1: send it or fix it?

Each text is from a fictional business. Decide whether it is ready to send or needs work.

Practice 2: write your own follow-up text

Draft a follow-up text for a fictional business. The checker updates as you type. It looks for the basics Stokes lists; tone and timing are still your call.

    Takeaway: Earn the right to text: get permission, send only what helps, when it helps, name yourself, keep it under 160 characters, and always offer a way out. Words that build up, at the right moment, strengthen the relationship.
