Week 6 Interactive: Diagnosing Barriers to Access

Week 6 objective: Classify barriers to health care and prevention programs using CDC’s seven common barriers, and match each barrier to a fix of the same type.

Barriers create disability

CDC adopts the World Health Organization’s definition: barriers are “factors in a person’s environment that, through their absence or presence, limit functioning and create disability.” CDC names seven common types.

Attitudinal
Stereotyping, stigma, prejudice, discrimination. The most basic; it contributes to the others.
Communication
Small print, no braille or screen-reader versions, uncaptioned video, technical language.
Physical
Steps, curbs, and equipment such as scales or mammography machines that exclude people.
Policy
Lack of awareness or enforcement of laws that require accessible programs; denied accommodations.
Programmatic
Inconvenient scheduling, too little time for examinations, little communication with patients.
Social
Conditions such as employment, education, and income that shape health.
Transportation
No accessible or convenient way to get there.

Takeaway: If environments create disability, organizations can reduce it. Name the barrier type first (attitudinal, communication, physical, policy, programmatic, social, or transportation), then choose a fix of the same type. The World Health Organization notes that people with disabilities are often left out of public health interventions, so design prevention programs for everyone from the start.
