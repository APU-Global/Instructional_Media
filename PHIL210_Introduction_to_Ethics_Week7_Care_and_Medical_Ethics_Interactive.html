Week 7 Interactive: Ethics of Care and Medical Ethics

Week 7 objective: Apply the four principles of medical ethics to healthcare scenarios and identify the core claims of care ethics.

Two frameworks

  • Care ethics (Gilligan, Noddings, Held): morality is relational; it stresses empathy, responsibility, and the needs of particular people.
  • The four principles (Beauchamp and Childress): autonomy (patients decide about their own care; informed consent), beneficence (act in the patient's best interest), non-maleficence ("first, do no harm"), and justice (fair distribution of care and resources).

Practice 1: Which principle?

Each scenario is fictional. Choose the principle it shows most clearly.

Practice 2: True or false?

Decide whether each statement about these frameworks is true or false.

Takeaway: Medical ethics gives healthcare a shared language of autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence, and justice; care ethics reminds us that each decision happens inside a relationship with a real person.

Sources: Washatka, J. (Ed.). (n.d.). Ethics of Care, An Overview and Medical Ethics, An Overview (PDFs provided in the PHIL 210 course). All scenarios are fictional.
