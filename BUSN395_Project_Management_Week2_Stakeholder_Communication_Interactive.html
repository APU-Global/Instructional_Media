Week 2 Interactive: Stakeholders and Communication

Week 2 objective: Identify a project's stakeholders and plan who needs what information, when, and how.

The case: Cedar Hills Community Center Renovation

You are the project manager for a fictional renovation of a neighborhood community center: new wiring, flooring, and a computer lab, finished in four months while the center stays partly open. Watt reminds us that stakeholders are anyone who cares about or has a vested interest in the project, and that project managers have little or no direct control over most of them.

Step 1: Internal or external stakeholder?

Watt's Figure 5.1 sorts stakeholders into internal (top management, the project team, your manager, peers, resource managers, internal customers) and external (external customers, government, contractors and subcontractors, suppliers). Sort each one.

Step 2: Synchronous or asynchronous?

Synchronous communication has everyone taking part at the same time. Asynchronous communication does not require that. Classify each message the way Chapter 15 does.

Step 3: Mind the time zones

The center's furniture designer works from Paris. Using the offsets in Watt's example, you are in Toronto (UTC−5) and the designer is in Paris (UTC+1), six hours ahead. Choose a start time in Toronto for a one-hour video call. Aim for a call that is inside 9:00–17:00 for both of you.

Step 4: Build the communication plan

Watt's seven-step template asks to whom, why, what, when, how, who, and which documents. Match each entry from the Cedar Hills plan to the question it answers.

Takeaway: A communication plan starts with every stakeholder, internal and external, then answers why, what, when, how, and who for each. Choose synchronous tools when you need everyone at once, asynchronous tools when time zones or records matter, and check the clock on both ends before you schedule the call.
