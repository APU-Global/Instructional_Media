Week 6 Interactive: Diagnosis vs. Identity

Week 6 objective: Recognize how anxiety disorders can present in children and choose responses that treat a diagnosis as information about a child rather than the child's identity.

Why this matters

Chapter 10 explains that an accurate diagnosis can open the door to accommodations and a supportive team, but a label applied without support, or a misdiagnosis, can shape how the child, teachers, and family see everything the child does. Anxiety is easy to miss: it can look like irritability or anger, show up as sleep problems, fatigue, headaches, or stomachaches, and some children keep their worries to themselves.

Practice 1: Which anxiety presentation fits?

Each child is fictional. Choose the anxiety presentation from Chapter 10 that best fits, then select Check my answers.

Practice 2: Choose the response that sees the whole child

These choices draw on an interview study of children with dyslexia and their mothers (Wilmot et al., 2023). Pick the response most consistent with what the children and parents described.

Takeaway: A diagnosis should open doors, not define a child. Look beneath behavior for hidden anxiety, name strengths alongside struggles, and build the friendships, family support, and school connection that help children feel they belong.
