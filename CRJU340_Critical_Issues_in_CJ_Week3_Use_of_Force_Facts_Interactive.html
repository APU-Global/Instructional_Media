Week 3 Interactive: Use of Force: Standards and Facts

Week 3 objective: Describe the use-of-force continuum and the reasonableness standard, and separate documented evidence from claims and opinions about a use-of-force incident.

The use-of-force continuum

Many agency policies describe an escalating series of responses, and an officer may move from one level to another in seconds: officer presence (no force), verbalization (commands, no physical force), empty-hand control (holds and joint locks, or punches and kicks), less-lethal methods (batons, chemical sprays, conducted energy devices), and lethal force, used only when a suspect poses a serious threat to the officer or another person.

The legal standard

Force must be reasonable: proportional to the threat and no more than needed for a legitimate purpose. Since Tennessee v. Garner (1985), deadly force may be used only when necessary and when the suspect poses a significant threat of death or serious physical injury to the officer or others.
