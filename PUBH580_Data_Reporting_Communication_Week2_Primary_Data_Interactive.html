Week 2 Interactive: Primary Data Sources

Week 2 objective: Tell primary from secondary data and recognize the data-quality attributes CDC asks investigators to weigh.

Key terms

  • Primary data are new data collected specifically for your evaluation or investigation (for example, interviews or a questionnaire you administer).
  • Secondary data are existing, previously collected data (for example, vital statistics, notifiable disease registries, population surveys, healthcare records, environmental data).
  • Whatever the source, CDC asks you to weigh quality, definitions, timeliness, completeness, simplicity, generalizability, validity, and reliability. Remember GIGO: garbage in, garbage out.

Takeaway: Primary data answer your question but cost time and money; secondary data are faster but were collected for someone else's purpose. Trade-offs are normal (faster collection can lower quality), and using multiple sources can strengthen credibility. Check timeliness, completeness, validity, and reliability before data carry a public message.
