Week 4 Interactive: Restorative Justice and Traditional Prosecution

Week 4 objective: Distinguish restorative justice from traditional, punishment-centered prosecution and identify the five Rs of restorative practice.

Two approaches

Traditional sentencing goals include retribution (punishment proportionate to the offense, or "just deserts") and deterrence (discouraging the offender, called specific deterrence, or others, called general deterrence). Restorative justice brings together those with a stake in an offense "to collectively identify and address harms, needs, and obligations" (Zehr), usually face-to-face with a facilitator.

The five Rs

Relationship: crime damages relationships and communities. Respect: a safe process where everyone communicates respectfully. Responsibility: the person who caused harm takes active accountability. Repair: a plan to make things as right as possible. Reintegration: rebuilding trust and, where appropriate, acceptance back into the community.

Takeaway: Traditional prosecution asks what law was broken and what punishment fits. Restorative justice also asks who was harmed, what they need, and who must make it right. Victims choose whether to take part and are not required to forgive.
