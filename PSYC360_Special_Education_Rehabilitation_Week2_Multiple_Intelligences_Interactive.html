Week 2 Interactive: Multiple Intelligences in Practice

Week 2 objective: Design learning strategies that reach a student through more than one intelligence, using practices with research support.

Eight ways to be smart, many ways in

Howard Gardner proposed that people have a mix of several intelligences: linguistic, logical-mathematical, spatial, bodily-kinesthetic, musical, interpersonal, intrapersonal, and naturalist. (Discussion 2 also names existential intelligence.) Researchers still debate the evidence, so treat the model as a reminder to offer several ways in, not as a way to label students.

Practice 1: Which intelligence does the activity draw on most?

Each activity is from a fictional lesson on the water cycle. Choose the intelligence it draws on most.

Practice 2: Supported by the readings, or a myth?

Decide whether each teaching move is supported by this week's sources.

Takeaway: Plan lessons with several ways in, let students choose, and chunk content into short pieces they can replay. Avoid locking any student into a single "style."
