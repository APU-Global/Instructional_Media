Week 5 Interactive: Sampling Techniques

Week 5 objective: Choose and justify a sampling strategy and data collection method that fit a public health research question.

Probability or nonprobability?

A population is the group you want to say something about; a sample is the group you actually collect data from. Probability samples give every element a known chance of selection and aim to resemble the population, so findings can be generalized. Nonprobability samples serve exploratory and evaluation research but do not support statistical generalization.

Takeaway: Let the question choose the sample. If you need to generalize to a population, use a probability sample and a sampling frame; if you need depth, specific experiences, or hard-to-reach voices, a well-justified nonprobability sample may fit better. Either way, plan for nonresponse and say who your findings apply to.
