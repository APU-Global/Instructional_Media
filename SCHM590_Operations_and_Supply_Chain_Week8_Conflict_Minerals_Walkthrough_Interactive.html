Week 8 Interactive: Conflict Minerals Walkthrough

Week 8 objective: Trace how a company’s responsibility for its supply chain reaches back to where its minerals originate.

The three steps (SEC final rule, 2012)

Step 1: Does the rule apply?
Are conflict minerals (cassiterite, columbite-tantalite, wolframite, and gold, or their derivatives such as tin, tantalum, and tungsten) necessary to the functionality or production of a product the company manufactures or contracts to manufacture?
Step 2: Where did they come from?
Conduct a good-faith reasonable country of origin inquiry: the DRC or an adjoining country, or recycled or scrap sources?
Step 3: Due diligence
If they came from those countries, exercise due diligence on source and chain of custody, using a recognized framework such as the OECD guidance, and file a Conflict Minerals Report with an independent audit.

Practice: Where does each company land?

Each company is fictional. Choose where the process ends for the product described, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: Supply chain integrity means knowing where materials originate, not only who your direct suppliers are. A good-faith inquiry comes first; when risk appears, due diligence, a recognized framework, and independent audit make the company’s claims credible to investors, customers, and the communities affected.

Rules summarized from Securities and Exchange Commission, “Conflict Minerals” final rule, 77 FR 56274 (September 12, 2012), as adopted. This is a simplified learning exercise, not legal guidance. Companies and scenarios are fictional.
