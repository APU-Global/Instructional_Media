Ethics, Belief, and Public Health

Public health professionals often work where communities disagree. Ethical reasoning helps them respond with integrity. Name the principles, weigh them against each other, and practice speaking with respect across disagreement.

Part 1 · Name the principle

Common principles include autonomy, beneficence, nonmaleficence, and justice. Use them to analyze difficult cases. Which principle does each statement express most directly?

Part 2 · When principles conflict

A county plans to expand adolescent health education. Two designs are on the table. Principles can conflict, requiring judgment, and thoughtful people weigh them differently. Set how much each principle matters to you and see how the comparison shifts.

Ratings (1 to 5) are illustrative simplifications, not research findings. The point is to practice noticing trade-offs, not to settle the debate.

Part 3 · Speak with respect

Leaders must speak to diverse audiences. Listen first and present evidence clearly. Maintain respect even when views differ. At a town meeting, some parents from a faith community have voiced concern about the program.

“We care about our children’s health, but we do not want the school to replace our values.”
How do you open your response?
Which approach best looks for common ground while staying honest?
Beliefs shape health decisions. Understand religious and cultural beliefs before proposing programs, seek common ground, and be honest about disagreements. Ask who benefits and who is left out, and apply the same frameworks consistently.
