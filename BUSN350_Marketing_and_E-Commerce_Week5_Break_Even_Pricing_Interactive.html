Week 5 Interactive: From Break-Even to Price

Week 5 objective: Calculate a break-even point from fixed costs, price, and variable costs, and identify common pricing strategies.

Key terms for Discussion 5

  • Fixed costs: costs paid regardless of how much is produced or sold, such as rent, equipment leases, contracted advertising, and insurance
  • Variable costs: costs that change with production and sales, such as raw materials, labor, and commissions
  • Contribution per unit: selling price minus variable cost per unit
  • Break-even point (BEP): total fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit; where total revenue equals total costs
  • Price elasticity: percentage change in quantity demanded ÷ percentage change in price

Practice 1: break-even calculator

The textbook's example: a DVD maker has $200,000 in fixed costs (copyright and distribution $150,000, package design $10,000, advertising $40,000), sells each DVD for $15, and spends $7 per unit (reproduction $5, labels and packaging $1, royalties $1). First, predict the break-even units. Then change the numbers to test your own scenario.

Takeaway: Break-even tells you the minimum a price must do: cover fixed costs through each unit's contribution. Strategy decides the rest, shaped by objectives, demand, competitors, and the law, and priced fairly toward the buyer.
