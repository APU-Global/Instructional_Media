Week 4 Interactive: ASAM Dimensions and Stages of Change

Objective: Identify the ASAM dimension behind a client concern and recognize a client’s stage of change, so a recovery plan fits the person.

Two tools for individualizing a plan

The American Society of Addiction Medicine (ASAM) assesses six dimensions: (1) risk of intoxication and withdrawal, (2) biomedical complications, (3) cognitive, emotional, and behavioral issues, (4) readiness to change, (5) relapse or continued-use risk, and (6) recovery environment. Dimensions 1–2 are biological, 3–4 psychological, and 5–6 social.

Prochaska and DiClemente’s stages of change describe readiness: precontemplation (no problem seen), contemplation (ambivalence), preparation (first steps), action (new behavior, up to about 6 months), and maintenance (beyond 6 months), with relapse common along the way (Florin & Trytek).

Takeaway: A good plan starts with a full biopsychosocial assessment and meets the person at their stage of change. Build relapse prevention in from the start, and revisit the plan as needs change.
