Week 8 Interactive: Five Dimensions of Culture

Week 8 objective: Use the Five Culture Dimensions to describe cultural differences and plan a humble, practical adaptation.

The scale

Each dimension runs from 0 to 10. Neither end is better; each has advantages and disadvantages. The farther apart two people sit on a dimension, the less easily they tend to relate.

DimensionOne end (0)Other end (10)
Equality vs. HierarchySelf-directed; free to challenge those in powerClear roles; take direction from above
Direct vs. IndirectFrank; confront issues openlyTactful; avoid conflict; read between the lines
Individual vs. GroupPersonal initiative; own decisionsGroup goals; loyalty; identity through the group
Task vs. Relationship"What do you do?"; straight to business"Who are you?"; trust before business
Risk vs. CautionQuick decisions; comfortable with changeGather information; follow precedent

Practice: which end of which dimension?

Each person is fictional, and one behavior never defines a whole culture. Choose the dimension end each behavior illustrates, then select Check my answers.

Prepare for Discussion 8: place yourself and the other person

Choose two dimensions and estimate where you and the other person fall. Nothing is saved or sent.

Takeaway: Know your own cultural style, learn the facts about the other person's, change one specific behavior, and respond with it. Cultural humility means not treating your own place on the scale as the default.

Dimensions, descriptions, and the four steps from Deborah Mackin, Cross Cultural Understanding (New Directions Consulting, 2006; adapted from Brooks Peterson, Cultural Intelligence), the Week 8 course reading. Scenarios are fictional.
