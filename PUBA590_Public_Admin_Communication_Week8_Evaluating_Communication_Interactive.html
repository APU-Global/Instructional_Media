Week 8 Interactive: Evaluating Communication

Week 8 objective: Tell output measures from outtake and outcome measures, and use a benchmark to judge whether an objective was met.

Key ideas from the reading

  • Outputs measure how well strategies were carried out (messages placed, audience size, share of positive stories). They are means, not ends.
  • Outtakes measure whether audiences received, understood, and retained the key messages.
  • Outcomes measure end results in attitudes, opinions, and behavior. They matter most and are the hardest to achieve.
  • Paine's steps: define your benchmark; select a measurement tool; analyze data, draw actionable conclusions, and make recommendations; make changes and measure again.

Practice 1: Output, outtake, or outcome?

Lakeview County ran a six-month campaign encouraging residents to request free water-saving kits. Classify each measure.

    Practice 3: Check the numbers

    Before the campaign, a survey found that 40% of residents knew about the free kits. The objective: increase awareness of the kits to 55% among county residents within six months. The follow-up survey found 58%.




    Takeaway: Counting messages shows effort, not effect. Set a benchmark, measure the same way again, and look for changes in understanding, attitudes, and behavior, monitoring during the campaign so you can correct course.
