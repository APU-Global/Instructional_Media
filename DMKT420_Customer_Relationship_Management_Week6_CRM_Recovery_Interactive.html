Week 6 Interactive: When CRM Goes Wrong

Week 6 objective: Diagnose why automated CRM messages misfire, choose responses that recover the relationship, and calculate a Net Promoter Score.

Errors are inevitable; dissatisfied customers are not

Automated CRM repeats whatever it is given. Stokes points to stale data (people move and change numbers and jobs), too many messages (endless texts drive customers off the list), and ignored opt-outs (which can bring stiff penalties). When something does go wrong, The Power of Selling describes heroic recovery: a quick response that delights the customer, plus a look at the root cause so it does not happen again. Unhappy customers talk: a happy customer tells one person, an unhappy one tells ten, and social media can multiply that.

Practice 1: why did it misfire?

Each fictional company sent a bad automated message. Choose the cause.

Practice 2: which reply recovers the relationship?

A customer posts publicly: "Your app canceled my appointment and nobody told me. I took the afternoon off for nothing." Classify each possible reply.

Practice 3: calculate a Net Promoter Score

Net Promoter Score asks: "How likely are you to recommend this company to a colleague or friend?" (0-10). Promoters answer 9-10, passives 7-8, detractors 0-6. NPS = % promoters − % detractors. Enter survey counts for a fictional company.

Takeaway: When CRM goes wrong, answer gently, fix it fast, and fix the cause. Then measure: tracking promoters and detractors shows whether your recovery is turning critics back into advocates.
