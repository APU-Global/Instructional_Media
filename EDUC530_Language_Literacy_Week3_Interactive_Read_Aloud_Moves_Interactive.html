Week 3 Interactive: Meaning-Making in the Interactive Read-Aloud

Week 3 objective: Identify the moves an interactive read-aloud uses to build comprehension, and sequence questions from literal to inferential.

Meaning making, in brief

The ELA/ELD Framework defines reading comprehension as "the process of extracting and constructing meaning through interaction and involvement with written language." Extracting meaning is understanding what the author stated, explicitly or implicitly. Constructing meaning is interpreting the text by bringing your own knowledge and experiences to it.

In the early grades, comprehension is taught mostly during read-alouds. Discussion 3 asks you to use four moves: open-ended questions, talk about an illustration, a background-knowledge connection, and academic vocabulary explained in student-friendly language.

Practice 1: Name the move

A kindergarten teacher is reading a fictional picture book, Abuela's Garden, in which a girl helps her grandmother grow vegetables and shares them with neighbors. Choose the move each teacher statement shows.

Practice 2: Literal or inferential?

The framework recommends asking literal text-dependent questions on a first reading and inferential questions on later readings. Sort each question about the same book.

Takeaway: An interactive read-aloud is a comprehension lesson. Plan your questions, the illustrations you will discuss, the connections you will invite, and the academic words you will teach before you read. Ask literal questions first, then build toward inference on rereading.
