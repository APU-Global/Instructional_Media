Week 8 Interactive: Fraud and Abuse Law Spotter

Week 8 objective: Recognize which federal fraud and abuse law a situation raises and match the parts of a compliance program to their purpose, as the new compliance manager in Discussion 8.

Three laws at a glance

FALSE CLAIMS ACT (31 U.S.C. § 3729)
Knowingly submitting, or causing, a false claim to the government. Liability: three times the damages plus a penalty. "Knowingly" includes deliberate ignorance and reckless disregard; no proof of specific intent to defraud is required.
ANTI-KICKBACK STATUTE (42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7b(b))
Knowingly and willfully offering, paying, soliciting, or receiving remuneration in return for referrals or purchases paid by a federal health care program. A felony: up to $100,000, up to 10 years, or both.
STARK LAW (42 U.S.C. § 1395nn)
A physician with a financial relationship with an entity may not refer Medicare patients to it for designated health services (e.g., clinical laboratory, physical therapy, radiology), and the entity may not bill for them, unless an exception applies.
COMPLIANCE PROGRAM (42 C.F.R. § 423.504)
Core requirements for Medicare Part D sponsors, used here as a model for any organization.
