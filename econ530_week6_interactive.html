Deciding When the Future Is Uncertain

Managers rarely know outcomes in advance. Weight each outcome by its probability, ask what information is worth, compare how risk-averse and risk-neutral rules choose, and see how hidden information shapes contracts.

Part 1 · Launch, test, or hold?

A firm can launch a product in a new foreign market now, pay $0.5M for a pilot test that reveals how the market will turn out and then decide, or not launch. Demand turns out strong (+$8M), moderate (+$2M, 30% likely), or weak (−$6M). Set how likely strong demand is.

Expected value weights each outcome by its probability. It does not capture a decision-maker’s attitude toward risk: a risk-averse manager may reasonably choose differently. The pilot is assumed to reveal the true outcome. All figures are in $ millions and invented for practice.

Part 2 · Who knows what?

Unequal information creates risk. Adverse selection occurs before a deal, when hidden characteristics lead to a poor mix of participants; moral hazard occurs after a deal, when hidden behavior changes because someone else bears the cost.

Part 3 · Design the response

Transparency builds trust. Signals, screening, warranties, and monitoring reduce information gaps. Being open about what you know, and about the limits of what you know, lowers cost and builds trust with partners and customers.
