Week 3 Interactive: Why Good Teens Make Risky Choices

Week 3 focus: Explain adolescent brain development and how it affects decision making, as Discussion 3 asks you to do for Gia’s mother.

Part 1: Which brain system?

Steinberg describes two systems on different timetables. Sort each feature into the socio-emotional (reward) system or the cognitive control system.

Part 2: The driving-game study

Adolescents (about 14), youths (about 20), and adults (about 34) played a video driving game alone or with two friends present. Predict the results.

Part 3: The counselor’s office

Marcus (fictional), 15, an A student and youth group volunteer, joined two friends in a dangerous skateboard stunt off a parking garage ramp. Alone, he says, he would never have done it.

Takeaway: Around puberty the socio-emotional system becomes more reward-seeking, while the cognitive control system matures slowly into the twenties. Calm and alone, teens can regulate themselves; with peers or strong emotion, reward-seeking can win. Structure, monitoring, and warm but firm parenting help while judgment matures.
