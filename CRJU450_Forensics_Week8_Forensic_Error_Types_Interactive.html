Week 8 objective: Classify the ways forensic evidence can contribute to wrongful convictions and identify DNA handling practices that protect against error.

Five types of forensic error

NIJ-funded research by Dr. John Morgan reviewed 732 wrongful conviction cases and sorted forensic errors into five types. Select each card.

Practice 2: Protecting DNA evidence

Decide whether each fictional practice protects DNA evidence or puts it at risk.

Takeaway: DNA is powerful, but forensic evidence can fail at every stage: the report, the interpretation, the testimony, the courtroom, and the handling of evidence. Careful collection, preserved samples, and honest testimony protect the innocent, and each wrongful conviction should be studied as a sentinel event.
