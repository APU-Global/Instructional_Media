Week 3 Interactive: Team Contracts

Week 3 objective: Explain how clear norms, roles, and expectations in a team charter support team-based problem solving.

The five parts of a team contract

Norms are shared expectations about how things operate within a team, and they form early. Teams that agree on ground rules, goals, and roles in a team contract are better equipped to face challenges. A contract answers questions in five areas: (1) values and goals; (2) roles and leadership (who sets the agenda, takes notes, assigns tasks, runs meetings); (3) decision making (how minor and major decisions are made); (4) communication (who contacts whom, what to do if you miss a meeting, how often the team meets); and (5) performance (what good work looks like and how poor attendance or quality is handled).

Practice

Team Northstar is a fictional ORGS 590 team drafting its charter. Choose the part of the team contract each line belongs to, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: A charter does not guarantee success, but it is a road map when a team veers off course. Write down your goals and values, roles, decision rules, communication habits, and performance standards early, while norms are still forming.
