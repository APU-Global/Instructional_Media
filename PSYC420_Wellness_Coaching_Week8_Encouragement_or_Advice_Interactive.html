Week 8 Interactive: The Act of Giving Advice

Week 8 objective: Distinguish client-centered coaching responses from advice-giving and recognize when information supports a client's own problem-solving.

Four kinds of coaching response

A review of 284 articles found that most health and wellness coaching is patient-centered, uses goals the client chooses, and relies on self-discovery rather than passive advice, while still offering some education.

Self-discovery questionInvites the client to find or generate their own solutions.
EncouragementBuilds the client's belief that they can succeed, for example by recalling past successes.
Advice-givingTells the client what to do, replacing their own thinking and choice.
Supportive educationOffers information in service of the client's own problem-solving and choice.

Practice: label each response

Tasha (fictional) wants to lower her stress. Choose the best label for each thing her coach says, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: Encouragement and self-discovery keep the client in charge of their goals. Information is ethical and helpful when it is wanted and serves the client's own choice; advice that replaces their choice is where coaching drifts from its client-centered role.
