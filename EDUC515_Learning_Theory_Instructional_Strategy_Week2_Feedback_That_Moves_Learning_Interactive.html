Week 2 Interactive: Feedback That Moves Learning Forward

Week 2 objective: Identify feedback that helps students answer Where am I going? Where am I now? Where to next?

What makes feedback useful

Useful feedback tells students what they have done well and what they can do next to improve. It focuses on the task, the processes students use, and students' self-regulation, not on the students themselves, and it avoids comparisons with other students. A score alone tells students what they achieved, but not how or why. Feedback should prompt a thinking reaction (next steps), not an emotional one.

Practice

Each comment comes from a fictional classroom. Choose the kind of feedback it is, then select Check my answers.

Takeaway: Plan feedback around three questions: Where am I going? (the goal and success criteria), Where am I now? (evidence of progress), and Where to next? (a concrete step). Then give students time to use it; feedback that is never acted on does not serve its purpose.

Feedback principles from English Language Arts/English Language Development Framework (California Department of Education, adopted 2014), Chapter 8 "Assessment" (sections on Feedback, Teacher Feedback, Peer Feedback, and Self-Assessment). Comments and classrooms are fictional.
