Week 2 Interactive: Chain of Custody

Week 2 objective: Identify what a chain of custody record must contain and spot the gaps that weaken it.

What the record shows

McKee calls chain of custody a legal safeguard that maintains the identity and integrity of evidence. It is a documented timeline noting every person who handled an item, when it was handled, where it was collected, and a detailed description. Evidence stays in someone's physical possession or locked away, handling is kept to a minimum, and every transfer is documented: who received it, when, and under what circumstances. A break in the chain can make evidence inadmissible.

McKee also lists what goes on the evidence container: your initials and ID number, the date and time of collection, the case number, the evidence number, and a detailed location where the item was found. The course's chain of custody tracking form adds a line for every transfer: date/time, item number, who released it, who received it, and the reason.

Practice 2: audit a custody log

Fictional case 26-0412, Elm Street Pharmacy burglary. Item 3 is a pry bar found at the rear door. Decide whether each entry is complete or what it is missing.

Takeaway: Chain of custody is a chronological history. Every item needs a collector, a description, and case information, and every transfer needs a name, a date and time, and a reason. Record each step when it happens; a missing link gives the defense a reason to question whether the evidence in court is the evidence from the scene.
