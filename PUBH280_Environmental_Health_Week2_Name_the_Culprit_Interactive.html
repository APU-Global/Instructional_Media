Week 2 Interactive: Historical Environmental Health Disasters

Week 2 objective: Identify the pollutants behind major historical environmental health disasters and what each one taught public health.

Every disaster had a culprit

Xu et al. (2022) group pollution-related emergencies into three kinds: notorious public health events (air pollution episodes, heavy metal pollution, chemical poisoning), endemic diseases caused by elements in the local environment, and occupational diseases from exposure at work. In each case, investigators traced an illness back to a specific pollutant.

Practice: what was the culprit?

Read each set of clues, choose the pollutant responsible, then select Check my answers. The feedback names the event.

Takeaway: Each disaster tied a specific pollutant to a specific illness. Air disasters struck when weather trapped emissions; metal and chemical disasters reached people through water, food, and work. Learning these lessons is how public health prevents the next crisis.

Facts from Xu, H., et al. (2022), "Environmental pollution, a hidden culprit for health issues," Eco-Environment & Health, 1(1), 31–45 (PMC10702928). Clues are paraphrased from the article.
