Week 6 Interactive: Which Theft Is It?

Week 6 objective: Distinguish nonviolent ("white-collar") theft crimes from forcible theft, and see how theft is graded.

Three ways to steal without force

TAKING
Larceny

A physical taking of personal property: control plus any movement. Larceny by trick gains possession through a lie.

CONVERSION
Embezzlement

Converting property the victim entrusted to the defendant, often in a relationship of trust and confidence.

DECEPTION
False pretenses

Obtaining ownership of property or services by a false representation of fact the victim relies on.

Modern consolidated theft statutes combine all three. Robbery adds force or a threat of imminent force; extortion uses a threat of future harm.

Theft grading under the Model Penal Code

Under consolidated theft statutes, grading depends mainly on value. The Model Penal Code makes theft a felony of the third degree if the amount exceeds $500 or the property is a firearm or motor vehicle; other theft is a misdemeanor or petty misdemeanor.

Takeaway: Ask how the property was obtained: taken (larceny), entrusted then converted (embezzlement), or obtained by a lie (false pretenses or larceny by trick). Force turns theft into robbery, and a threat of future harm makes it extortion. White-collar crime is nonviolent, but it can cause enormous loss, and fraud using the mail is a federal felony.
