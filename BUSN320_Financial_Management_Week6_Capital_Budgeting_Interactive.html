Week 6 Interactive: Capital Budgeting

Week 6 objective: Identify the incremental cash flows of a capital project and use NPV and payback to recommend whether to go ahead.

The project: Trailhead Footwear's new hiking shoe (fictional)

Trailhead Footwear is considering a new line, the Ridge Runner. Managers expect it to sell 1.5 million pairs a year for four years. Market research shows that 1.0 million of those pairs would be bought by customers who would otherwise buy Trailhead's existing trail shoe.

Step 1: decide whether each item belongs in the project's cash flow analysis.

Step 1: Incremental or not?

Only cash flows that exist because of the project count. Choose Include or Exclude for each item.

Step 2: Run the numbers

Use these figures (all fictional):

  • Each incremental pair brings in $3.00 of cash after production costs.
  • The athlete endorsement costs $300,000 a year.
  • New molding equipment costs $3,000,000 today and can be sold for $200,000 at the end of Year 4.
  • Trailhead's required rate of return (its next-best alternative) is 10%. Cash flows arrive at the end of each year.

Takeaway: Count only the cash a project adds. Leave out sunk costs, subtract cannibalized sales, and include new costs such as an endorsement and end-of-life value such as equipment resale. Counting all 1.5 million pairs would have overstated the yearly cash flow at $4,200,000 instead of $1,200,000. Then judge the project by NPV at the required rate, not by payback alone.
