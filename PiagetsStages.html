Piaget’s Stages of Cognitive Development

A Quick Visual Guide to How We Grow and Learn.

Jean Piaget, a Swiss psychologist, proposed that children move through four predictable stages of cognitive development as they grow from infancy to adulthood, shaping how they perceive and reason about the world.

  1. Sensorimotor Stage

    Birth to ~2 years

    Babies learn about the world by using their senses—touching, looking, listening, and tasting - and physical movement, like grabbing. A key milestone here is learning "object permanence," which is knowing that a toy still exists even when hidden under a blanket. During this time, infants also realize that they are separate from the world around them. They start doing things on purpose, like shaking a rattle just to hear the noise it makes.

  2. Preoperational Stage

    ~2 to 7 years

    Kids start using words and symbols or pretend play to represent ideas. However, they don't use strict logic yet and often struggle to see things from someone else's point of view (egocentrism), like thinking that if they close their eyes, you can't see them either. Children at this age tend to focus on just one obvious detail at a time (centration), like picking the tallest glass because it looks like it holds more. Because of this, looks can easily trick their thinking.

  3. Concrete Operational Stage

    ~7 to 11 years

    Children begin thinking logically about real, physical things. For example, they understand "conservation", that squishing a ball of clay flat doesn't change the amount of clay you have. They can also sort items by size or type and undo steps in their heads. However, they still need to see or touch real objects to solve problems and struggle with purely imaginary ideas.

  4. Formal Operational Stage

    ~12 years and older

    Adolescents and adults can think about abstract ideas, test out hypotheses, and consider "what if" situations without needing to see or touch real objects. This allows them to think deeply about morality, politics, and future goals. They can now test different solutions step-by-step to figure out why something works.
