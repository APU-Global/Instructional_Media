Week 6 Interactive: What Helps and What Hurts

Week 6 objective: Tell supportive responses from hurtful ones and name the communication habits that shut grief down.

What the readings say

Lewis notes that no perfect combination of words takes away a grieving person's pain, but some actions show care: calling, texting, or showing up in person; sending cards or gifts; anticipating needs; checking in; and listening with compassion. Responses to avoid include trying to fix the grief, not saying the name of the person who died, making it about ourselves, and urging the person to move on. The Open RN text adds communication habits that block feelings, such as false reassurance, sympathy that centers the helper, and giving personal opinions.

Practice 1: Helps or hurts?

Each situation is fictional. Decide whether the response is likely to help or to hurt.

Practice 2: Name the habit

Each statement is a nontherapeutic response from the Open RN list. Choose the habit it shows.

Takeaway: Comfort rarely comes from the perfect sentence. Show up, make specific offers, say the person's name, and listen more than you advise.
